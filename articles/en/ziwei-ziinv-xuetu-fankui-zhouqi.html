Zi Wei Dou Shu Children Palace and Trainee Rework: Check the Feedback Cycle

Separate the mentoring relationship from delivery standards when a trainee repeats revisions.

Zi Wei Dou Shu2026-11-18 09:08

A trainee has revised a client design three times, yet the required size is still wrong. A Zi Wei Dou Shu reading may raise a question about mentoring through the Children Palace, but the first evidence is the work record: when did the requirement arrive, who reviewed the draft, and what counted as acceptance?

Relationship and responsibility are different questions

The Children Palace can frame the interaction with someone you train. The Career Palace points to the job standard and final responsibility. If Tian Ji is prominent in the Children Palace, an iterative teaching style may be worth discussing. If Wen Chang appears in the Career Palace, written examples may matter. Neither placement proves that the trainee is careless. Read the actual brief before judging a person.

Find the first avoidable revision

Mark four moments: assignment, first draft, review, and delivery. A missed requirement already present in the assignment suggests a checklist problem. A requirement added after the second draft is a scope change. A vague instruction such as “make it better” is not a usable acceptance rule. Give a photographer a sample review after ten images, or approve one product image template before making fifty variants. Then observe whether the next cycle improves.

Confirm birth data and the palace being discussed before interpreting the chart. Read Children for the mentoring relationship and Career for the deliverable, then compare any transformations with the dated briefs and review notes. One round of rework does not settle a long term working relationship.

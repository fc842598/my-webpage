How Zi Wei Dou Shu Reads Exam and Study Luck: Life, Career, Parents Palaces with Chang-Qu and Hua Ke

For study and exams, Life sets learning quality, Career (study as the body of career) sets effort direction, Parents as documents/repute palace shows diploma and examiner rapport, then Chang/Qu, Hua Ke and Kui/Yue scholarly stars, and finally decade/year for which exam has the best chance—a full chain of quality, effort, repute and timing.

Zi Wei Dou Shu2026-09-06

Exams are not raw intelligence: Life is base, Career is sustained effort, Parents is documents and on-site repute, scholarly stars performance, decade/year timing—one missing link can cause an on-site stumble.

What This Question Is Really Asking

Bright Life without Career Ke or with malefic Parents means strong in practice, poor in exams; an average base in a scholarly decade with right method can over-perform—the key is knowing your strong and weak links.

The Underlying Logic

Life: learning ability and overall quality; a strong star with Chang/Qu/Ke means a clear mind, good memory and comprehension.

Career: study is a student's career; Career Lu makes study engaging, Quan means willing hard work, Ke methodical order, Ji means blocks, aversion or wrong method.

Parents: anciently the documents/repute palace for diploma, exams, teachers and school; auspicious means smooth performance and teacher favor, malefics/Ji mean carelessness, nerves or mismatch with standard answers.

Scholarly stars and timing: Chang/Qu govern exams and documents, Ke reputation, Kui/Yue mentors; applying, certifying or evaluating titles when a decade/year activates them is most favorable.

A Step-by-Step Way to Read It

  1. Read Life and Chang/Qu for learning base, comprehension and memory
  2. Read Career transformations for effort state and method
  3. Read Parents for diploma luck, exam performance and teacher rapport
  4. Find decades/years activating Ke and Kui/Yue to set exam windows
  5. Strengthen weak links: weak Parents means practicing standard answers and controlling nerves
  6. For exams in malefic/Ji periods, prepare more thoroughly—never sit unprepared

Three Common Mistakes

Mistake 1: Looking only at intelligence, ignoring effort and performance

Mistake 2: Treating Parents only as family relations, missing documents/repute

Mistake 3: Ignoring decade/year and choosing the wrong year for a key exam

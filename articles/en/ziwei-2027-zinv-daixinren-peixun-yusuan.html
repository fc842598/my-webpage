Zi Wei Dou Shu 2027 Children Palace: Budgeting a New Hire's Training

Separate mentoring time, training costs, and job responsibility when a new hire joins your team.

Zi Wei Dou Shu2026-11-20 09:00

A manager asks you to train a new colleague in 2027 but assigns no time or budget. In a Zi Wei Dou Shu chart, the annual Children Palace can frame work that develops over time, including mentoring. It does not assign unlimited unpaid responsibility. Read the Career Palace for formal duties and the Wealth Palace for resources.

Locate the annual question

Map the 2027 Children Palace onto the natal chart. If the question connects strongly with Career, ask who owns the new hire's deliverables and who signs them off. If Wealth is involved, list training fees and the work hours displaced by mentoring. Read stars, transformations, and connected palaces before drawing conclusions. A favorable marker is not a purchase order or a staffing plan.

Count three kinds of cost

Record weekly mentoring hours, software or course fees, and the review time needed before client work goes out. One team may have a small cash budget but remove a project from the mentor's workload. Another may pay for a course while leaving the full workload in place. The second arrangement can cost more time despite its visible budget.

Put ownership in writing

A trainee can practice a client response while an authorized colleague approves the final version. If you are accountable for the outcome, ask for the authority to set training steps and request review. Verify birth details and the annual chart convention, read Children, Career, and Wealth together, then compare the chart questions with the written hours, expense approval, and sign-off rule. Review the arrangement after two weeks of actual work.

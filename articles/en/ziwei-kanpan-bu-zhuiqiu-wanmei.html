Don't Seek a Perfect Chart: Every Chart Has Flaws

There is no "perfect" Zi Wei chart — charts with many auspicious stars may lack malefic drive; charts with many malefics may have more resilience. Flaws aren't the problem; playing your hand well is.

Methods2026-08-20 15:30

Many people start finding "bad" things after casting: Ji in Spouse — "my marriage is doomed"; Qing Yang in Life — "my fate is bad"; too many malefics — "my life is over." But consider: a chart full of auspicious stars without malefics may produce a "nice person" who is mediocre — no drive, no breakthrough, no story. Flaws aren't defects; they're features.

Why no perfect chart exists: first, twelve palaces inherently have "six strong six weak" — not every palace can be good; second, auspicious and malefic stars are two sides — auspicious without malefic is "hothouse flower," malefic without auspicious is "runaway horse"; third, transformations have Lu and Ji — heaven opens one door and closes another; fourth, cycles rotate — not every cycle is good. Accepting imperfection lets you use the chart's strengths.

The Trap of a "Good Chart"

Too many auspicious stars — smooth life but possibly can't handle setbacks, "hothouse flower."

No malefics — lacks drive and breakthrough, easily settles.

Too much Lu — many opportunities but choice paralysis, "want everything, catch nothing."

No malefics at all — gentle but no edge, easily outcompeted.

Good chart in bad cycles — "wealth doesn't last three generations"; good cards played badly are common.

The Advantage of a "Bad Chart"

Many malefics — resilient, can carry pressure, indomitable.

Ji in key palace — that domain is stuck, but deep cultivation may produce expertise.

Inauspicious pattern controlled — controlled malefics are "whetstones," sharpening the blade.

Bad cycles early — "poor children mature early"; hardship in youth is wealth.

Bad chart in good cycles — "fortune turns"; comeback stories are the best.

How to Face Flaws

Accept — flaws are normal; no one has a perfect fate.

Identify — know your weak spots to prepare in advance.

Compensate — through choices, environment, effort.

Leverage — focus on strengths rather than obsessing over weaknesses.

Wait — cycles have highs and lows; lie low in bad times, strike in good.

The chart is the hand; you are the player — good or bad, you play; what matters is how.

Reading Order

When reading, keep this mindset:

  1. Find strengths first — where is your chart best; that's your main battlefield.
  2. Then find flaws — where is weakest; that's what to watch.
  3. Don't lament flaws — they're features, not verdicts.
  4. Check cycles — when strengths express, when to defend.
  5. Make a strategy — how to charge in good times, how to defend in bad.
  6. There are no perfect charts, only people who play their chart well.

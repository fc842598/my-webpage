Strong Major Star but Many Malefics in Zi Wei Dou Shu: Main Line First or Risk First

The major star sets your main line and ability; malefics set friction and cost along the way. A stable major star turns many malefics into struggle rather than collapse—confirm the main line, then assess how the malefics operate.

Zi Wei Dou Shu2026-09-04

Many malefics do not equal a bad fate; what matters is whether the major star can bear them—if so they are a whetstone, if not, repeated setbacks.

What This Question Is Really Asking

People tense up at Yang, Tuo and Huo in the trine before judging whether the major star is pioneering or conservative and whether auspicious stars assist, inflating the risk.

The Underlying Logic

The major star is the person; malefics are slopes and pits on the road—first confirm the person can walk, then discuss the road.

Pioneering stars (Sha-Po-Lang, Zi Wei) are sparked by malefics; conservative stars are more easily drained.

Check whether auspicious stars or Hua Ke/Quan balance the malefics—controlled, they become useful; unchecked, they harm.

A Step-by-Step Way to Read It

  1. Fix the major star's type and strength to confirm core ability
  2. Count whether malefics share the palace or meet by trine to gauge closeness
  3. Look for auspicious stars, Hua Ke or Hua Quan that balance them
  4. Strong major star with checks: read malefics as execution and resilience training
  5. Weak major star without checks: focus advice on risk management and pacing
  6. Finally use decade and year to see when the malefics are triggered

Three Common Mistakes

Mistake 1: Judging a bad fate from many malefics without the major star's strength

Mistake 2: Treating all malefics alike, ignoring that Yang, Tuo, Huo and Ling differ

Mistake 3: Counting quantity without checks, inflating or understating risk

Back to Your Own Chart

The major star is the foundation and malefics the process. On a solid foundation, friction shapes growth—give confidence first, then ways to work with the friction.

Reading Order

  1. Cast the natal chart first and locate the palaces and major stars tied to major stars versus malefics before judging anything
  2. Open the triple-direction of those palaces and count the auspicious, malefic and assistant stars separately
  3. Check where the natal Four Transformations land to set the chart's baseline tone
  4. Read the decade-limit transformations to see whether the major stars versus malefics line is activated in this stage
  5. Then read the annual transformations and the year's palace to pin down this year's trigger and timing
  6. Layer natal, decade and year before concluding, then decide how to act rather than labeling good or bad

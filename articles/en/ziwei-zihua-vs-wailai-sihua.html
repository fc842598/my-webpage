Self-Transformation versus External Transformations in Zi Wei Dou Shu: Whose Energy Is Moving

Natal, decade and year transformations are external energy flying into a palace, while self-transformations arise from the palace's own stem as an internal reaction. Self Ji often means energy that does not stay, and self Quan or Ke may be proactive creation or outward-spreading reputation; direction differs, so reading differs.

Zi Wei Dou Shu2026-09-27

Many see a transformed star and credit it to the year without telling whether it is external or flown out by the palace itself. That one distinction flips who is active and who is passive.

What This Question Is Really Asking

External transformations are wind blowing in from outside, support or pressure from timing and others; a self-transformation is the palace itself flapping its wings, that matter actively running outward or leaking on its own. One receives, the other acts.

The Underlying Logic

Natal, decade and year transformations arise from a stem and land a star in a palace, marking external energy and timing.

Self-transformation uses the home palace's stem on its own star, an internal force pointing to self-spending or outward expansion.

Self Ji often means energy that will not stay, half-finished matters and mood circling back; hold steady rather than charge.

Self Quan or Ke may be proactive creation or outward-spreading reputation, beneficial but prone to leaking outward instead of holding.

A Step-by-Step Way to Read It

  1. First separate which transformed stars are natal, decade or year external ones
  2. Then find which palaces carry self-transformations from their own stem
  3. Read external ones as timing and others bringing support or pressure
  4. Read self Ji for energy that will not stay; add holding power and steady ground
  5. Read self Quan or Ke as either proactive creation or outward reputation, and treat them differently
  6. Read both layers: what comes in from outside and what leaks out by itself, for a complete picture

Three Common Mistakes

Mistake 1: Reading self-transformation as an external yearly fortune and flipping active versus passive

Mistake 2: Seeing self Ji only as disaster without recognizing it as energy that will not stay

Mistake 3: Ignoring the outward nature of self Quan or Ke and assuming the benefit holds by itself

Back to Your Own Chart

External transformations are the wind and self-transformations the sail. The wind says where you are pushed; the sail says where you pull and where you leak. Tell wind from sail and you know whether timing pushes the matter or you yourself drive it.

Reading Order

  1. Cast the natal chart first and locate the palaces and major stars tied to self-transformation versus external transformations before judging anything
  2. Open the triple-direction of those palaces and count the auspicious, malefic and assistant stars separately
  3. Check where the natal Four Transformations land to set the chart's baseline tone
  4. Read the decade-limit transformations to see whether the self-transformation versus external transformations line is activated in this stage
  5. Then read the annual transformations and the year's palace to pin down this year's trigger and timing
  6. Layer natal, decade and year before concluding, then decide how to act rather than labeling good or bad

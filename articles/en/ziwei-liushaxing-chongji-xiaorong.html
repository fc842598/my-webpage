How to Read the Six Malefics by Palace: First Separate Impact from Dissolving

The six malefics divide into direct impact, sustained friction and resource dissolving: Qing-Yang and Huo-Xing for sudden change, Tuo-Luo and Ling-Xing for delay, and Kong and Jie for draining. Combinations and auspicious support decide whether friction becomes execution.

Zi Wei Dou Shu2026-09-25

At the mention of malefics, many think only of misfortune, but the six act in entirely different ways: some strike like a blow, others grind like a dull blade, and others drain like an hourglass. Separate the types before responding.

What This Question Is Really Asking

This replaces fear-based reading with risk-management reading. Malefics do not judge you as bad but mark the shape of risk: sudden change, delay or draining. Set processes to match and friction can become execution.

The Underlying Logic

Qing-Yang and Huo-Xing lean toward direct impact and sudden change; Tuo-Luo and Ling-Xing toward sustained friction and delay.

Di-Kong and Di-Jie lean toward resource draining and weak retention, giving three distinct modes.

The same malefic in the Life, Wealth, Career or Health palace carries different real problems.

Combinations and auspicious support decide whether friction becomes execution; remedies should be written as risk management and action boundaries.

A Step-by-Step Way to Read It

  1. First list the six malefics and group them into impact, friction and draining
  2. Confirm which palace each lands in and what theme it affects
  3. For the impact type, focus on safety plans and buffers
  4. For friction and delay, focus on process checkpoints and deadlines
  5. For draining, focus on accumulation and forced retention
  6. See auspicious support to convert the matching friction into execution rather than seeking a universal remedy

Three Common Mistakes

Mistake 1: Treating the six malefics vaguely as bad without distinguishing their modes

Mistake 2: Thinking only of remedies without concrete risk-management processes

Mistake 3: Ignoring palace differences and generalizing the same malefic across positions

Back to Your Own Chart

Malefics are not bad labels but different shapes of risk. Learn to distinguish impact, friction and draining, then set matching processes, and you often find charts with malefics carry stronger execution.

Reading Order

  1. Cast the natal chart first and locate the palaces and major stars tied to the impact and dissolving types of the six malefics before judging anything
  2. Open the triple-direction of those palaces and count the auspicious, malefic and assistant stars separately
  3. Check where the natal Four Transformations land to set the chart's baseline tone
  4. Read the decade-limit transformations to see whether the the impact and dissolving types of the six malefics line is activated in this stage
  5. Then read the annual transformations and the year's palace to pin down this year's trigger and timing
  6. Layer natal, decade and year before concluding, then decide how to act rather than labeling good or bad

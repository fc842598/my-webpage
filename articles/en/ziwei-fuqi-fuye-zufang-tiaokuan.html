Zi Wei Dou Shu Spouse and Property Palaces: Leasing a Studio for a Side Business

Separate relationship negotiation, workspace needs, and cash exposure before signing a studio lease.

Zi Wei Dou Shu2026-11-18 09:10

One partner wants to teach weekend pottery classes and has found a studio with a one year lease. The other supports the idea but is unsure who signs, whose savings cover the deposit, and what happens if classes do not fill. In Zi Wei Dou Shu, the Spouse Palace frames the negotiation, the Property Palace the space, and the Wealth Palace the cash commitment.

Put names beside obligations

Check the tenant, guarantor, deposit, exit fee, and subletting rules. A cooperative relationship does not answer who is legally responsible for rent. Tian Tong in the Spouse Palace may prompt a conversation about keeping peace, while Wu Qu may prompt one about how money decisions are made. Neither star writes the lease. Agree who approves renewal and who keeps the records.

Test the space and the budget separately

The Property Palace question is whether the room supports ventilation, storage, visitors, and noise limits. The Wealth Palace question is whether rent and equipment remain affordable during an empty class month. A favorable room can still be too expensive; a cheap room can still be unusable for teaching. Estimate fixed costs with a conservative class count before committing household funds.

A three month trial in a shared space can reveal demand before a long lease. If a year is required, set an investment cap, a review date, and a clear exit decision. Confirm birth inputs before comparing Spouse, Property, Wealth, and Career Palace roles. Use the signed contract and actual accounts for legal and financial decisions.

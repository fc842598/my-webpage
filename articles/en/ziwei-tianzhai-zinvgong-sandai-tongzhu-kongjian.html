Zi Wei Dou Shu Home and Children Palaces: Planning Space for Three Generations

Use the Home and Children Palaces to frame shared-space questions, then test schedules, care needs, and costs.

Zi Wei Dou Shu2026-11-04 11:03

Three generations sharing one home often face a practical question: whose routine gets interrupted, and when? In Zi Wei Dou Shu, the Home Palace points to the living setting. The Children Palace can help frame the children's routine and care arrangements. Neither palace tells a family to move by itself.

Separate the chart question from the floor plan

Start with the Home Palace and its supporting palaces, then examine the Children Palace. Add the Wealth Palace if a renovation or another rental would cost money. A Tian Fu pattern in the Home Palace may suggest resources to organize, yet it does not create an extra room. A Tian Ji pattern involving children may invite a closer look at changing schedules, not a label for a child's personality.

Two households need different fixes

In one family, online lessons disturb a grandparent's sleep. Testing sound control and moving the lesson time may help. Another family has a spare room, but nighttime care makes uninterrupted work impossible. That family needs a care schedule. The same pair of palaces leads to different questions because the observed conflict differs.

Map a week of room use, note the recurring collisions, and compare the cost of schedule changes, a divider, or outside workspace. Read the natal chart first, then the relevant time layer. Let the chart organize the discussion while the family makes the decision.

Hua Ji in the Home Palace vs. Clashing the Opposite: Sitting-Ji and Clashing-Ji Differ in Force—Do Not Fear Both Equally

Ji sitting in a palace blocks that palace's matter for direct work; its opposite palace simultaneously receives the "Ji clash," tending to external/result-level turbulence. Sitting is an inner knot, clashing is impact across—the force and response differ.

Zi Wei Dou Shu2026-09-08

Many fear Hua Ji without telling whether it sits in the asked palace or clashes from across; one position over, it is either the lesson of the matter itself or being pulled by the opposite matter.

What This Question Is Really Asking

People see Hua Ji in the Spouse palace's opposite and declare the marriage bad; that is actually Career (its opposite) clashing—work pressure spilling into the relationship, quite different from Ji sitting in Spouse itself.

The Underlying Logic

Sitting-Ji (Ji-in): the transformed star is in the asked palace, energy concentrated inside the matter—fixation, debt, block; the matter itself needs repeated work, deep but focused.

Clashing-Ji: Ji in a palace clashes its opposite; the home palace is the cause and the opposite the affected result; clash force is external and sudden, showing in environment, others or outcomes.

Take Wealth: Ji sitting in Wealth knots the earning method and money attitude—fix the way you seek money; Ji in Wealth clashes Karma (its opposite), so the pursuit of money directly hits mental state and wellbeing.

In reading, first fix sit vs. clash, then severity: sitting-Ji needs deep work in that palace; clashing-Ji requires managing both cause and affected palaces, cutting the cause-to-impact transmission rather than mistaking the clashed palace for the source.

A Step-by-Step Way to Read It

  1. Locate where the Hua Ji star actually sits—the "sitting" position
  2. Follow the opposite line to the clashed palace and mark the cause→affected pair
  3. Decide whether your question belongs to the cause palace or the affected one, not mistaking impact for source
  4. Sitting-Ji: find fixation/debt in that palace and do deep corrective work
  5. Clashing-Ji: manage both ends, focusing on cutting transmission and stabilizing the affected side
  6. With decade/year, a decade-Ji clashing a natal palace or annual-Ji clashing a decade palace marks the stage's key external risk point

Three Common Mistakes

Mistake 1: Ignoring sit/clash and condemning a palace just because its opposite holds Ji

Mistake 2: Mistaking the external clash for the palace's own structural problem

Mistake 3: Assuming clash is always worse than sitting when depth depends on star brightness and buffers, case by case

Back to Your Own Chart

Distinguishing sitting-Ji from clashing-Ji turns Hua Ji from a scary star into precise navigation: deepen where it sits, cut transmission to whom it clashes. Mark each Ji's sit and clash on your chart and your response to blocks goes from panic to targeted action.

Reading Order

  1. Cast the natal chart first and locate the palaces and major stars tied to Hua Ji in a palace versus Ji clashing the opposite palace before judging anything
  2. Open the triple-direction of those palaces and count the auspicious, malefic and assistant stars separately
  3. Check where the natal Four Transformations land to set the chart's baseline tone
  4. Read the decade-limit transformations to see whether the Hua Ji in a palace versus Ji clashing the opposite palace line is activated in this stage
  5. Then read the annual transformations and the year's palace to pin down this year's trigger and timing
  6. Layer natal, decade and year before concluding, then decide how to act rather than labeling good or bad

How to Handle Empty Palaces in Zi Wei Dou Shu: Reading the Opposite Palace When a Palace Has No Main Star

When none of the twelve main stars falls in a palace, it is called empty; that does not mean the palace is blank. You borrow the main stars from the opposite palace, read them through that opposite palace's nature, and then layer the palace's own assistants, malefics and transformations for its real state.

Zi Wei Dou Shu2026-09-28

New readers see an empty palace and think that area has nothing to read. An empty palace simply has no main star seated; the opposite palace's main stars shine across, and the palace is still read.

What This Question Is Really Asking

The question is: an empty palace is not really empty; its main star sits across and is borrowed. Clarify which opposite palace the borrowed stars come from, read them through that palace's nature, then layer the local minor stars and transformations; the three together are the palace.

The Underlying Logic

An empty palace has none of the twelve main stars, but assistants, malefics and transformations still sit there and must not be skipped.

When borrowing the opposite main stars, read them through that opposite palace's nature, not as if born in this palace.

Stars shining from across carry a layer of distance and are less intimate than main stars seated in the palace.

The palace's own assistants, malefics and transformations decide whether the borrowed stars help or burden here, and must be layered on.

A Step-by-Step Way to Read It

  1. Confirm the palace truly has no main star, ruling out a weakly seated one
  2. Find the opposite palace and note its main-star combination
  3. Read that combination across into this palace through the opposite palace's nature
  4. Then read this palace's own assistants, malefics and minor stars
  5. Layer the transformations from both this and the opposite palace to see activation
  6. Judge the palace as borrowed force rather than a self-seated main star

Three Common Mistakes

Mistake 1: Skipping an empty palace as if that area needs no reading

Mistake 2: Borrowing the opposite stars but forcing them through this palace's own nature

Mistake 3: Reading only the borrowed main stars and ignoring local assistants, malefics and transformations

Back to Your Own Chart

An empty palace is not blank; its main star sits across and shines over. Borrow the opposite stars, switch to that perspective, then layer local minor stars and transformations; the three steps fill the emptiness.

Reading Order

  1. Cast the natal chart first and locate the palaces and major stars tied to borrowing the opposite palace for empty palaces before judging anything
  2. Open the triple-direction of those palaces and count the auspicious, malefic and assistant stars separately
  3. Check where the natal Four Transformations land to set the chart's baseline tone
  4. Read the decade-limit transformations to see whether the borrowing the opposite palace for empty palaces line is activated in this stage
  5. Then read the annual transformations and the year's palace to pin down this year's trigger and timing
  6. Layer natal, decade and year before concluding, then decide how to act rather than labeling good or bad

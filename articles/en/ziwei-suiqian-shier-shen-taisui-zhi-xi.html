The Twelve Pre-Year Spirits in Zi Wei Dou Shu: A Year's Footnotes Starting From the Annual Grand-Duke

The twelve Pre-Year spirits start at the annual branch (the Grand-Duke palace) as Year-Builder and proceed clockwise: Year-Builder, Gloom, Mourning, Coil, Official, Small Loss, Great Loss, Virtue-Dragon, White Tiger, Heavenly Virtue, Guest-Hanging and Illness. They rotate yearly to mark each palace's joy, blockage and friction as an annual auxiliary layer.

Doctors start from the stem-based Lu Cun, Pre-Generals stay fixed for life, and Pre-Year spirits start from the annual branch and rotate each year—three auxiliary sets from different sources, not to be mixed.

What This Question Is Really Asking

Asking what this year's Year-Builder, Mourning or White Tiger beside each palace means and whether it matters means knowing they run clockwise from this year's Grand-Duke, then weighing them with that palace's stars.

The Underlying Logic

Placement: the annual branch palace (year Life/Grand-Duke) starts Year-Builder, then one per palace clockwise; each year the Grand-Duke moves and the whole set shifts one palace.

Joy and resolution: Year-Builder is the year's host governing its main affairs and atmosphere; Virtue-Dragon turns danger to safety through mediation; Heavenly Virtue brings rescuing help—pressure relief in a good palace, buffer in a bad one.

Block and friction: Gloom delays and lowers mood; Mourning brings worry over loss and stagnation; Coil binds and stalls process; Official brings legal/fine trouble; Small/Great Loss drain money; White Tiger brings injury, clash and accident; Guest-Hanging anxiety; Illness minor ailments.

They act only at the annual level and only add/subtract: a palace with good stars plus Virtue-Dragon/Heavenly Virtue runs smoother; a stressed palace with White Tiger/Coil/Official amplifies that year's friction, again timing down to the month.

A Step-by-Step Way to Read It

  1. Set the asked year's branch palace as the Year-Builder start
  2. Run clockwise placing Year-Builder through Illness
  3. Separate joy/resolution spirits from block/friction ones and mark each palace
  4. Read only their co-residence with that year's stars and transformations
  5. A good base with resolving spirits is a buffer; a stressed base with White Tiger/Official calls for prevention
  6. Overlay months for timing, and remember the set governs only this one year

Three Common Mistakes

Mistake 1: Mixing Pre-Year with Doctor and Pre-General sets without separating sources

Mistake 2: Predicting disaster from White Tiger/Mourning without the stars-and-transformations premise

Mistake 3: Thinking the set is lifelong when it shifts each year with the Grand-Duke

Back to Your Own Chart

The Pre-Year spirits are marginal notes on the annual chart, marking where the year runs smooth or sticks without overriding the stars and transformations. Keep the three auxiliary sets' sources and weight straight and your annual reading stays both detailed and steady.

Reading Order

  1. Cast the natal chart first and locate the palaces and major stars tied to the twelve Pre-Year spirits from the annual branch before judging anything
  2. Open the triple-direction of those palaces and count the auspicious, malefic and assistant stars separately
  3. Check where the natal Four Transformations land to set the chart's baseline tone
  4. Read the decade-limit transformations to see whether the the twelve Pre-Year spirits from the annual branch line is activated in this stage
  5. Then read the annual transformations and the year's palace to pin down this year's trigger and timing
  6. Layer natal, decade and year before concluding, then decide how to act rather than labeling good or bad

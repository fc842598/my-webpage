The Servants Palace in Zi Wei Dou Shu: Whether Subordinates, Friends and Partners You Rely On Hold Up

The Servants Palace, also called the Friends Palace, maps onto three real groups: your subordinates and team, your regular friends, and the partners you do business with. It is not only about whether people help you; it is whether they hold up at key moments and whether they turn on you.

Zi Wei Dou Shu2026-09-28

Hearing Servants Palace, some picture domestic staff. In modern terms it is your team under you, your circle of friends, and the business partners—the outer network you lean on.

What This Question Is Really Asking

Read the three groups separately: subordinates for execution and loyalty, friends for long-term fit, and partners for whether interests are clearly written down. Partnerships ultimately follow a professional lawyer and a written contract; the chart only shows a tendency.

The Underlying Logic

The Servants Palace originally read servants and subordinates, and now extends to teams, reports and assistants—the execution layer.

It also reads friends and the social circle: whether the people around you help or drain you.

Partnerships and joint ventures borrow this palace: whether the other side can share risk and whether money splits will turn ugly.

When a malefic or Hua Ji sits there, watch turnover, draining friends and falling-out partners; for money and equity, rely on written contracts and a lawyer, never on the chart as a legal document.

A Step-by-Step Way to Read It

  1. First locate the Servants Palace and decide whether you ask about subordinates, friends or partners
  2. On the subordinate line: whether the team is stable and key people may leave
  3. On the friend line: whether your circle nourishes or drains you
  4. On the partner line: whether roles, splits and exit terms are written down
  5. If a malefic sits there, do not trust sentiment alone; put key deals in writing
  6. For equity and liability, defer to a professional lawyer, proper channels and a written contract

Three Common Mistakes

Mistake 1: Using the Servants Palace only for benefactors while ignoring subordinate and partner risk

Mistake 2: Assuming an auspicious star means partnering on trust alone with no contract

Mistake 3: Recalling the malefic only after a partnership sours, without earlier written terms

Back to Your Own Chart

The Servants Palace is the network you can lean on. Whether people hold up shows over time; how money splits and responsibilities sit must be written down—the chart only reminds you not to trust sentiment alone.

Reading Order

  1. Cast the natal chart first and locate the palaces and major stars tied to the Servants Palace and subordinate loyalty before judging anything
  2. Open the triple-direction of those palaces and count the auspicious, malefic and assistant stars separately
  3. Check where the natal Four Transformations land to set the chart's baseline tone
  4. Read the decade-limit transformations to see whether the the Servants Palace and subordinate loyalty line is activated in this stage
  5. Then read the annual transformations and the year's palace to pin down this year's trigger and timing
  6. Layer natal, decade and year before concluding, then decide how to act rather than labeling good or bad

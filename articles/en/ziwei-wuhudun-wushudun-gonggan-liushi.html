Where Palace Stems Come From in Zi Wei Dou Shu: Five-Tiger for Palace Stems and Five-Rat for Hour Stems

The stem above each palace is not random: palace stems derive from the birth-year stem via the Five-Tiger rule starting at Yin for the first month, while flowing-hour stems derive from the day stem via the Five-Rat rule starting at Zi. Palace stems are the origin of flying transformations; get them wrong and every later flying transformation is wrong—a required skill for casting and checking charts.

App casters rarely notice where palace stems come from, but to check a chart, cast flowing hours or fly transformations by palace stem, the Five-Tiger and Five-Rat derivations are unavoidable底层 algorithms.

What This Question Is Really Asking

Asking why your palace stems differ from another app, or which stem a flying transformation starts from, you must derive the stems by hand with Five-Tiger/Five-Rat to find the error.

The Underlying Logic

Five-Tiger places palace stems: the birth-year stem sets the stem of the Yin palace (first month) by the mnemonic—Jia/Ji years start with Bing, Yi/Geng with Wu, Bing/Xin with Geng, Ding/Ren with Ren, Wu/Gui with Jia at Yin—then stems run in order from Yin around to Chou, fixing all twelve palace stems.

Why Five-Tiger: Yin is the Tiger in zodiac signs and the first month always starts the stem at Yin; five year-stem groups map to five starting stems at Yin, hence Five-Tiger—essentially a fixed mapping plus sequential placement.

Five-Rat places flowing-hour stems: the day stem sets the Zi-hour stem by the mnemonic—Jia/Ji add Jia, Yi/Geng start with Bing, Bing/Xin from Wu, Ding/Ren at Geng-Zi, Wu/Gui at Ren-Zi—then stems run through the twelve watches. Zi is the Rat and the entry is fixed at Zi, hence Five-Rat, used for flowing hours and finer time layers.

Palace stems are the origin of flying transformations: a palace stem gives that palace the ability to send Lu/Quan/Ke/Ji to specific palaces. Five-Tiger must place palace stems correctly before flying and self-transformations have a valid takeoff. If flying results look odd when checking a chart, first recheck the year stem and Five-Tiger palace stems, then the app's calendar and leap-month settings.

A Step-by-Step Way to Read It

  1. Get the exact birth-year stem as Five-Tiger input
  2. Use the Five-Tiger mnemonic to set the Yin stem, then place all twelve palace stems and cross-check with the app
  3. For flowing hours, fix the day stem and use the Five-Rat mnemonic for the Zi-hour stem
  4. Run stems through the twelve watches to get each flowing-hour stem
  5. Use the correct palace stems as takeoff for flying and self-transformations
  6. When checking, trace odd flying results first to Five-Tiger stems, then calendar, leap month and birth hour

Three Common Mistakes

Mistake 1: Assuming palace stems are random, not knowing they derive from the year stem via Five-Tiger

Mistake 2: Mixing the Five-Tiger and Five-Rat mnemonics, confusing the Yin entry with the Zi entry

Mistake 3: Flying transformations from wrongly placed palace stems, cascading errors across the chart

The Tenth Month of Bing-Wu 2026: Leaving Written Records Under the Monthly Transformations

In the tenth month, Hai, the Ji stem brings Wu-Qu Lu, Tan-Lang Quan, Tian-Liang Ke and Wen-Qu Ji. When discussing income, partnership and exposure, things are easily agreed verbally but left hanging; write down owner, deadline, acceptance criteria and payment status in a record.

Zi Wei Dou Shu2026-09-27

This month partnerships are discussed warmly and terms sound full, yet Wen-Qu Ji sits right on messages, verbal promises and paperwork; spoken words do not count, only what is written does.

What This Question Is Really Asking

Wu-Qu Lu helps with income talks, Tan-Lang Quan with resources and exposure, Tian-Liang Ke with reputation backing, but Wen-Qu Ji warns: every promise must be recorded. Land each cooperation on four fields: who owns it, the deadline, the acceptance criteria and the payment stage; where contracts and rights are concerned, defer to professional lawyers, proper channels and written agreements.

The Underlying Logic

The tenth Hai month Ji-stem transformations: Wu-Qu Lu for income, Tan-Lang Quan for resources and exposure, Tian-Liang Ke for reputation, Wen-Qu Ji for messages and paperwork.

Lu, Quan and Ke together favor talks on money, resources and exposure.

Wen-Qu Ji warns verbal promises drift and that messages and documents are the month's weak point.

Cooperation must leave traces, turning verbal consensus into checkable written fields.

A Step-by-Step Way to Read It

  1. Confirm where the Hai-month Ji-stem transformations land
  2. Identify the cooperation topics Lu, Quan and Ke activate: income, resources, exposure, reputation
  3. Find which cooperation step Wen-Qu Ji hits in messages or paperwork
  4. Write down the owner and deadline for each cooperation
  5. Write down acceptance criteria and a deliverables list
  6. Write down payment status: deposit, milestone and balance stages

Three Common Mistakes

Mistake 1: Treating a warm chat as a settled deal with no written record

Mistake 2: Taking Wen-Qu Ji as mere chat friction without paperwork

Mistake 3: Relying on words rather than a written contract for rights and obligations

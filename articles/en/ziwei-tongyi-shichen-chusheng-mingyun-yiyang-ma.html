Do People Born in the Same Shichen Share the Same Fate?

People born in the same shichen (two-hour window) produce similar chart structures, but their fates are not the same. The chart gives shared tendencies and probability ranges; environment, family, effort and key choices separate outcomes. Time is infinitely divisible, so shichen-based reading has inherent limitations.

What This Question Is Really Asking

People ask whether those born in the same shichen share fate, with the underlying question does my effort still matter; the answer is the chart only gives tendencies, effort and choice are the variables.

The Underlying Logic

People born within the same shichen (two hours) produce essentially the same chart structure.

But actual fates differ greatly: family, region, education, choices and effort all vary.

Zi Wei uses two-hour shichen units, and time is infinitely divisible, an inherent limitation of the system.

The chart gives probability ranges and tendencies, not a single precise script for each individual.

A Step-by-Step Way to Read It

  1. Understand that same-shichen charts are structurally similar but do not mean the same fate
  2. Read the chart as tendencies and probability ranges, not verdicts
  3. Compare people of the same shichen to see differences from environment and choice
  4. Find optimizable directions in your own chart rather than looking for destiny
  5. Use decades and years for timing, and let effort and choice change how much materializes
  6. Stay open, do not give up personal effort because charts are similar

Three Common Mistakes

Mistake 1: Believing people born in the same shichen have exactly the same fate

Mistake 2: Denying the role of personal effort and choice because charts are similar

Mistake 3: Treating probability ranges as precise individual verdicts

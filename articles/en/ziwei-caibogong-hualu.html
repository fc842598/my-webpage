Wealth Palace With Hua Lu: Earning Well and Keeping It Are Two Different Skills

Hua Lu in the Wealth Palace means earning opportunities come easily. But money arriving and money staying are different matters. You need the Property and Mental palaces too.

Zi Wei Dou Shu2026-08-11 10:15

A quick note on terms. In Zi Wei Dou Shu, the Wealth Palace (also called the Money Palace) shows how you earn and how you relate to cash flow. Hua Lu is the "Resource Star" — the transformation that brings ease and incoming support. When Hua Lu lands in the Wealth Palace, earning tends to feel natural. Opportunities show up. Clients say yes. But in this system, real wealth is read across three palaces — Wealth, Property, and Mental — and Hua Lu in just one of them tells you about the faucet, not the tank.

This article covers the main combinations: when this placement genuinely builds savings, when it means you earn a lot and spend a lot, and when the money comes at a cost to your health or peace of mind.

First Question: Where Does the Money Come From?

Before asking how much, ask what kind. The main star carrying the Hua Lu sets the direction of the income.

Wu Qu (the "Finance Star") with Hua Lu points to steady, professional income — finance, engineering, skilled trades. Tai Yin (the "Moon Star") with Hua Lu favors savings, investments, real estate, and industries tied to women. Tai Yang (the "Sun Star") with Hua Lu earns through visibility, reputation, and public platforms — the money comes with attention and overhead. Tian Tong (the "Comfort Star") with Hua Lu brings easy money, sometimes through inheritance or lifestyle businesses, but Tian Tong is also the star that says "enough is enough" and stops pushing. Tan Lang (the "Desire Star") with Hua Lu brings side income, networking money, and multiple revenue streams — but also multiple appetites.

Once you know the direction, look for Lu Cun (the "Storage Star"). Hua Lu is the spring; Lu Cun is the bucket. A spring without a bucket runs through.

Hua Lu With Lu Cun: The Combination That Actually Builds Savings

Hua Lu and Lu Cun sharing the Wealth Palace is one of the strongest money combinations. Hua Lu opens the income channel; Lu Cun closes the spending leaks. People with this pairing earn well and hold what they earn. The side effect is that Lu Cun makes you careful with money — sometimes to the point that others see you as stingy.

If Lu Cun sits not in Wealth but in the opposite Property Palace, the effect is also strong: earned money converts into real estate and assets. This is called "money entering the storehouse." If Lu Cun is in the Mental Palace, you earn to fund a quality of life — you spend on comfort, hobbies, and experiences, and that spending is what motivates you. You're not a miser; you're someone who works to live well.

A concrete pattern: Wealth Palace has Wu Qu with Hua Lu, and the Property Palace holds Lu Cun with Tian Fu (the "Treasury Star"). Wu Qu earns through skill, Hua Lu brings the work in, Lu Cun and Tian Fu in Property convert it into assets. This is someone who builds net worth, not just income.

With Challenging Stars: Big Earners, Big Spenders

Hua Lu plus Di Kong or Di Jie (the "Emptiness Stars") is the classic "money passes through" pattern. Income is real, but so is the outflow — bad investments, loans to friends, impulse buys, sudden expenses. The fix isn't to earn more; it's to automate savings, buy illiquid assets, and keep less cash within reach.

Hua Lu plus Qing Yang (the "Sharpening Star") or Tuo Luo (the "Stagnation Star") makes earning itself a fight. Qing Yang means competition — you win the contract, but you had to scrap for it. Tuo Luo means delay — the money arrives eventually, but you chase it. Neither reduces your lifetime income; both reduce how pleasant the earning feels.

Hua Lu plus Huo Xing (the "Fire Star") or Ling Xing (the "Sirens Star") brings fast money with risk attached. Huo Xing is sudden — a big month, a bad month. Ling Xing is hidden cost — money that leaks away invisibly. Both demand careful contracts and risk controls.

Also check the Mental Palace. Wealth Palace Hua Lu with Mental Palace Hua Ji is a painful mismatch: you earn well but never feel you have enough. Spending brings guilt; saving brings anxiety. The money is there; the peace isn't. Wealth and Mental must be read as a pair.

In a Current Year: How to Handle a Good Money Year

There are three ways the Wealth Palace activates in timing. Read them separately.

First: the yearly Life Palace lands in your natal Wealth Palace. That year revolves around money — earning, negotiating, pricing. The risk is tunnel vision: you trade every hour for income and let health and relationships run on fumes. Push hard, but set a stop time.

Second: a yearly Hua Lu flies into the Wealth Palace. This is a concrete payday year — a raise, a bonus, a big client, an investment gain. Where the Lu flies from tells you the source: from the Career Palace it's a job win, from Property it's a real-estate gain, from the Spouse Palace it's money through a partner, from the Travel Palace it's income from abroad or out of town. A yearly Lu expires. When it lands, bank it.

Third: a ten-year Wealth Palace carries Hua Lu. This is a decade of rising income, but it's back-loaded. The early years are setup; the later years pay off. Don't change direction in year two because the money isn't there yet. Stay on one track and the second half of the decade compounds.

Reading Order After You Cast the Chart

When you find Hua Lu in the Wealth Palace, read it in this order:

  1. Check the main star to identify the income type — professional, investment, visibility, lifestyle, or networking money.
  2. Locate Lu Cun: in the Wealth Palace is strongest, in Property means assets, in Mental means lifestyle spending, absent means weak retention.
  3. Check the Property Palace to see whether earnings convert into assets.
  4. Check the Mental Palace to see whether earning and spending feel good or anxious.
  5. Check for Kong-Jie, Huo-Ling, and Qing Yang to find the leak points.
  6. For timing, separate the yearly Life Palace landing (focus on earning), yearly Lu flying in (a specific payday), and ten-year Lu (slow rise).

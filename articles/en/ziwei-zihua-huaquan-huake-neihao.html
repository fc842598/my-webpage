Self-Transformed Quan and Ke: Forced Control and Face-Consciousness, and Why Energy Dissipates Inside the Palace

When a palace's own stem transforms a star back in that same palace into Quan or Ke, it is self-Quan/self-Ke: self-Quan is willful competitiveness that ends hollow, self-Ke is self-polishing where reputation outruns substance—both cycle energy inside the palace, hard to capture externally.

Zi Wei Dou Shu2026-09-08

Natal transformations are given at birth, flying transformations pass between palaces, and self-transformation is given to oneself; it describes an inner tendency to wrestle with yourself in that matter.

What This Question Is Really Asking

People read self-Quan as holding power and self-Ke as fame. Ask whether a self-transformation can be retained—its common mark is "looks present but actually scatters or self-consumes."

The Underlying Logic

First get the direction: a palace stem transforms Lu/Quan/Ke/Ji and the transformed star sits in that same palace—self-transformation, not outside help but energy the palace generates and reclaims itself.

Self-Quan: willful, unwilling to lose, needing the final word in that matter; drive is real short-term, but because energy does not go outward and cooperation is lacking, one wins a hollow title or strains relations, ending empty.

Self-Ke: face-conscious, decorous, skilled at self-polishing and softening, strong first impression, but reputation can outrun real accumulation; substance must catch up with the polished surface.

Self-Quan/self-Ke, with self-Lu/self-Ji, are four faces of one phenomenon—energy cycling inward. The fix is not suppression but turning internal friction into deliverables: self-Quan learns to delegate and land things, self-Ke converts reputation into portfolio and results.

A Step-by-Step Way to Read It

  1. Go palace by palace to see whether a stem-transformed star sits back inside, identifying self-transformation
  2. Tell self-Quan from self-Ke to set the inner pattern (competitive control vs. face/polish)
  3. Read by palace: Life is temperament, Career is work style, Spouse is relating style
  4. Check whether natal or flying transformations come from outside to receive it; external response offsets internal drain
  5. Adjust: self-Quan practices delegation and landable goals; self-Ke builds portfolio so reputation equals strength
  6. Use the decade to spot when internal friction peaks and design external feedback and delivery nodes in advance

Three Common Mistakes

Mistake 1: Taking self-Quan as real power and self-Ke as real fame while the energy does not stay

Mistake 2: Reading only natal transformations and missing the self-wrestling hidden line

Mistake 3: Treating self-transformation as bad luck when it is a convertible behavior pattern, not a verdict

Back to Your Own Chart

Self-Quan and self-Ke are inward mirrors showing where you force control or keep up appearances. Find them on your chart not to blame yourself but to redirect that cycling energy outward—Quan must land as real authority, Ke as genuine skill.

Reading Order

  1. Cast the natal chart first and locate the palaces and major stars tied to self-transformed Quan and self-transformed Ke before judging anything
  2. Open the triple-direction of those palaces and count the auspicious, malefic and assistant stars separately
  3. Check where the natal Four Transformations land to set the chart's baseline tone
  4. Read the decade-limit transformations to see whether the self-transformed Quan and self-transformed Ke line is activated in this stage
  5. Then read the annual transformations and the year's palace to pin down this year's trigger and timing
  6. Layer natal, decade and year before concluding, then decide how to act rather than labeling good or bad

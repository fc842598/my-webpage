Zi Wei Dou Shu Career Palace: Authority or Expertise for Promotion?

Use Hua Quan and Hua Ke to separate decision authority from evidence of professional work in a promotion review.

Zi Wei Dou Shu2026-11-21 09:04

A team is choosing a lead. One candidate approves changes and handles cross-team tradeoffs. Another has built a reliable method that colleagues and clients reuse. Both may look strong in a Zi Wei Dou Shu career reading, but a promotion review needs different evidence for authority and expertise.

Locate the transformation first

Check whether Hua Quan or Hua Ke belongs to the natal chart, a ten-year cycle, or the current year. Record the palace where it sits and whether it meets the Career Palace directly or through connected palaces. The carrying star and the actual job matter too. A chart marker cannot grant an employee a title or an approval right.

Test authority against responsibility

For a management track, list the decisions the person can make, budget limits, escalation rules, and the outcomes they must own. Someone may chair meetings yet lack permission to approve a supplier change. Another may carry the blame for delays without access to the approval system. Hua Quan directs attention to this gap; the written role and workflow show what is real.

Test expertise against reproducible work

For a specialist track, save examples, review notes, error rates, and methods that others can use. A polished report with unchecked numbers is weak evidence, while a documented fix that colleagues reproduce is strong evidence. If both transformations matter, prepare a decision record and a separate portfolio of technical work. Read the chart in order, then compare it with the company's promotion criteria and available roles.

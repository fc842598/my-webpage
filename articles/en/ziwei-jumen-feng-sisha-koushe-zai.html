Ju Men and the Four Malefics: Reading Communication Under Pressure

Read Ju Men with Qing Yang, Tuo Luo, Huo Xing, and Ling Xing in their actual positions. A communication theme is not proof of gossip, dishonesty, or a legal dispute.

Zi Wei Dou Shu2026-09-09Updated 2026-11-10 14:36 (UTC+8)

Ju Men is traditionally associated with speech, inquiry, and examining a question closely. The four malefics discussed here are Qing Yang, Tuo Luo, Huo Xing, and Ling Xing. Their involvement can frame a reading about friction or pressure, but it does not show that someone is a troublemaker.

Say How the Stars Are Connected

Sharing a palace, appearing in the opposite palace, and participating through the triad are different arrangements. Do not count every difficult star anywhere in the chart as the same combination. Identify Ju Men, its placement, the relevant malefics, and the timing layer before discussing an amplified theme.

Keep Transformations Attached to Real Stars

Hua Lu and Hua Ke are not universal switches that turn disagreement off. Identify which star actually carries a transformation under the calculation settings being used. Support from another star is different from Ju Men itself transforming. Hua Ji is a technical term, not an English label meaning jealousy or dishonesty.

Compare With the Actual Conversation

A person questioning an unclear instruction is not necessarily arguing. A technically accurate point delivered without context may still be hard to follow. Ask what was said, what each person understood, and which part remains unresolved. Use the chart to organize that discussion, not to predict a lawsuit or accuse an absent person of spreading rumors.

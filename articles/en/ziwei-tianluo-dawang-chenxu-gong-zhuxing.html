The Net of Heaven and Earth in Zi Wei Dou Shu: Are Stars in Chen and Xu Always Bad

Chen is the Net of Heaven and Xu the Net of Earth; stars here often feel bound and unable to express. Yet the net is not a malefic—it is more like an enclosed period for settling and breaking out, and when used well it becomes a late-bloomer structure.

Zi Wei Dou Shu2026-09-09

Many see the Life Palace or a key star in Chen/Xu and assume life is trapped. In fact the net describes confinement before breakthrough; the question is whether the star has power and when it walks out.

What This Question Is Really Asking

Asking whether Life in Chen/Xu, or Zi Wei-Xiang in the net, is hopeless means first understanding these two palaces, then the star combination and decade, rather than condemning Chen/Xu on sight.

The Underlying Logic

Chen and Xu are the storage branches and the Net of Heaven and Earth; stars here are gathered as if in a net, outward force slows, and temperament turns inward, enduring and reflective, often feeling constrained early on.

The net binds speed, not ceiling: a strong star here (Zi Wei, Tian Fu, an empowered Sun) is often suppressed first and erupts later, with firmer roots when it walks out in midlife—a late bloomer.

Three tests: is the star exalted or fallen, does its triple-direction offer Lu/Quan/Ke and assistants as an exit, and when does the decade reach opening palaces such as Hai-Zi-Chou or Si-Wu-Wei.

Only when a netted star also meets clustered malefics and Ji with no auspicious exit is it a structure prone to long internal friction; then deliberately changing environment and building skills is how you break the net.

A Step-by-Step Way to Read It

  1. Confirm whether Life or the asked palace sits in Chen or Xu, Net of Heaven or Earth
  2. Read the seated star's brightness to tell a strong star held down from a genuinely weak one
  3. Open the triple-direction and find the net opening offered by Hua Lu/Quan/Ke and assistants
  4. Check whether malefics and Ji cluster with no rescue, distinguishing temporary settling from long entrapment
  5. Lay out decades to find which one reaches an opening palace—that is the breakout window
  6. Accumulate instead of forcing while netted, then push when the exit luck arrives; it beats reckless early effort

Three Common Mistakes

Mistake 1: Condemning Chen/Xu on sight and treating the net as a malefic

Mistake 2: Seeing the net but ignoring star strength and exits, missing the late-bloomer possibility

Mistake 3: Forcing ahead during confinement instead of using the decade window to break out in rhythm

Back to Your Own Chart

The net is not a verdict but a weave that lets you sink in first. See whether your netted star has power and which decade opens the exit, and Chen/Xu stops being a fear and becomes a phase of quiet accumulation before release.

Reading Order

  1. Cast the natal chart first and locate the palaces and major stars tied to the Chen-Xu Net of Heaven and Earth before judging anything
  2. Open the triple-direction of those palaces and count the auspicious, malefic and assistant stars separately
  3. Check where the natal Four Transformations land to set the chart's baseline tone
  4. Read the decade-limit transformations to see whether the the Chen-Xu Net of Heaven and Earth line is activated in this stage
  5. Then read the annual transformations and the year's palace to pin down this year's trigger and timing
  6. Layer natal, decade and year before concluding, then decide how to act rather than labeling good or bad

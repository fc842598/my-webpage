What Is Self-Transformation: Why a Palace's Own Four Changes Come and Go Quickly

Self-transformation happens when a palace's own transformation lands on a star in that same palace, and it is read as quick to arrive and quick to fade.

Zi Wei Dou Shu2026-11-16 10:35

Self-transformation is a special case of flying stars: the four transformations produced by a palace's own stem happen to land on a star sitting in that same palace. It differs from the birth-year transformations and leans toward later change, often read as quick to arrive and quick to fade. Understanding the structure comes before reading its real-life effects.

How it forms

Every palace has its own stem, and that stem produces one of the four transformations. If the transformed star sits in the same palace, it is called self-transformation. For example, a palace stem may produce a prosperity transformation on a star sitting right there, forming self-prosperity. The energy turns within the palace rather than flying elsewhere.

How the four types read

Self-prosperity often means benefits arrive quickly and also leave quickly, so something should be set aside in good times. Self-power means wanting to take charge and easily pushing too hard at once. Self-reputation leans toward name and surface smoothness, while the challenging type suggests the topic may repeat and be hard to keep. It describes later, dynamic change rather than a static birth-year base.

Step-by-step reading

  1. Find the target palace's stem and confirm which transformation it produces.
  2. Check whether the transformed star sits in the same palace, which is required for self-transformation.
  3. Separate the four types rather than applying one verdict to all.
  4. Use the palace topic to judge whether the change is quick, controlling, or hard to keep.
  5. Then check the opposite and supporting palaces to see whether outside forces strengthen or weaken it.

Reading limits

Self-transformation describes later change tendencies; it does not guarantee things will fall apart or that stability is impossible. In real life, keeping room in good times and settling results into something durable reduces the quick-come-quick-go loss. The chart flags rhythm, while management decides whether things stay.

Chart reading order

  1. Fix the chart and mark the target palace's stem and its stars.
  2. Confirm the stem's transformation first, then judge whether the matching star sits in the palace.
  3. Classify by the four types rather than mixing their wording.
  4. Include the opposite and supporting palaces to see the outside environment.
  5. Match the conclusion to real arrangements without absolute success-or-failure claims.

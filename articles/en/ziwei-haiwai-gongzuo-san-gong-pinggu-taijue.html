Reading Overseas Work in Zi Wei Dou Shu: Assess Travel, Career and Wealth Together

For overseas assignment or development, assess three palaces together: Travel for the stage (whether you can go and how smoothly), Career for the role (what you do and growth), and Wealth for salary and tax (how much and how to keep it); then prepare contract, insurance, tax and exit checklists, with the actual offer and contract prevailing.

Zi Wei Dou Shu2026-09-26

Overseas work needs more than the Travel palace: whether you can go is one matter, what you do there and whether you earn and save are two more, so read all three.

What This Question Is Really Asking

The landing checklist matters: after Travel and Career look good, clarify offer terms, salary and tax, insurance and healthcare, visa status and an exit path; base major decisions on written contracts and proper institutional information rather than longing alone.

The Underlying Logic

The Travel Palace shows the overseas stage: whether you can go, how it goes and adaptation.

The Career Palace shows the role: position, growth and professional fit.

The Wealth Palace shows salary, tax, living cost and actual savings.

Major decisions rest on the actual offer, written contract and proper tax and insurance arrangements.

A Step-by-Step Way to Read It

  1. Read Travel to judge whether you can go and how it goes
  2. Read Career to assess the position and growth
  3. Read Wealth to calculate salary, tax and actual savings
  4. Check the three palaces for malefics or Ji to spot risks
  5. Prepare contract, insurance, tax, visa and exit checklists
  6. Make the final decision on the actual offer and written contract

Three Common Mistakes

Mistake 1: Judging overseas suitability from Travel alone

Mistake 2: Ignoring role growth, salary and tax and finding a gap after arriving

Mistake 3: Skipping contract, insurance and exit preparation and deciding on impulse

Back to Your Own Chart

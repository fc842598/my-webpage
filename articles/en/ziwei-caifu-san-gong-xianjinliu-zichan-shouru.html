Why Wealth Needs Three Palaces: Cash Flow, Assets and Income Source Are Not the Same

The Wealth Palace reads cash flow and spending habits, the Property Palace fixed assets and accumulated wealth, and the Career Palace the income your work provides; together they form the full financial structure. One palace alone misreads cases such as earning well yet saving little or holding a high title with tight cash.

Zi Wei Dou Shu2026-10-01

Many read wealth only through the Wealth Palace, then wonder why a good income never accumulates or why sizable assets coexist with tight cash. Wealth is really three things: how money flows, how it is stored, and where income comes from.

What This Question Is Really Asking

The question is which palaces wealth requires and how to read them together. First separate cash flow, stored assets and income source, then see how they combine on your chart.

The Underlying Logic

The Wealth Palace is cash flow: how income arrives, money is spent, turnover works and what risk you prefer, showing your current relationship with money.

The Property Palace is stored assets: real estate and possessions that settle and accumulate, showing long-term capacity and whether wealth is kept.

The Career Palace is the income source: what work and role earn the money and how stable it is, showing the upstream of cash flow.

Read them together: strong Wealth with weak Property means money passes through; strong Career with weak Wealth often means a high title but limited cash; strong Property with tight cash means solid assets but careful present spending.

A Step-by-Step Way to Read It

  1. First locate the Wealth, Property and Career palaces and read their major stars and brightness
  2. Read the Wealth Palace to judge cash flow and spending habits
  3. Read the Property Palace to judge accumulated assets and capacity
  4. Read the Career Palace to judge whether the income source is stable and growing
  5. Layer the three to locate whether your weak point is flow, storage or source
  6. With the decade’s ten-year financial cycle, decide which to strengthen first rather than fixating on income

Three Common Mistakes

Mistake 1: Reducing wealth to the Wealth Palace while ignoring assets and income source

Mistake 2: Assuming high income means rich without checking flow and storage

Mistake 3: Treating signals such as Great Expenditure or void stars as temporary without reading the long-term structure

Back to Your Own Chart

Wealth is the combined result of cash flow, assets and income source. Reading the three palaces together shows whether to fix turnover, storage or source, which is more actionable than one number.

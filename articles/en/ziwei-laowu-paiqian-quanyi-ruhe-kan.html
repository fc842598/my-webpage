Zi Wei Dou Shu on Labor-Dispatch Rights: A Three-Party Relationship

Labor dispatch means you contract with the dispatch firm but work at the using company, corresponding to the Career and Friends Palaces being tied together. The key is recognizing the three-party relationship and verifying the labor contract, social insurance and equal pay, rather than accepting lower treatment as an outsider.

Zi Wei Dou Shu2026-10-29

Labor dispatch is not second-class employment; equal pay, social insurance and a contract are all required.

What This Question Is Really Asking

Some are dispatched for years without a proper contract, with underpaid insurance and lower treatment than regular staff, and never make claims, their rights discounted over time.

The Underlying Logic

Labor dispatch links the Career and Friends Palaces, marking a split between where you work and where you are contracted.

The dispatch firm should sign a contract of at least two years and pay social insurance, and the using company must provide equal pay for equal work.

Positions should generally be temporary, auxiliary or substitute, and dispatched labor has a ratio cap.

For work injury, unpaid wages or being returned, clarify which party is responsible and rely on the contract to claim.

A Step-by-Step Way to Read It

  1. Confirm whether this is labor dispatch and clarify the dispatch firm, using company and you
  2. Check whether a contract of at least two years is signed and social insurance is paid in full
  3. Confirm equal pay and whether the position meets the three conditions
  4. Read the Career and Friends Palaces to judge relationship stability
  5. For unpaid wages, injury or return, use the contract to determine the responsible party
  6. Where needed, use labor arbitration, with the written contract as the basis

Three Common Mistakes

Mistake 1: Treating dispatch as casual work without a contract or insurance

Mistake 2: Accepting lower pay for the same work

Mistake 3: Not knowing the responsible party when problems arise and being passed between parties

How to Read the Annual Tian Gou Star: Loss, Disfigurement and Small Accidents, Guarding Bumps and Watching Over Children and Pets

Tian Gou is one of the annual auxiliary stars, traditionally governing loss, disfigurement, small accidents and bumps, as well as small situations related to pets and children, and things easily getting damaged. Yet auxiliary stars only act as a temporary plus-minus layer; the major stars and four transformations carry the real reading, and one cannot conclude misfortune from Tian Gou alone. Seeing it should instead prompt paying attention to safety that year, watching over children and pets, taking care of belongings, and not taking small losses to heart.

When Tian Gou appears in the annual cycle, one often feels that year brings more bumps and bruises, things breaking more easily, or small situations with pets and children; it is more a small reminder for safety and belongings than a major disaster.

What This Question Is Really Asking

The question is what the annual Tian Gou indicates and how to respond. First understand that auxiliary stars are only a temporary plus-minus layer, then see which palace it sits in and how the sharing major star and malefics look.

The Underlying Logic

Tian Gou governs loss, disfigurement, small accidents and bumps; whichever palace it sits in, that palace’s matters tend to have small injuries and small financial losses.

It also governs matters related to pets, children and small animals, and that year tends to bring worry or small expenses in these areas.

Auxiliary stars must be read with major stars and transformations: only when the same palace has malefics, especially Qing Yang or fire stars, and the decade activates it should external injury, disfigurement and property loss be a focus; with auspicious stars it is mostly small bumps and small losses with limited impact.

The right response is being careful without anxiety: pay attention to safety in travel and sports, watch over children and pets, store valuables properly, and do not amplify small losses into psychological burden.

A Step-by-Step Way to Read It

  1. First find which palace Tian Gou sits in on the annual chart
  2. Read the palace’s major star, transformations and malefics to judge whether it is small-loss-related or needs focused prevention
  3. In Life or Travel Palace: guard against bumps in travel, sports and driving
  4. In Children or Property Palace: watch over children and pets and pay attention to home safety
  5. Store valuables properly and avoid unnecessary high-risk operations
  6. Deal with small losses promptly and do not take them to heart in a way that affects mood

Three Common Mistakes

Mistake 1: Seeing Tian Gou and concluding the year brings disfigurement and major loss while ignoring major stars, transformations and probability

Mistake 2: Daring to do nothing because of fear of loss, disrupting normal life

Mistake 3: Neglecting safety and supervision, turning small bumps into real injuries

Back to Your Own Chart

The annual Tian Gou is a reminder about small losses, bumps and children and pets. Paying attention to safety, watching over the people and animals around you, and not taking small losses to heart makes it only a small warning layer, not destined depletion.

Reading Order

  1. Cast the natal chart first and locate the palaces and major stars tied to the annual Tian Gou star and how to read it before judging anything
  2. Open the triple-direction of those palaces and count the auspicious, malefic and assistant stars separately
  3. Check where the natal Four Transformations land to set the chart's baseline tone
  4. Read the decade-limit transformations to see whether the the annual Tian Gou star and how to read it line is activated in this stage
  5. Then read the annual transformations and the year's palace to pin down this year's trigger and timing
  6. Layer natal, decade and year before concluding, then decide how to act rather than labeling good or bad

Qi Sha Star in Life Palace: The General Who Carries All

Qi Sha in the Life Palace brings ferocity, decisiveness, and resilience — a born pioneer. But it also means isolation; too much independence means fighting alone, and learning to leverage others is the lesson.

Main Stars2026-08-16 10:15

People with Qi Sha in the Life Palace have a "get out of my way" aura. You decide fast, stay calm in chaos, and solve rather than panic. You carry what others can’t. But the problem is being too independent — so independent that you don’t trust others, carry everything alone, and leave people feeling you don’t need them.

Qi Sha is element Metal (and Fire), the general star, ruling assault, pioneering, and authority. Unlike Zi Wei the marshal commanding from the tent, Qi Sha is the vanguard leading the charge. Life is dramatic, but you’ve never been afraid — you were born for hard fights.

Core Traits

First, decisive — quick calls, no agonizing. Second, pressure-resistant — calmer in chaos. Third, independent — you hate asking for help. Fourth, fierce — when temper hits, no one stops you.

You’re the firefighter — the hardest tasks come to you. Valuable but exhausted. You think "I’d rather do it myself" and end up doing everything.

Suits military/police, entrepreneurship, surgery, competitive sports — anything requiring daring and backbone. Routine desk work would suffocate you.

The Isolation

Like Wu Qu, Metal gives Qi Sha an isolated edge — thin family bonds, perhaps leaving home young, poor communication with relatives. Marriage needs work because you’re strong and bad at showing tenderness.

With Qing Yang: fiercest temper, prone to injury or lawsuits. With Tuo Luo: delayed explosion. With Huo Ling: short-fused but quick to cool.

It isn’t hopeless. Learning to say "I need you," delegating, and softening in intimacy makes life much lighter.

Qi Sha, Zi Wei, and Tian Fu

Qi Sha always opposes Zi Wei and Tian Fu. This creates natural tension with authority and resources — you need them but resist them.

If Zi Wei is strong (with a full court), Qi Sha is a great general with a platform. If Zi Wei is weak (lonely ruler), Qi Sha becomes a rebel who goes independent.

With Tian Fu it forms part of the "Sha Po Lang" pattern — massive change and opportunity. Qi Sha people don’t fear change; that’s where your ability shows.

No Transformations

Qi Sha never transforms to Lu/Quan/Ke/Ji. It has no inherent bias — its quality depends entirely on companion stars.

With Lu Cun or Hua Lu: "charging with supplies" — profitable. With Hua Quan: heavier authority. With Hua Ke: firm but refined. With another star’s Hua Ji clashing: charge blocked, potential disaster.

In good cycles you become famous in one battle; in bad ones you lose it all. But you have what others don’t: the ability to rise again, stronger.

Reading Order After You Cast the Chart

For Qi Sha in Life:

  1. Which palace — Yin/Shen is best ("Qi Sha facing the dipper"), Zi/Wu is good.
  2. Check opposite Zi Wei/Tian Fu — strong Zi Wei gives platform; strong Tian Fu gives resources.
  3. Check Lu Cun/Hua Lu — reward for the charge.
  4. Check malefics: Qing Yang = injury, Tuo Luo = delay, Huo Ling = impatience.
  5. Check triple: Lian Zhen and Tan Lang determine method and desire.
  6. Are you independent or isolating yourself?

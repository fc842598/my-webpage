How to Read Qing Yang in Zi Wei Dou Shu: Impulsive Punishment or Decisive Execution

Qing Yang is metal and transforms into punishment, one of the four malefics: fallen and guarding the life it often means a hasty, argumentative nature that clashes head-on; strong and restrained it means decisiveness and the ability to achieve where judgment is needed. The key is separating impulsiveness from decisiveness and channeling the force into execution with discipline and buffers.

Zi Wei Dou Shu2026-10-28

Qing Yang is like a blade; unsharpened it harms self and others, sharpened it is clean execution, and the difference is restraint and direction.

What This Question Is Really Asking

Some see Qing Yang and think only of injury, quarrels and disputes without checking whether it is strong or fallen and whether auspicious stars transform it, and become frightened.

The Underlying Logic

Qing Yang is metal, a floating Northern Dipper star that transforms into punishment, governing injury, decision and hardness, fallen in Zi Wu Mao You and strong in Chen Xu Chou Wei.

Fallen and unrestrained, it often shows as haste, argument, falling out easily and pushing matters to the extreme, and with romance stars may show in relationships or injury.

Strong and balanced by Lu, Quan, Ke or auspicious stars, the force turns into resolve and execution, suiting work needing judgment, discipline and pioneering.

Qing Yang does not guarantee disputes; the key is direction and buffer: use the urgency on proper matters, leave room and avoid head-on clashes, turning punishment into authority.

A Step-by-Step Way to Read It

  1. Check whether Qing Yang is strong or fallen to tell decisiveness from haste
  2. Look at the stars sharing and meeting the palace for auspicious or Lu-Quan-Ke restraint
  3. See which palace it occupies to judge where the force mainly applies
  4. When fallen and unrestrained, add discipline and buffers and avoid head-on clashes
  5. Direct the urgency toward matters needing judgment and execution rather than winning or losing
  6. Layer the limits and years to see which stages invite impulse and leave room in advance

Three Common Mistakes

Mistake 1: Thinking only of injury and disputes at the sight of Qing Yang

Mistake 2: Ignoring strength and restraint, taking decisiveness as temper

Mistake 3: Clashing head-on without buffers and pushing matters to extremes

Back to Your Own Chart

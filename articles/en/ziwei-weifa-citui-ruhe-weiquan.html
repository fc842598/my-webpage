Zi Wei Dou Shu on Unlawful Dismissal: A Dispute Sign, Handled by Evidence and Arbitration

Unlawful dismissal is termination without a legal basis, often shown in the chart by Tai Yang or Lian Zhen with Hua Ji, marking disputes and unstable authority. The chart only shows a tendency toward conflict; protecting your rights depends on evidence and labor arbitration, not on the chart.

Zi Wei Dou Shu2026-10-29

The key to unlawful dismissal is whether the reason is lawful and the evidence complete; with a dispute sign, pursue claims step by step through legal procedure.

What This Question Is Really Asking

Some are told verbally to leave and stop coming in anger, only to be marked absent and, having kept no dismissal evidence, find themselves at a disadvantage.

The Underlying Logic

Unlawful dismissal often shows Tai Yang or Lian Zhen with Hua Ji, marking unstable authority and a tendency toward disputes, but this is a tendency, not a verdict.

The employer bears the burden of proving the dismissal lawful, often failing to show unmet hiring conditions or relying on unpublicized rules.

If the dismissal is ruled unlawful, you may demand reinstatement or double severance as damages.

To protect your rights, keep the dismissal notice, messages, attendance and pay records, first negotiate then arbitrate, relying throughout on written evidence.

A Step-by-Step Way to Read It

  1. Confirm the specific reason for termination and whether there is written notice
  2. Judge whether the reason is lawful and the employer can prove it, not relying on verbal claims
  3. Keep dismissal evidence, attendance, pay records and the rulebook
  4. Keep working normally or demand written termination to avoid being marked absent
  5. First negotiate compensation, then file for labor arbitration if it fails
  6. You may seek reinstatement or double damages, subject to the arbitration outcome

Three Common Mistakes

Mistake 1: Leaving on verbal dismissal and being marked absent

Mistake 2: Seeing only the dispute sign in the chart without gathering evidence

Mistake 3: Skipping legal procedure and missing the arbitration time limit

Back to Your Own Chart

Unlawful dismissal appears as a dispute tendency in the chart, not an outcome. Proving the employer acted unlawfully and obtaining compensation depend on evidence and labor arbitration: keep written materials and follow procedure, and where needed rely on professional legal advice.

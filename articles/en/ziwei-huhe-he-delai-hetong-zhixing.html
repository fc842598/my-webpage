Zi Wei Dou Shu Partnership: Compatibility Versus an Enforceable Contract

Read the Friends Palace for working relationships and the Parents Palace for written agreements, then check both before partnering.

Zi Wei Dou Shu2026-11-06 11:34

Two good friends start a business with no relationship problems, yet fall out over division of work and exit terms. Others have an ordinary relationship but cooperate for years through a clear contract. Getting along and having an enforceable contract are different matters, tied to different palaces, and should be checked against real terms rather than friendship alone.

Friends Palace for rapport, Parents Palace for documents

The Friends Palace and its related transformations show whether partners can get along, how strong trust is, and how smoothly communication works. That is the relationship layer. Whether a contract or written rule can actually be enforced belongs to the Parents Palace, which governs formal agreements. Good rapport cannot replace a contract, and a detailed contract cannot guarantee personal chemistry. Reading them separately shows whether the risk lies in the relationship or the paperwork.

Ask the two sets of questions separately

Rapport questions cover values, how disagreements are handled and trust. Enforcement questions cover capital contributions, division of work, decision-making, profit sharing, exit conditions and breach liability. Many partnerships fail not because the relationship was bad, but because “we get along” was mistaken for “the rules are clear.” A Hua Ji in the Friends Palace should not be read as an absolute ban on partnership; it flags an area prone to friction that needs clearer terms and advance agreement, not a blanket rejection.

Case and checklist

One pair were long-time friends but signed only a vague agreement, later disputing daily management, loss sharing and exit valuation. Another pair had an ordinary relationship but wrote out contributions, work, decisions, exits and breach terms clause by clause, and cooperated steadily. Read the Friends Palace first, then the Parents Palace, use the decade and annual cycles for timing, and finally put every enforcement question into the written agreement. The palaces flag risks; whether the partnership works is decided by both the relationship record and the contract.

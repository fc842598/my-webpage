Natal and Annual Transformations Both in the Wealth Palace: How Not to Blend the Layers

How to separate the lifelong natal tone from the short-term annual trigger by their time scales before reading how they overlap.

Zi Wei Dou Shu2026-11-17 10:18

Sometimes the Wealth Palace holds both a natal transformation and a given year's annual transformation, and the two layers of signals are easily read as one. The natal transformation describes a long-term stable tone, while the annual transformation describes a short-term trigger for that year, and their time scales differ. Keeping the two layers apart prevents using one year's fluctuation to explain a long-term pattern.

The time scales of the two transformation layers

The natal transformation follows you for life and describes your long-term tendency and recurring themes around money. The annual transformation acts only in this year as a short-term background and trigger. If both layers are on the same star, first separate which is long term and which is for the year, then see how they overlap.

Step-by-step layering check

  1. Confirm which transformations are in the Wealth Palace and whether each belongs to the natal or annual layer.
  2. Mark the natal transformation separately and understand it as a long-term money pattern.
  3. Mark the annual transformation separately and understand it as this year's short-term trigger.
  4. Read the long-term tone first, then see how the year's background acts on it.
  5. Record the two layers apart before overlaying, rather than reading short-term fluctuation as a long-term verdict.

The short term does not change the long term

The annual transformation makes one part of the year especially smooth or stuck, but it does not directly rewrite the natal long-term tendency. Keeping the time scale steady prevents judgment from swinging on one good or bad year.

Chart reading order

  1. Fix the chart and separate natal from annual transformations.
  2. Read the long-term natal layer first, then the short-term annual layer.
  3. Record the two layers apart, then see the overlap.
  4. Check against the real arrangements of this year.
  5. Keep the conclusion conditional rather than blending the two layers into one.

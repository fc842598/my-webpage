What Is Self-Transformation in Zi Wei Dou Shu: Energy That Leaves and Returns to Itself

When a palace's stem transforms a star that sits back inside that same palace, it is self-transformation. Energy cycles internally rather than flowing out, reflecting the person's inner response pattern in that domain rather than anything the environment imposes.

Zi Wei Dou Shu2026-09-05

Flying is exchange between palaces; self-transformation is a palace relating to itself. It often explains why a matter keeps circling internally.

What This Question Is Really Asking

Reading self-transformation as ordinary Si Hua sends you searching outside, when the issue is the domain's own internal digest-or-drain pattern.

The Underlying Logic

Self-transformation splits into self-Lu, self-Quan, self-Ke and self-Ji, each the palace's own stem returning to it.

Self-Lu comforts and digests things itself, but also settles too easily and does not keep gains long.

Self-Quan pressures and pushes itself, but over-controls and drains in that domain.

Self-Ji needs the most care: self-binding, repeated brooding and gaining-then-losing there, the heaviest inner lesson.

A Step-by-Step Way to Read It

  1. Check each palace whether its stem transforms a star back inside itself; mark all self-transformations
  2. Tell self-Lu/Quan/Ke from self-Ji to set the inner-response tone
  3. Self-Lu: guard against quick satisfaction and lost gains by making external commitments
  4. Self-Quan: guard over-control; learn to delegate and relax
  5. Self-Ji is the key repair: spot self-binding there and deliberately seek outside feedback
  6. Use the decade to see which ten years activate the self-transformation and reveal the pattern

Three Common Mistakes

Mistake 1: Treating self-transformation as ordinary flying and seeking causes in the wrong place

Mistake 2: Memorizing luck only, missing that it is an inner response pattern

Mistake 3: Panicking at self-Ji instead of seeing a fixable lesson

Back to Your Own Chart

Self-transformation mirrors your relationship with yourself. Seeing which palace self-cycles and self-drains reveals the true inner entrance for the work.

Reading Order

  1. Cast the natal chart first and locate the palaces and major stars tied to self-transformation before judging anything
  2. Open the triple-direction of those palaces and count the auspicious, malefic and assistant stars separately
  3. Check where the natal Four Transformations land to set the chart's baseline tone
  4. Read the decade-limit transformations to see whether the self-transformation line is activated in this stage
  5. Then read the annual transformations and the year's palace to pin down this year's trigger and timing
  6. Layer natal, decade and year before concluding, then decide how to act rather than labeling good or bad

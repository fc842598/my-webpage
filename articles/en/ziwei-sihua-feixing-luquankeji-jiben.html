The Four Transformations in Zi Wei Dou Shu: What Lu, Quan, Ke and Ji Actually Say

Hua Lu gives resources and opportunity, Hua Quan gives initiative and pressure, Hua Ke gives reputation and cushion, and Hua Ji gives attachment and a lesson. The four transformations describe where energy flows, not simple good or bad luck; read them together as a current.

Zi Wei Dou Shu2026-09-27

Many smile at Hua Lu and fear Hua Ji, treating the four as four fortune cards. They are better read as four forces: some open resources, some push you to act, some give you face, and some keep you attached.

What This Question Is Really Asking

The four transformations answer where energy flows: from which palace resources enter, who holds the initiative, where reputation lands, and what keeps one attached. Connecting the four forces into a line reveals how a matter moves.

The Underlying Logic

Hua Lu stands for resources, opportunity and ease; where it lands, that area tends to receive a flow of fresh supply.

Hua Quan stands for initiative, control and pressure; where it lands, that area is driven and must carry weight.

Hua Ke stands for reputation, paperwork and cushion; where it lands, that area tends to have face and room to maneuver.

Hua Ji stands for attachment, blockage and a lesson; where it lands, that area recurs as a worry to face. In the 2026 Bing-Wu year the yearly transformations are Tian Tong Lu, Tian Ji Quan, Wen Chang Ke and Lian Zhen Ji, useful as a reference.

A Step-by-Step Way to Read It

  1. Mark the natal transformations by the birth year stem and their palaces
  2. Separate the four forces Lu, Quan, Ke and Ji by where each lands
  3. Trace where Lu comes from and where Ji goes to, clarifying resource in and out
  4. See which palace Quan lands in to judge who holds initiative and pressure
  5. See which palace Ke lands in to locate reputation and cushion
  6. Connect the four forces into a current and judge smooth or stuck rather than reading one star alone

Three Common Mistakes

Mistake 1: Declaring good at Lu and bad at Ji without the flow

Mistake 2: Treating transformations as isolated events apart from the palaces they fly into and out of

Mistake 3: Keeping only natal transformations and ignoring temporary decade and yearly ones

Back to Your Own Chart

The four transformations are four currents, not four fortune cards. Once you see where resources flow and where attachment stops, the chart turns from a pile of stars into a readable river.

Reading Order

  1. Cast the natal chart first and locate the palaces and major stars tied to four transformations basics: Lu Quan Ke Ji before judging anything
  2. Open the triple-direction of those palaces and count the auspicious, malefic and assistant stars separately
  3. Check where the natal Four Transformations land to set the chart's baseline tone
  4. Read the decade-limit transformations to see whether the four transformations basics: Lu Quan Ke Ji line is activated in this stage
  5. Then read the annual transformations and the year's palace to pin down this year's trigger and timing
  6. Layer natal, decade and year before concluding, then decide how to act rather than labeling good or bad

Questions like "同时有三件大事想问，先按什么顺序排，才不互相污染答案" look like minor technique questions, but they decide whether the whole later reading will drift. If 主线优先级 and 问题之间的依赖关系 are not set correctly first, the answer often follows mood and approximation instead of structure.

Birth-time boundaries, calendar conversion, and asking order are not small details. Once the entry point is corrected, the same topic usually becomes far more specific and testable.

Set the input and the asking order correctly first

The key move is not asking one extra question. It is setting 主线优先级 and 问题之间的依赖关系 correctly before the reading opens up.

Once the entry is stable, you can tell whether later vagueness comes from missing data or from a question that is still too broad.

Where the question most easily drifts

If you want to ask about a job move, a long-distance relationship, and a housing decision, the best order is usually the question that reshapes the other two, not the one that feels most urgent.

When three big questions are asked in one lump, the answer often sounds full but stays unranked. Splitting the order usually makes the judgment cleaner.

Do not rush to the conclusion first

Do not treat calibration as wasted turns. Many empty follow-ups come from a weak first setup.

And do not rush toward the result first. If the entry is unstable, more result-chasing usually creates more error.

A steadier asking order

  1. Shrink the input range until it becomes comparable.
  2. Use one or two easy verification points to test the difference.
  3. Only then move into the full follow-up.

Income Responsibility, Family Time, and Risk: How to Land the Three Sentences

How to write each of the three core matters as one concrete, verifiable sentence and then make them work through execution and review.

Zi Wei Dou Shu2026-11-17 10:45

Between a partner's career and the family, the core is really three things: how income responsibility is divided, how family time is protected, and who carries the risk. Writing each as one clear, executable sentence is more useful than repeatedly saying "support me." Once the three sentences land, many vague areas in the relationship become clear.

What the three sentences each say

Income responsibility says who carries how much fixed expense and in what ratio. Family time says which periods are fixed for family and how to communicate when busy. Risk says where the family's bearable boundary is if the business or change does not go well. All three must be concrete and verifiable rather than staying at the level of attitude.

Step-by-step landing check for the three sentences

  1. First separate the three matters rather than discussing them together.
  2. Write income responsibility as a concrete sentence: who pays fixed costs, the ratio, and when to review.
  3. Write family time as a concrete sentence: fixed periods and how overtime or travel is communicated in advance.
  4. Write risk as a concrete sentence: the bearable limit and how to adjust once triggered.
  5. Combine the Spouse Palace stars to understand the rhythm, write the three sentences, and check them regularly rather than treating oral statements as arrangements.

Landing depends on execution and review

Writing the sentences is only the start, and real landing depends on daily execution and regular review, changing together when circumstances change. Clarifying the three things makes support concrete and the boundary clear.

Chart reading order

  1. Fix the chart and read the Spouse Palace stars and transformations.
  2. Separate the three matters first, then write one concrete sentence for each.
  3. Make responsibility, time, and risk individually verifiable.
  4. Execute and review regularly, adjusting in time.
  5. Keep the conclusion conditional rather than treating attitude as an arrangement.

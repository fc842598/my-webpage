What This Question Is Really Asking

The Underlying Logic

Four essentials: birth year/month/day (solar or lunar stated), exact time (down to the block or minute), birth city (for apparent-time/longitude correction) and gender (for decade direction); the more complete, the more reliable.

When only an approximate hour is known, narrow it first: map life descriptions such as dawn, lunchtime or dusk to local daylight times then, compressing candidates to two or three blocks and casting each.

Verification reverse-infers from events: cast all candidates and check year by year against what already happened—schooling, major illness/injury, financial loss, moves, marriage, job changes—fixing whichever chart's decade/year timing matches best.

If truly undeterminable, you may still read parts that do not depend on the exact Life position (e.g. transformations from the birth-year stem, some zodiac and year-branch stars) as limited reference, while clearly telling yourself it is rough; fine conclusions on Life, Body and specific palaces must wait until the chart is verified.

A Step-by-Step Way to Read It

  1. Collect the four essentials: date, exact time, birth city and gender
  2. Unify solar/lunar first, then correct apparent solar time and early/late Zi hour
  3. For a fuzzy hour, use life descriptions to compress candidates to two or three
  4. Cast all candidates and check year by year against schooling, illness/injury, loss, romance/marriage and moves
  5. Fix whichever chart matches timing best and keep that convention afterward
  6. If truly unfixable, reference only stem-based content with stated limits, never forcing Life/Body or specific-palace conclusions

Three Common Mistakes

Mistake 1: Picking any hour with uncertain time and reading on, possibly building on a wrong palace

Mistake 2: Ignoring birthplace and skipping apparent-time correction

Mistake 3: Not knowing past events can verify the chart, giving up or blindly trusting app defaults

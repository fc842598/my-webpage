Tian Xing, Tian Yue, Tian Shang, Tian Shi and Yin Sha: Subtle Auxiliary Malefics That Mean Chronic Drain, Not Major Disaster

Tian Xing is aloofness/legal matters, Tian Yue chronic minor ailments, Tian Shang/Tian Shi drain and worry, Yin Sha hidden mental knots. Far lighter than the six malefics, they mostly adjust the score and amplify only when sharing a palace—no need to fear their names.

These subtle stars are background noise; one alone rarely decides fate, but they tell you whether drain leans physical, mental or procedural, annotating the main line when read together.

What This Question Is Really Asking

People tense up at a list of small malefics. Ask who they share a palace with and whether they stack on the six malefics or Hua Ji; a floating small star only微调, stacking on the main line deserves attention.

The Underlying Logic

Tian Xing is principle, aloofness, needles/scalpels and legal/contract matters; in Life it makes one exacting, and only with Qing Yang/Tian Xiang does it stress disputes/procedures; it also links to medicine, law or metaphysics.

Tian Yue is chronic weak spots and low mood, not major illness; sharing Health-palace malefics suggests recurring minor issues managed by routine.

Tian Shang and Tian Shi always sit in fixed relative positions, meaning drain, care and hidden effort, mostly mental burden and worrying for others, not sudden disaster.

Yin Sha is inexpressible hidden knots, subconscious sensitivity, a psychological layer; all these stars are light, slow and hidden—annotating a major-star combination rather than deciding misfortune alone.

A Step-by-Step Way to Read It

  1. Mark these subtle stars and remember they rank below the six malefics
  2. For each, read the palace to classify drain as procedural (Tian Xing), physical (Tian Yue) or mental (Tian Shang/Tian Shi/Yin Sha)
  3. Focus on which major star, malefic or Hua Ji it shares; only stacking adds weight
  4. A floating or merely trining star slightly adjusts the score, never decides alone
  5. Turn health hints into routine/checkup reminders only—a tendency, not a diagnosis; rely on formal medical care
  6. Finally fold the hints into the main star/transformation line; do not let small stars dominate

Three Common Mistakes

Mistake 1: Treating subtle auxiliaries as equal in severity to the six malefics

Mistake 2: Making health/disaster claims from a small star detached from its major star

Mistake 3: Missing their value: pointing to the direction of chronic drain for light early management

Back to Your Own Chart

Subtle auxiliaries are footnotes, not protagonists. Layer them onto the main line and you will find major stars, transformations and the six malefics still decide direction; these stars only flag which chronic drain deserves early attention.

Reading Order

  1. Cast the natal chart first and locate the palaces and major stars tied to the group of subtle auxiliary malefics before judging anything
  2. Open the triple-direction of those palaces and count the auspicious, malefic and assistant stars separately
  3. Check where the natal Four Transformations land to set the chart's baseline tone
  4. Read the decade-limit transformations to see whether the the group of subtle auxiliary malefics line is activated in this stage
  5. Then read the annual transformations and the year's palace to pin down this year's trigger and timing
  6. Layer natal, decade and year before concluding, then decide how to act rather than labeling good or bad

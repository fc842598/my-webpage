Annual Triggers and the Opposite Palace

A plain-English guide to Annual Triggers and the Opposite Palace, with a practical reading order, simple examples, and clear boundaries for Zi Wei Dou Shu learners.

Zi Wei Dou Shu2026-07-01 08:43

Annual Triggers and the Opposite Palace is a practical Zi Wei Dou Shu topic. Start with the relevant palace, then read the opposite palace, supporting structure, and current timing.

What This Means

A minor annual year should not be read from one palace alone. Treat the triggered palace as the main stage, then read the opposite palace as the answering side: people, pressure, environment, or consequences that reflect back into the year.

How To Read It

Start with the palace named by the annual or minor-year trigger. Then check the opposite palace and the wider supporting structure before deciding whether the year is easy, blocked, or simply busy. A strong signal still needs a workable setting.

Simple Examples

  • If the main palace shows income but the opposite palace shows pressure, read the year as cash-flow management, not pure gain.
  • If career is triggered and the opposite palace points to people or clients, responsibility and negotiation must be read together.
  • If the main palace looks quiet but the opposite palace is strongly activated, events may arrive through outside pressure or other people.

Practical Order

First define the year question. Then identify the triggered palace, read its opposite palace, check the triad and the ten-year cycle, and finally compare the pattern with real events. This keeps annual readings practical instead of vague.

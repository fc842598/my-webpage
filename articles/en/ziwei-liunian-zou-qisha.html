A Qi Sha Year: Taking On Hard Problems Independently and Seeing Them Through

When Qi Sha hosts the annual Life Palace, the year leans to tough battles, pioneering and relying on oneself, often bringing situations that demand resolve and accountability, and also favoring changing tracks and opening new ground. Yet the annual cycle sets only one year’s rhythm; the key is choosing the right battles and keeping a fallback, rather than charging on impulse and harming health and relationships together.

Zi Wei Dou Shu2026-10-02

A Qi Sha year often feels like a year with few easy fights: things arrive hard and require one’s own call; pushing through opens real ground, while overreaching easily costs.

What This Question Is Really Asking

The question is how to use a Qi Sha year and guard against isolation. Understand the annual cycle as the year’s tone, then see whether Qi Sha’s general-star energy is aided in hard tasks by auspicious stars.

The Underlying Logic

A Qi Sha year brings strong drive and the courage for hard tasks, suiting pioneering, transitions, high-difficulty work and cutting ties.

Qi Sha also governs solitude: relying on oneself means less support and less room to maneuver, so health and safety need extra care.

With Lu, Quan, Ke and auspicious stars, daring becomes career and new ground that year; with malefics and Ji, risk, head-on clashes and impulsive decisions bring setbacks.

A Qi Sha year is annual practice in courage with a plan: choose battles worth fighting, assess risk and keep a fallback, then commit fully, and do not treat one year’s drive as the long-term norm.

A Step-by-Step Way to Read It

  1. First confirm Qi Sha hosts that year’s annual Life Palace
  2. List the hard battles and decisions the year brings
  3. Separate what deserves full commitment from what should be let go
  4. Before acting, weigh risk, cost and fallback rather than charging on impulse
  5. Protect fixed time for health, safety and rest
  6. Review at year end to see whether the year’s pioneering left a sustainable situation

Three Common Mistakes

Mistake 1: Taking a Qi Sha year as a year to push and pioneer while ignoring risk and fallback

Mistake 2: Meeting every hard fight head-on and mistaking brute force for daring

Mistake 3: Focusing only on the hard tasks while neglecting health, safety and relationships

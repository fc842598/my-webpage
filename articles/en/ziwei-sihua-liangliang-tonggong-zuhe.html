Reading Two Transformations in One Palace: Lu-Quan, Lu-Ke, Quan-Ke, Quan-Ji and Ke-Ji Explained

When two transformations share a palace, a single image becomes a paired one: Lu-Quan is wealth plus drive, Lu-Ke gains through reputation, Quan-Ke is professional standing, Quan-Ji is fast in and fast out, Ke-Ji is slow repetition. Each pair reads differently.

Zi Wei Dou Shu2026-09-08

A single image does not make an event—one transformation is only a tendency. Two in a palace or opposition make the matter take shape, and the pair, not the sum of labels, is what you read.

What This Question Is Really Asking

People vaguely call two transformations "great" or "complicated." Ask which two, on whose star, sharing or opposing—Lu-Quan and Quan-Ji feel almost opposite in real life.

The Underlying Logic

Lu-Quan: Lu supplies opportunity/resources and Quan execution/control—a wealth-and-status, business type that innovates, benefit over fame, risking forceful expansion without a plan.

Lu-Ke: Lu gives tangible gain, Ke reputation and mentors; profit comes through expertise or word of mouth, fame over benefit, a steady "live on your brand" pair that also aids exams and credentials.

Quan-Ke: Quan acts, Ke eases and regulates; firm yet flexible, standing on a craft with both title and competence, avoiding the rigidity of Quan alone.

Quan-Ji and Ke-Ji: Quan-Ji is fire burning water—hard force against a block, fast in/out, toilsome and uncertain; Ke-Ji is wood draining water—protecting image while blocked, slow and repetitive, hard to close; both need the hosting star to set severity.

A Step-by-Step Way to Read It

  1. Confirm which two transformations share the palace (or oppose) and name the pair
  2. Distinguish sharing (forces fused) from opposition (two faces, inner-outer tension)
  3. Set the broad tone by pair: Lu-Quan pushes, Lu-Ke names, Quan-Ke professionalizes
  4. Then identify the transformed major star and palace, translating the abstract pair into concrete people/matters
  5. Count sharing malefics/assistants: auspicious pairs with malefics have setbacks; tangled pairs with Ke/Lu get buffers
  6. Finally layer decade/year to see when the paired image activates and delivers

Three Common Mistakes

Mistake 1: Mechanically adding two meanings instead of reading the pair's chemistry

Mistake 2: Reading shared palaces but not opposites, missing the inner/outer tension of a paired image

Mistake 3: Ignoring the hosting star—Quan-Ji on Wu Qu versus on Tian Tong are two experiences

Back to Your Own Chart

Paired transformations are where Zi Wei Dou Shu shows its precision. On your chart, isolate every palace with two transformations and read it in this paired language; much of the "seems good yet stuck" ambiguity gains a clear name and handling.

Reading Order

  1. Cast the natal chart first and locate the palaces and major stars tied to two transformations sharing one palace before judging anything
  2. Open the triple-direction of those palaces and count the auspicious, malefic and assistant stars separately
  3. Check where the natal Four Transformations land to set the chart's baseline tone
  4. Read the decade-limit transformations to see whether the two transformations sharing one palace line is activated in this stage
  5. Then read the annual transformations and the year's palace to pin down this year's trigger and timing
  6. Layer natal, decade and year before concluding, then decide how to act rather than labeling good or bad

Parents and Career Palaces: Clarify Vague Manager Feedback in Zi Wei Dou Shu

Read authority dynamics and job duties separately, then turn unclear feedback into criteria, examples, approvers, and deadlines.

Zi Wei Dou Shu2026-11-04 21:15

“Please improve this” can lead to ten revisions when nobody has defined what counts as finished. In Zi Wei Dou Shu, the Parents Palace can frame how someone reacts to authority, while Career covers the actual work. Reading them together should lead to better questions, not a guess about a manager's mood.

Translate feedback into criteria

Ask which parts need changing, who signs off, and when the next version is due. “Improve” might mean missing data, a budget issue, a customer objection, or simply unclear presentation. A chart may suggest where communication becomes tense, but it cannot supply an approval rule that has not been agreed.

Two different examples

A designer hearing “make it more premium” should request references, audience goals, and required brand elements. Offer two clear directions with version notes. An operations employee told that a monthly report is wrong should check a sample transaction, the reporting period, and the treatment of refunds. The first case needs a creative standard; the second needs a data definition.

Record the decision

Hua Ke may raise a question about how work is documented; Hua Quan about responsibility and approval; Hua Ji about a recurring bottleneck. Always locate the transformation before using it. In practice, restate the feedback, agree on a sample, name the approver, and save the deadline. Use that record when the next review is still vague.

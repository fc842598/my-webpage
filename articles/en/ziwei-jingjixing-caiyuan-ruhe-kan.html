Zi Wei Dou Shu on Economic Layoffs: When Career and Travel Activate Together

Economic layoffs are a department or company-wide reduction, not personal, and often appear when the decade and annual Career and Travel Palaces are activated together. The key is recognizing the collective nature of the cut and arranging severance and re-employment.

Zi Wei Dou Shu2026-10-29

Economic layoffs are the tide going out, not a personal failure; verify compensation against the standard plan and look for the next role together.

What This Question Is Really Asking

Some still feel they performed poorly in a collective cut, hesitate to discuss severance, and overlook whether the layoff plan is lawful and complete.

The Underlying Logic

Economic layoffs often occur when the decade and annual Career Palaces are activated together with the Travel Palace, marking an overall contraction rather than a personal outcome.

Such cuts usually follow a fixed plan, paying severance by years of service and, where applicable, pay in lieu of notice and unused annual leave.

When the Career Palace main star is fallen and meets Hua Ji, it often accompanies an industry-wide decline, and re-employment may require a change of direction rather than the same role.

With the Travel Palace activated well, a collective layoff can offer a clean break and a chance to change tracks; if blocked by malefics, extend the buffer.

A Step-by-Step Way to Read It

  1. Confirm whether this is a collective economic-layoff plan rather than individual dismissal
  2. Check whether the plan was lawfully explained and severance is paid in full by years of service
  3. Calculate severance, pay in lieu of notice and unused annual leave together
  4. Read the decade and annual Career and Travel Palaces to judge an industry decline or a chance to change tracks
  5. Update your resume and contact peers to arrange re-employment with the collective timeline
  6. Reserve enough cash flow and avoid accepting overly poor terms at a low point

Three Common Mistakes

Mistake 1: Taking a collective cut as personal failure and hesitating to discuss severance

Mistake 2: Seeing only compensation without checking the plan legality and accepting a discounted deal

Mistake 3: Sticking only to the old role amid an industry decline and lengthening re-employment

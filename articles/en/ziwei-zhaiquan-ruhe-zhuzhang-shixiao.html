Zi Wei Dou Shu on Debt Claims and Limitation: Keep the Claim Alive on the Record

How to recover money owed often maps to the Wealth Palace for the claim and the Siblings or Friends Palace for the debtor. Ordinary claims have a limitation period; a demand, a lawsuit or the debtor’s promise to pay restarts it, and the key is not letting the claim sit indefinitely.

Zi Wei Dou Shu2026-10-30

The key is not whether the other side will repay, but making documented demands on a regular basis so the limitation period stays alive.

What This Question Is Really Asking

Some hold an IOU for years without demanding payment, and when they finally sue the debtor argues the period has passed, leaving them without any demand evidence and at a disadvantage.

The Underlying Logic

A debt is usually read in the Wealth Palace, with the debtor in the Siblings or Friends Palace, marking money already due and waiting to be recovered.

The ordinary limitation period is generally three years from when the right and the obligor are known or should have been known.

The period is interrupted by a demand, a lawsuit, the debtor’s promise or partial performance, after which it restarts; the longest protection period has a separate cap.

When the Wealth Palace is steady and the IOU and demand records are complete, the claim is easier to pursue; with Hua Ji and malefics, make written demands and keep proof of delivery rather than relying on words.

A Step-by-Step Way to Read It

  1. Check the IOU, contract and transfer records to confirm the amount and repayment time
  2. Confirm the debtor’s identity and asset clues to assess ability to pay
  3. Make written demands within the limitation period and keep proof of delivery and replies
  4. Read the Wealth Palace and the Siblings or Friends Palace to judge how hard recovery will be
  5. When the debtor promises or pays in part, record it promptly to interrupt the period
  6. If negotiation fails, sue in time, with written evidence and the law as the basis

Three Common Mistakes

Mistake 1: Not demanding payment for years and missing the limitation period

Mistake 2: Making oral demands without records, unable to prove interruption

Mistake 3: Not checking the IOU and transfer proof, leaving the amount unclear

When Tai Sui Presides Over Your Year: Authority in Hand, but Clashes Cost Most

A Tai Sui year brings initiative, opportunities and changes, but it favors steadiness and punishes head-on clashes; pushing too hard can turn an opening into a deadlock, so advance steadily.

Zi Wei Dou Shu2026-10-04

Tai Sui is not a malefic so much as the year presiding over you; your voice carries more weight, but when you force a confrontation, the pushback is strongest too.

What This Question Is Really Asking

People approach a Tai Sui year with fear, but the real question is not whether misfortune strikes; it is whether, given more initiative this year, you end up using it too aggressively.

The Underlying Logic

Tai Sui presides over authority and change; this year events tend to move because of you, and you are more likely to be pushed to the front.

It favors a settled position and dislikes clashes; in a year that clashes with it, big matters repeat themselves, and force only hardens them.

Tai Sui is an amplifier: strong structures grow stronger and weak spots are exposed, but it creates no fortune or misfortune on its own.

An annual Tai Sui sets only one year rhythm; the long-term course still belongs to the natal chart and decade limit, not a second fate.

A Step-by-Step Way to Read It

  1. Locate which natal palace the annual Tai Sui sits in to define the year main stage
  2. Read that palace triple-direction to see whether strengths or weak spots are amplified
  3. Check whether anything clashes with Tai Sui this year; if so, slow major decisions down
  4. Take initiative, but leave margin in important decisions and avoid absolute statements
  5. When stalled, step back and use time or a third party rather than forcing a head-on win
  6. At year-end, separate healthy initiative from stubborn force and recalibrate

Three Common Mistakes

Mistake 1: Treating a Tai Sui year as a disaster year and limiting yourself everywhere

Mistake 2: Assuming more authority means you can force things, ignoring the rebound when it clashes

Mistake 3: Reading only the annual Tai Sui and drawing long-term conclusions without the natal chart and decade

Back to Your Own Chart

A Tai Sui year teaches power without force. Direct your initiative toward what truly matters and be willing to yield at a clash, and the year steadies itself.

Reading Order

  1. Cast the natal chart first and locate the palaces and major stars tied to the year when Tai Sui presides before judging anything
  2. Open the triple-direction of those palaces and count the auspicious, malefic and assistant stars separately
  3. Check where the natal Four Transformations land to set the chart's baseline tone
  4. Read the decade-limit transformations to see whether the the year when Tai Sui presides line is activated in this stage
  5. Then read the annual transformations and the year's palace to pin down this year's trigger and timing
  6. Layer natal, decade and year before concluding, then decide how to act rather than labeling good or bad

How to Respond to a Drug Allergy: Stop the Drug, Assess Severity and Get Care

If a rash, itching or other suspected allergy appears after a drug, stop the suspected medication and note its name. If there is throat tightness, wheezing, swelling of the face, lips or tongue, widespread hives or dizziness, call emergency services immediately as it may be life-threatening anaphylaxis. Even mild reactions should be assessed promptly, the allergy history should be reported in future, and doctors and pharmacists should guide medication.

Can You Read Charts for Family and Others: Yes for Practice, but Keep Boundaries and Do Not Make Major Decisions for Them

After learning Zi Wei, many want to read charts for family and friends, which is itself a good way to practice and show care, but several boundaries must be kept: first, do not make major decisions for others, such as quitting, separating, investing or treating illness, only offer angles and information; second, do not create anxiety or turn possibility into certainty; third, do not force readings when the other does not want to hear, respecting their receptive rhythm; fourth, for health, finance and legal matters, always recommend professionals. Chart reading is a tool for self-observation and reference, not a substitute for decisions, and when reading for others, saying this is reference up front matters even more.

The easiest pitfall when reading for others is sliding from offering reference into making decisions for them, even carrying their life choices on your own shoulders. Keeping boundaries is good for both sides.

What This Question Is Really Asking

The question is about the boundaries and cautions when reading for others. First understand that chart reading is a reference tool, not a decision tool, then clarify what can be said and what cannot be decided for others.

The Underlying Logic

Reading for others is good practice: others’ charts help you verify methods and build cases, but say up front that this is reference, not a verdict.

Boundary one: do not make major decisions for others. For quitting, separating, investing, moving and treating illness, only offer angles and risk warnings from the chart; the final decision is theirs.

Boundary two: do not create anxiety. Do not turn possible into certain, do not make malefics and Ji sound terrifying, do not label the other negatively.

Boundary three: respect the other’s wishes. When they do not want to hear or accept, do not force or persist; for health, finance and legal matters, always recommend professionals such as doctors, licensed financial advisors and lawyers.

A Step-by-Step Way to Read It

  1. Before reading for someone, state that this is a reference angle, not a verdict, and you will not make decisions for them
  2. First ask what specific question they want to look at, not reading the whole chart vaguely or voluntarily digging into areas they do not want seen
  3. Only offer information, angles and risk warnings from the chart, not conclusions for them
  4. For major decisions, quitting, separating, investing and treating illness, clearly say this decision is theirs and you can help sort angles
  5. For health, finance and legal issues, recommend the corresponding professionals
  6. After the reading, do not chase them for feedback or carry the outcome of their choices on yourself

Three Common Mistakes

Mistake 1: Treating chart reading as making decisions for others, turning reference into decision-making

Mistake 2: Turning possibility into certainty, creating anxiety and fear

Mistake 3: Forcing readings when the other does not want to hear, disrespecting boundaries and turning care into pressure

Back to Your Own Chart

Reading charts for family and friends is good, but keeping boundaries is true help: offer angles, do not decide for them, do not create anxiety, and refer major issues to professionals. Treating chart reading as reference keeps both you and them at ease.

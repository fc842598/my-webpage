If Ke, Quan, and Lu Are Split Apart, Fix Entry or Reception First?

A chart can show good signals in several places and still fail as a whole. Locate whether the break is before entry or after opportunity arrives.

Zi Wei Dou Shu2026-09-04 19:18

Split Ke, Quan, and Lu often create the illusion that everything is progressing because each part of the system looks bright in isolation.

The real problem is that brightness does not guarantee continuity. Opportunity, authority, and monetization can light up in different places and still fail to form one working chain.

Distribution is not weakness by itself

Hua Ke can describe examinations, writing, artistic ability, professional standing, or public recognition. Hua Quan adds command, firmness, and accountable authority. Hua Lu adds access to money or resources and may also favor preservation. Combining the labels into a general promise of success erases the decision each one helps explain.

The palace must match the question. For promotion, Quan in Career is more direct than Quan in a relationship palace. For professional fees, Ke must connect with Wealth and an actual paying market. For personal visibility, Life and Travel may matter more than an internal title.

Check whether recognition actually becomes responsibility

When Ke, Quan, and Lu occupy the same palace, recognition, control, and resources gather around that palace's topic. This can produce a clear professional identity, but it also concentrates dependency. If the institution, market, or role cannot support the pattern, all three functions may remain underused together.

Example 1: Hua Ke, Hua Quan, and Hua Lu together in the Career Palace can concentrate expertise, authority, and organizational resources in one role. Lu here may appear as a project budget, spending authority, or responsibility for company money; it does not prove that the money belongs to the employee. Check the job description, signing authority, and budget ownership first, then verify personal pay through the Wealth Palace and the actual compensation package.

Then test whether responsibility turns into resources and income

Life, Career, Wealth, and Travel are read as a connected structure. Transformations distributed among them can divide the process into stages: the person becomes known for a skill, an organization gives that person responsibility, the work creates revenue, and external channels, institutions, or professional networks bring customers and broader market access.

Example 2: Hua Ke in Life, Hua Quan in Career, and Hua Lu in Wealth can form a sequence in which reputation creates responsibility and responsibility produces income, provided each real-world link exists. Strong recognition without a pricing system may stop before reaching Lu; authority without resources may stop at accountability.

Do not confuse a beautiful layout with a complete result chain

A concentrated pattern is not inherently stronger than a distributed one. For a same-palace pattern, ask whether that domain offers enough room for expertise, authority, and resources to operate. For a distributed pattern, ask whether one result can genuinely pass into the next palace's domain.

Create three evidence columns. Record where recognition comes from, what decisions the person can actually make, and what form the resource takes. Credentials, staff authority, signed sales, and cash received are different facts. The chart becomes useful when those facts reveal which link is active and which still needs construction.

Practical Reading Order

Map the chain as entry, role, and monetization first. Then place Ke, Quan, and Lu on that chain and decide whether the urgent repair belongs to access or to reception.

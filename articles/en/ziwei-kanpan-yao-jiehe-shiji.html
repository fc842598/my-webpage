Ground Readings in Reality: What the Chart Shows Isn't Always What Happens

Chart shows "wealth luck this year" — in reality it may just be a small raise. "Relationship turbulence" may just be an argument. Readings must be grounded in the client's actual life; don't mistake symbols for facts.

Methods2026-08-20 15:30

Beginners' most common error is "literal translation" — Lu means "get rich," Ji means "disaster," Hong Luan means "marriage." But between chart symbols and real events there's a "translation" process: Lu may be a raise or a free meal; Ji may be job loss or just a very busy period. Translation accuracy depends on knowing the person's actual life.

How to ground readings: first, ask about their situation — job, relationship, recent events; second, "dimension-reduce" symbols to their life level — same Lu in Wealth means a "big deal" for a boss, a "raise" for an employee, "more allowance" for a student; third, verify with the past — check past events first, then predict; fourth, give concrete advice rather than scary prophecies.

Why Ground in Reality

Chart symbols are "probabilities," not "facts" — Lu in Wealth means "affinity with money," not "win the lottery."

Same symbol, different events for different people — "wealth luck" differs entirely for boss vs employee.

Regional and cultural differences — relationship turbulence manifests differently across cultures.

Era differences — ancient "passing the imperial exam" corresponds to modern "passing exam/promotion."

Predictions without grounding are either too vague ("you have changes this year") or absurd ("you'll be emperor next year").

How to Ground

Ask about现状 — occupation, age, relationship, top concern.

Translate into their language — for a programmer "project opportunity"; for business owner "customers"; for student "exam luck."

Verify with past — predict 2-3 past events first; if correct, continue.

Distinguish "signal" from "event" — Ji is a signal; what specific event depends on their life.

Give concrete advice — not "be careful" but "read contract terms carefully this year."

Real-World Translation of Symbols

Lu in Wealth — employee: raise or bonus; business owner: more orders; investor: gains but not necessarily big.

Ji in Career — employee: work pressure or wanting to quit; business owner: blocked deals; freelancer: late payments.

Hong Luan/Tian Xi — young: romance; married: pregnancy or family joy; elderly: late companion.

Travel palace active — employee: business trip or transfer; business owner: expanding to other markets; young: travel or moving.

Qing Yang in Health — watch for injuries, surgery, inflammation; not necessarily serious illness.

Reading Order

When reading:

  1. Know the person — job, age, situation.
  2. Then read the chart — symbols and trends.
  3. Translate into plain language — what might actually happen in their life.
  4. Verify with past — predict past first, then future.
  5. Give specific advice — actionable, verifiable.
  6. The chart is a map, not the terrain; a river on the map may be just a stream on the ground.

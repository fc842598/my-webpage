Yang and Tuo Luo Flanking a Palace: Separating Clash from Delay

How to distinguish the direct clash of Yang from the dragging delay of Tuo Luo when they flank a palace.

Zi Wei Dou Shu2026-11-13 11:04

When Yang and Tuo Luo sit on the two sides of a palace, they keep pressure on the palace between them. Yang brings open, direct clash, while Tuo Luo brings delay and entanglement. Separating the two helps target the right response.

Different effects

Yang is like a visible blade: quick, direct, and sometimes decisive. Tuo Luo is like turning in place: repeated, slow, and hard to finish. One makes a matter confrontational, while the other makes it drag.

Judgment steps

  1. Confirm the positions of Yang and Tuo Luo and whether they truly flank the target palace.
  2. Mark Yang's direct clash separately from Tuo Luo's delay.
  3. Read the host star and transformations of the palace between them to judge resilience.
  4. Separate real events that confront from those that keep circling.
  5. Handle the direct decision first, then unpack the delayed step.

Reading order

  1. Fix the inputs and software version, then confirm the flanking position.
  2. Record the two stars separately rather than using one difficult-star label.
  3. Check the host star, transformations, and supporting palaces for resilience.
  4. Add the current cycle to see whether clash or delay is being activated.
  5. Write the answer as open clash plus hidden drag, not one negative label.

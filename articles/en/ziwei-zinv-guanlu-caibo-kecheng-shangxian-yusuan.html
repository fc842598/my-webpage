Zi Wei Dou Shu Children Palace: Budget a Course Before Launch

Separate creative output, operating hours, and cash needs before selling an online course.

Zi Wei Dou Shu2026-11-21 09:04

Recording three lessons is a start, not a complete course business. In Zi Wei Dou Shu, the Children Palace can frame a project that grows from an idea. The Career Palace asks who will run it, while the Wealth Palace points to money that must actually be collected and reserved. Read the three questions separately before opening sales.

Separate the time layers

Record whether the reading concerns the natal chart, a ten-year period, or a current annual trigger. Then examine the stars and transformations in the Children Palace and its connected palaces. A favorable marker does not prove demand. If two people create the course, write down ownership, maintenance, and revenue terms outside the chart.

Run a small lesson first

Offer a short trial in which students must finish one real task. For a spreadsheet course, count how many complete the exercise, where they stop, and how long support takes. If most people fail at step one, revise the lesson before recording twenty more. Student work provides better evidence than a general compliment.

Count hours and cash separately

Use the Career Palace question to list weekly updates, corrections, and support ownership. Use the Wealth Palace question to list platform fees, production tools, payment charges, and a refund reserve. One creator may have beautiful materials but no time to answer students. Another may sell fewer seats yet set a workable service schedule. Compare actual receipts with promised service before calling either model profitable. Read the chart, then set a launch threshold based on trial results, available hours, and cash.

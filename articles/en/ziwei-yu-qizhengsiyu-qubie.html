Zi Wei Dou Shu vs Qi Zheng Si Yu: Two Chinese Systems Reading Different Things

Qi Zheng Si Yu, also tied to the Guo Lao star tradition, reads the sun, moon and visible planets against the zodiac and lunar lodges, leaning toward cosmic correspondence and the shape of a whole life; Zi Wei Dou Shu places symbolic stars in twelve human-affairs palaces with the four transformations, leaning toward concrete affairs and timing. Both use birth time but differ in coordinates, stars and focus.

People ask whether Zi Wei Dou Shu and Qi Zheng Si Yu are the same and can be read together. Both are traditional Chinese systems that cast star charts, but one faces the visible heavens and the other human-affairs palaces—two independent systems.

What This Question Is Really Asking

The question is where the two differ and how to choose. Look at three points: whether the stars are visible bodies or symbolic stars, how the coordinates divide, and whether the focus is a life’s overall climate or concrete affairs.

The Underlying Logic

The stars differ: Qi Zheng Si Yu uses the sun, moon, the five visible planets and added symbolic nodes, while Zi Wei uses stars such as Zi Wei and Tian Fu whose names are symbolic rather than tied to a planet.

The coordinates differ: Qi Zheng Si Yu locates by the zodiac and the twenty-eight lunar lodges in degrees, while Zi Wei locates by twelve human-affairs palaces such as Life, Wealth, Spouse and Career, where the name is the matter.

The focus differs: Qi Zheng Si Yu leans to the overall climate and endowment of a life and cosmic correspondence, while Zi Wei leans to the shape and timing of wealth, career, marriage and family.

They can inform each other but not be forced: compare the life-tone from Qi Zheng Si Yu with the concrete palaces of Zi Wei without demanding the same answer.

A Step-by-Step Way to Read It

  1. First decide whether you want the overall climate of a life or concrete affairs and timing
  2. Questions of life pattern and cosmic correspondence lead with Qi Zheng Si Yu’s stars and degrees
  3. Concrete domains—wealth, career, marriage, family—lead with the Zi Wei twelve palaces
  4. When comparing, only contrast broad directions such as firmness and softness, movement and stillness, without forcing star names
  5. When the two clash, return to reality and see which fits you better
  6. Treat them as complementary tools for self-knowledge rather than competing for accuracy

Three Common Mistakes

Mistake 1: Matching the two charts by star name while ignoring that the coordinates and stars differ at the root

Mistake 2: Expecting identical answers because both use birth time

Mistake 3: Trusting one and dismissing the other without seeing the questions each handles well

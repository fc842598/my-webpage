Self-Authority in Zi Wei Dou Shu: From Control Desire to Leadership

Self-Authority occurs when a palace stem flies Authority, meaning that palace actively seeks dominance and wants to control the situation. Unlike natal Authority, which is "power given to you by others" or "authority built into the chart," Self-Authority is "you actively taking power." It gives strong drive and opinions, but used in the wrong place it becomes domineering, stubborn, and power-struggling.

Zi Wei Dou Shu2026-09-24

The core characteristic of Self-Authority is "active" and "in control." In palaces with Self-Authority, you unconsciously want to take charge and do things your way. If this is a domain you can legitimately lead (work, profession, your own affairs), Self-Authority is leadership. If it is a domain you should not control (others affairs, the other person in a relationship), it becomes control desire. Palace-specific manifestations: Life Palace Self-Authority—strong personality, opinionated, refuses to lose, but can be deaf to advice. Suitable for decision-making roles but should practice listening. Wealth Palace Self-Authority—desire to control money, likes managing finances and making investment decisions independently, but can be dictatorial and ignore risk. Career Palace Self-Authority—actively seeks leadership at work, suitable for team leadership or entrepreneurship, but easily struggles for power with superiors or peers. Spouse Palace Self-Authority—wants to take charge in relationships, control the pace, easily making the partner feel suppressed. Children Palace Self-Authority—strong opinions on child education, easily over-interfering. Parents Palace Self-Authority—wants a voice in relations with elders or superiors, easily talks back. The difference between Self-Authority and Self-Prosperity: Self-Prosperity is "giving out," Self-Authority is "taking hold." One leans toward sharing, the other toward control. If a chart has much Self-Authority, the person is tired from trying to grasp everything; if much Self-Prosperity, the person is scattered from trying to give everything. Balance is best.

What This Question Is Really Asking

A typical case: Career Palace Self-Authority, with Life Palace Self-Prosperity. These people are very proactive at work and want to lead projects (Self-Authority in Career), while being generous and sociable (Self-Prosperity in Life). Together, they make good managers—can make decisions and unite people. But if Career Self-Authority has Jealousy in the opposite Spouse Palace, they easily bring workplace dominance home and try to control everything in the relationship, causing partner dissatisfaction. The breakthrough is "scene switching": fully use Self-Authority for decisions at work, but actively let go of control in relationships, using Self-Prosperity sharing and tolerance to interact. Another case: Wealth Palace Self-Authority, with Prosperity in Property Palace. These people have strong investment opinions and like managing things themselves, but Property Prosperity indicates good fixed-asset luck. The suitable approach: use Self-Authority for "research and decisions," but allocate some assets to Property (real estate, long-term savings)—domains that do not require daily trading—satisfying the control desire while reducing frequent-trading risk.

The Underlying Logic

Dismissing Self-Authority as simply "bad temper, domineering," ignoring that it is the ability to actively take charge.

Not checking which palace Self-Authority falls in, assuming the person wants to control every domain.

Trying to suppress Self-Authority, resulting in loss of opinions and drive.

Using Self-Authority in domains that should not be controlled (like others lives), causing relationship tension.

A Step-by-Step Way to Read It

  1. Locate palaces with Self-Authority and confirm whether it returns to self or flies to another palace.
  2. Assess whether that palace is a domain you can legitimately lead (work/profession vs. others/relationships).
  3. Fully express Self-Authority in legitimate domains: making decisions, setting direction, leading teams.
  4. In domains that should not be controlled, deliberately practice "letting go of control," converting Self-Authority into "suggesting rather than ordering."
  5. If there is much Self-Authority, consciously delegate and divide work to avoid grasping everything.
  6. During Decade or Annual periods activating Self-Authority, be careful not to "fight for power due to momentary impulse."

Three Common Mistakes

Mistake 1: Self-Authority is not a flaw; it is the ability to actively take charge and lead, which when used correctly is leadership.

Mistake 2: People with Self-Authority are not "unwilling to listen"; they need to be respected in their expertise before opening up.

Mistake 3: When Self-Authority meets Jealousy, it is not "power blocked" but "learning to use power more wisely through setbacks."

Back to Your Own Chart

People with Self-Authority naturally have the courage to say "I will take charge," a scarce quality in situations requiring responsibility. But the wisdom of power lies not in grasping everything, but in knowing what to hold and what to release. Understanding where your Self-Authority lies lets you cultivate "control desire" into "leadership"—decisive when you should take charge, calm when you should let go.

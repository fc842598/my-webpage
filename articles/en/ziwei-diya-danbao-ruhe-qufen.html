Zi Wei Dou Shu on Mortgages and Guarantees: Property for Assets, Wealth for Debt

Borrowing money or backing someone often corresponds to the Property and Wealth Palaces being activated. A mortgage does not transfer possession and usually needs registration, while a guarantee is a personal duty split into ordinary and joint liability; before signing, tell whether you are giving property security or a personal guarantee.

Zi Wei Dou Shu2026-10-30

The key is first seeing whether you secure with property or in person and how far the duty reaches, rather than signing before understanding the consequences.

What This Question Is Really Asking

Some back others out of goodwill without telling ordinary from joint guarantee, only to find they must pay first when the debtor defaults, or even have their property enforced.

The Underlying Logic

Mortgages and guarantees often appear when the Property and Wealth Palaces are activated, marking responsibility for a debt with your own assets or credit.

A mortgage is property security without transferring possession, and a real-estate mortgage generally takes effect on registration; a pledge transfers possession.

A guarantee is personal security: an ordinary guarantee has the right to demand action against the debtor first, while a joint guarantee lets the creditor demand repayment directly.

When the Property and Wealth stars are steady and the contract is clear, the duty has clear boundaries; with Hua Ji and malefics, verify the type, scope and term so limited liability is not signed away as unlimited joint liability.

A Step-by-Step Way to Read It

  1. First confirm whether you provide a mortgage, pledge or guarantee
  2. For real estate, check whether the mortgage is registered
  3. For a guarantee, tell ordinary from joint and verify the scope and term
  4. Read the Property and Wealth Palaces to judge asset and debt risk
  5. Ask to see the main contract and assess the debtor’s ability to repay
  6. Keep the security contract and communication records, with professional legal advice as the basis

Three Common Mistakes

Mistake 1: Backing others out of goodwill without ordinary versus joint guarantees

Mistake 2: Not checking the scope and term, letting the duty expand

Mistake 3: Skipping registration or not reading the main contract, leaving the risk unknown

Yearly Transformations: Fix the Landing Palace Before Discussing Opportunity and Friction

Lu, Quan, Ke and Ji translate into resources, drive, credibility and ongoing friction. Test whether Ke has real substance, and treat Ji like a long-term cost to manage through boundaries and review rather than fear.

Zi Wei Dou Shu2026-09-25

The most useful way to read a yearly plan is treating the transformations as signals: where resources come, what pushes you, where you become visible, and where you keep paying cost. Read the signals and your actions gain precision.

What This Question Is Really Asking

The common bias is chasing only Lu for opportunity and avoiding Ji. But opportunity and friction are one fabric: Lu also brings spending and Ji helps you cut losses; the landing palace sets the topic and the star sets the expression.

The Underlying Logic

The landing palace sets the topic, while the star itself sets the expression.

Lu gives resources but also corresponding spending; Quan gives drive but also competition.

Ke gives visibility but cannot replace ability and evidence; without real skill it is an empty name.

Ji is ongoing friction and cost, to be handled through boundaries, review and restraint rather than fear.

A Step-by-Step Way to Read It

  1. First fix the landing palaces of the four transformed stars in the natal chart
  2. For Lu, list where resources come from and what spending accompanies them
  3. For Quan, see what is pushed forward and what competition you take on
  4. For Ke, test whether works and evidence support the visibility
  5. For Ji, translate the ongoing cost into boundaries and a review checklist
  6. Set one verifiable indicator for each transformation, then arrange the year's actions

Three Common Mistakes

Mistake 1: Chasing only Lu's opportunity and hiding from Ji

Mistake 2: Taking Ke's empty name as strength without checking real ability

Mistake 3: Treating Ji as fear rather than managing boundaries, letting costs run out of control

Back to Your Own Chart

Yearly transformations are not fortune-telling verdicts but an action map. Grasp opportunities, manage costs, and tie each item to a verifiable indicator so the year stays steady.

Reading Order

  1. Cast the natal chart first and locate the palaces and major stars tied to the opportunities and friction of yearly transformations before judging anything
  2. Open the triple-direction of those palaces and count the auspicious, malefic and assistant stars separately
  3. Check where the natal Four Transformations land to set the chart's baseline tone
  4. Read the decade-limit transformations to see whether the the opportunities and friction of yearly transformations line is activated in this stage
  5. Then read the annual transformations and the year's palace to pin down this year's trigger and timing
  6. Layer natal, decade and year before concluding, then decide how to act rather than labeling good or bad

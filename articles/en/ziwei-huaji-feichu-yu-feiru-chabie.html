Hua Ji Flying Out vs. Flying In in Zi Wei Dou Shu: Direction Sets Who Is Attached

When a palace stem makes a star Hua Ji and that star sits elsewhere, it flies out, meaning this palace cannot let go of that one; when another palace flies Ji into this one, it flies in, meaning the outside palace attaches here. The directions reverse, and so do subject and object; first see who flies to whom.

Zi Wei Dou Shu2026-09-29

Beginners only see where Ji sits without asking who flew it, reversing subject and object and skewing the conclusion.

What This Question Is Really Asking

The question is why the same Ji reads differently by direction. Find the takeoff palace stem; whichever palace it flies from is the one that is attached, invested and working the lesson.

The Underlying Logic

Flying out: the home stem makes a star elsewhere Hua Ji, meaning this palace invests energy and attachment there, often giving and draining toward it.

Flying in: another stem makes a star in the home Hua Ji, meaning an outside palace attaches to this one through a person or matter.

Direction sets subject and object: the takeoff palace is active and the landing palace the target.

Hua Ji means attachment and inability to let go; the flight marks its two ends, reading a relationship rather than foretelling disaster.

A Step-by-Step Way to Read It

  1. First find which palace the Hua Ji star sits in
  2. Then check which palace stem makes it Hua Ji, the takeoff
  3. The takeoff is the active, attached party and the landing is the target
  4. For flying out, see where this palace gives and cannot let go
  5. For flying in, see who attaches to this palace through a person or matter
  6. Read it as relational attachment, not direct fortune

Three Common Mistakes

Mistake 1: Only seeing where Ji lands without asking the takeoff palace

Mistake 2: Reversing flying out and in and swapping subject and object

Mistake 3: Judging a flying Ji as disaster and ignoring it describes attachment and relationship

Back to Your Own Chart

For a flying Ji, read direction first: whichever stem takes off is the attached party. Flying out is me reaching out and flying in is another reaching to me; with subject and object clear, the relationship reads correctly.

Reading Order

  1. Cast the natal chart first and locate the palaces and major stars tied to the directional difference of Hua Ji flying out versus flying in before judging anything
  2. Open the triple-direction of those palaces and count the auspicious, malefic and assistant stars separately
  3. Check where the natal Four Transformations land to set the chart's baseline tone
  4. Read the decade-limit transformations to see whether the the directional difference of Hua Ji flying out versus flying in line is activated in this stage
  5. Then read the annual transformations and the year's palace to pin down this year's trigger and timing
  6. Layer natal, decade and year before concluding, then decide how to act rather than labeling good or bad

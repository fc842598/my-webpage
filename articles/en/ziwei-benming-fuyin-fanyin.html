Fu-Yin and Fan-Yin in Zi Wei Dou Shu: What Happens When the Year Repeats or Clashes the Natal Palace

When a year (or decade) reaches a palace with the same stem-branch as a natal palace it is Fu-Yin—repetition, stagnation, old matters returning; reaching the branch-clashed opposite line is Fan-Yin—turbulence, reversal and external impact. They are not omens but two different tempo signals.

Zi Wei Dou Shu2026-09-09

Fu-Yin is retaking the same exam on the same spot; Fan-Yin is being jolted from across. One is inward and repetitive, the other outward and changing; tell them apart and such years stop looking uniformly bad.

What This Question Is Really Asking

Asking why a year keeps repeating old matters or is suddenly disrupted by outside change means checking whether it reaches a natal Fu-Yin or Fan-Yin position, then using that palace's stars and transformations to locate the concrete matter.

The Underlying Logic

Fu-Yin is stem-branch overlap: the year (or decade) Life shares a natal palace's stem-branch and stars return to the same setup, stacking energy in one spot—re-experiencing, redoing or closing a matter, often old people, old affairs or unfinished lessons resurfacing.

Fan-Yin is the six branch clashes: Zi-Wu, Chou-Wei, Yin-Shen, Mao-You, Chen-Xu, Si-Hai; the year reaches a natal palace's clash position and energy strikes from the opposite, bringing environmental change, reversed plans and impact from outside or the opposite person.

Fu-Yin's keywords are repetition and settling: it offers to finish what was left and resolve an old issue for good; handled well it closes a loop, handled poorly it is spinning in place, emotional drain and replay.

Fan-Yin's keywords are change and response: it forces adjustment, moving whichever two palaces clash (year clashing natal Wealth means financial fluctuation, clashing Spouse means an external test of the bond); it is not inherently good or bad—turbulence carries new chance, and the key is how solid the clashed palace is.

A Step-by-Step Way to Read It

  1. Lay out natal palace stem-branches, then the asked year (decade) Life stem-branch
  2. The same stem-branch as a natal palace is Fu-Yin; mark which matter repeats
  3. The year on a natal six-clash position is Fan-Yin; mark which two palaces and matters are clashed
  4. In a Fu-Yin year focus on closing loops: clear old affairs, debts and unfinished lessons
  5. In a Fan-Yin year focus on contingency and flexibility: buffer the clashed area and seek new exits in the change
  6. Let that palace's stars and transformations decide the real verdict; Fu-Yin/Fan-Yin only describe tempo, not outcome

Three Common Mistakes

Mistake 1: Treating both as bad years when one is repetition and one change, neither a verdict

Mistake 2: Knowing the terms but unable to find stem overlap and six-clash on a real chart

Mistake 3: Letting Fu-Yin/Fan-Yin replace the stars and transformations in reaching a conclusion

Back to Your Own Chart

Fu-Yin and Fan-Yin are two annual tempos: one lets you retake and pass an old lesson, the other forces adjustment amid change. See which one a year is and which matter it touches, and a repeat year becomes a closed loop while a clash year becomes a turning point.

Reading Order

  1. Cast the natal chart first and locate the palaces and major stars tied to natal Fu-Yin and Fan-Yin (repeat and clash) before judging anything
  2. Open the triple-direction of those palaces and count the auspicious, malefic and assistant stars separately
  3. Check where the natal Four Transformations land to set the chart's baseline tone
  4. Read the decade-limit transformations to see whether the natal Fu-Yin and Fan-Yin (repeat and clash) line is activated in this stage
  5. Then read the annual transformations and the year's palace to pin down this year's trigger and timing
  6. Layer natal, decade and year before concluding, then decide how to act rather than labeling good or bad

Reading the Children Palace for a New Recruit: Child or Subordinate First?

Why you confirm the subject before choosing the symbolism, and how to keep the reading within the limits of mentoring and duties.

Zi Wei Dou Shu2026-11-17 09:12

With the same Children Palace, one person asks about a child while another asks about a new recruit, and the symbolism differs. Its original meaning is parent-child, later extended to students, apprentices, juniors, and people you personally train. Confirming which subject is being discussed prevents a mentoring question from being read as a child question.

Ask the subject, then choose the symbolism

The palace offers a range of meanings rather than one answer. For a child, the focus is the bond and interaction; for a junior or recruit, it is how to teach and whether they can take over. With different subjects, the same stars land in different real situations. Clarifying the question is the first step in reading this palace.

Step-by-step mentoring check

  1. Confirm whether the subject is a child, student, or new recruit rather than mixing them.
  2. Read the major stars to judge the recruit's learning style and rhythm.
  3. Use helper and challenging stars to see where teaching tends to get stuck.
  4. Return to the Career and Friends palaces to confirm duties and reporting lines.
  5. Place the chart tendency and real mentoring performance side by side.

Where not to cross the line

The Children Palace in a parent's chart only reflects the relationship tone and interaction. It cannot replace the recruit's own full chart or decide a person's success. Whether mentoring goes smoothly also depends on job design, authorization, and real feedback. Keeping the palace's limits prevents the conclusion from going too far.

Chart reading order

  1. Fix the chart and confirm the Children Palace position and stars.
  2. Clarify the subject before choosing the child or junior symbolism.
  3. Read the major stars for interaction and learning, then the helper stars.
  4. Check duties and reporting through the Career and Friends palaces.
  5. Keep the conclusion conditional rather than reading one palace as a whole life.

How to Read the Siblings Palace: Siblings, Peers, and the Boundaries of Partnership and Lending

The Siblings Palace governs siblings, peers and the bond over cash reserves, touching the resource boundaries of partnership and lending; warmth and rules must be kept separate, and any lending or cooperation relies on a qualified lawyer, proper channels and a written contract.

Zi Wei Dou Shu2026-10-04

The Siblings Palace covers more than brothers and sisters; it also covers resource dealings with peers, who are helpers when clear and a source of hurt over money when boundaries are vague.

What This Question Is Really Asking

Some treat the palace as only sibling relationships and find no rules when friends partner or borrow; the issue is not separating warmth from financial boundaries.

The Underlying Logic

The Siblings Palace governs siblings and peers and is also a reference for cash reserves and short-term turnover.

Its stars and transformations show whether peers support or drain, with Hua Lu reliable and Hua Ji tangled.

Partnership and lending also read the Wealth and Friends palaces, with roles, repayment and exit written down.

Warmth is warmth and rules are rules; contracts and lending rely on a qualified lawyer and proper channels.

A Step-by-Step Way to Read It

  1. First locate the Siblings Palace major star and trine to write the basic bond with siblings and peers
  2. Read natal transformations to judge whether peers lean support or tangle
  3. Sort cash dealings and turnover separately
  4. For lending, set up writing first with amount, repayment and exit clear
  5. Before partnership, make roles and responsibilities clear rather than relying on spoken loyalty
  6. In disputes use proper channels and rely on the written contract

Three Common Mistakes

Mistake 1: Speaking only of warmth without rules and getting hurt over money

Mistake 2: Treating the palace as only siblings and ignoring partnership and lending boundaries

Mistake 3: Relying on spoken agreements with no evidence when trouble comes

Back to Your Own Chart

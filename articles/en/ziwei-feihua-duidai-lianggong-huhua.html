Mutual Transformation in Zi Wei Dou Shu: When Two Palaces Transform Into Each Other, a Relationship Becomes Clear

One palace flying outward shows only a one-way flow; a real relationship needs mutuality: A sends Lu into B while B sends Ji back to A. The round trip reveals who gives, who owes and who holds whom—essential for love, partnership and work.

Zi Wei Dou Shu2026-09-09

Beginners see "I send Lu to Spouse, so I treat my partner well" but ignore what flies back. A bond is two-way; without the return flight, any verdict is only half right.

What This Question Is Really Asking

Asking who is more active, who drains whom, or who contributes versus benefits in a partnership means flying transformations from both palace stems and reading the Lu/Quan/Ke/Ji round trip between them.

The Underlying Logic

Every palace has its own stem that can send transformations to others; what flies out is my attitude and action toward that matter or person, and what flies back is how it responds to me.

Mutual Lu is a virtuous cycle of shared resources and growing closeness; I send Lu while the other sends Ji back means I give but it leaves me attached and constrained—often one-sided devotion or being held.

The Ji round trip matters most: mutual Ji drains and entangles both; I send Ji to the other means I cannot let go; the other sends Ji to me means it holds me back. Opposite directions, opposite situations.

For any bond set two focal points (Life-Spouse, Siblings-Friends, Career-Wealth), fly each once, list the outbound and inbound transformations, then judge mutual growth, mutual clash or one-way tilt.

A Step-by-Step Way to Read It

  1. Lock the two palaces whose relationship you are reading as a focal pair
  2. Take each palace's stem and derive a set of transformations
  3. List which of Lu/Quan/Ke/Ji palace A sends into B
  4. Then list what B sends back to A to complete the round trip
  5. Classify: mutual Lu growth, mutual Ji drain, Lu-out/Ji-back one-way tilt and so on
  6. Combine with stars and the decade to name who gives, who owes and who holds whom

Three Common Mistakes

Mistake 1: Flying only one way and declaring the verdict without the return trip

Mistake 2: Treating all Ji as bad when I-send-Ji means I care; opposite directions mean opposite things

Mistake 3: Choosing the wrong focal pair, e.g. Life-Spouse for a partnership, so even correct flights answer the wrong question

Back to Your Own Chart

The essence of mutuality is that a relationship lives in the round trip. Fly both palaces of the bond you care about most; that back-and-forth table comes closer to truth than any single-palace verdict.

Reading Order

  1. Cast the natal chart first and locate the palaces and major stars tied to mutual transformations between two palaces before judging anything
  2. Open the triple-direction of those palaces and count the auspicious, malefic and assistant stars separately
  3. Check where the natal Four Transformations land to set the chart's baseline tone
  4. Read the decade-limit transformations to see whether the mutual transformations between two palaces line is activated in this stage
  5. Then read the annual transformations and the year's palace to pin down this year's trigger and timing
  6. Layer natal, decade and year before concluding, then decide how to act rather than labeling good or bad

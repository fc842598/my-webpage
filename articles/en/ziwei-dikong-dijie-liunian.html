Di Kong and Di Jie in an Annual Cycle

A plain-English guide to Di Kong and Di Jie in an Annual Cycle, with a practical reading order, simple examples, and clear boundaries for Zi Wei Dou Shu learners.

Zi Wei Dou Shu2026-07-01

Di Kong and Di Jie in an Annual Cycle is a practical Zi Wei Dou Shu topic. Start with the relevant palace, then read the opposite palace, supporting structure, and current timing.

What This Means

Di Kong and Di Jie in an annual cycle often point to gaps, leakage, or things that do not stay. They do not mean every income event disappears; they ask where money, time, attention, or certainty leaks after it arrives.

How To Read It

Start with the annual palace being triggered, then check the Wealth Palace, the opposite palace, and the ten-year cycle. If income appears but the structure cannot hold it, the year may bring repairs, delays, unclear pricing, or obligations that drain the gain.

Simple Examples

  • If the Wealth Palace is activated with Di Kong or Di Jie, income may arrive but leave through debt, equipment, medical costs, or poor budgeting.
  • If career is activated, a new project can raise status while hidden time costs reduce the actual return.
  • If travel or clients are involved, platform fees, cancellations, or unpaid preparation may explain why money feels hard to keep.

Practical Order

First define what is leaking: cash, time, trust, or stability. Then read the triggered palace, the Wealth Palace, the opposite palace, and the current cycle. The useful question is not only whether money comes in, but whether the chart can keep it.

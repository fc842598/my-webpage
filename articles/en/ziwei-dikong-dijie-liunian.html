Di Kong and Di Jie are often read as emptiness and depletion. In an annual cycle, they can describe plans that fail to settle, resources that leak, or effort whose return is delayed. The useful question is what becomes unstable this year—not whether everything will be lost.

Annual emptiness needs a palace topic

If the annual cycle activates Wealth, watch collection, pricing, debt, and unplanned costs. In Travel, the issue may be clients, platforms, transport, or changing external rules. In Career, a project may consume time without delivering the expected title or margin. The palace keeps the interpretation specific.

Two ways gains can fail to remain

Example one: annual Wealth meets Di Kong and Di Jie while the decade remains strong. Income may still arrive, but repairs, equipment, taxes, or delayed payment reduce what stays. Example two: annual Travel holds the two stars while Wealth receives Hua Lu. Leads may be plentiful, yet cancellations, platform fees, or unpaid preparation make the apparent opportunity less profitable.

Rules can reduce avoidable leakage

These stars do not require abandoning all activity. They favor verification: written contracts, deposits, payment milestones, budget ceilings, and realistic timelines. If Hua Ji also affects the axis, reduce leverage and leave more room for delay. If Hua Quan or strong main stars support it, the year may reward disciplined restructuring rather than expansion by instinct.

Chart-reading order

Begin with the ten-year context. Locate Di Kong and Di Jie in the annual chart and name the palace topic. Read the opposite palace and triad, then check Wealth even when another palace is primary. Compare Hua Lu, Hua Quan, and Hua Ji. Finish by identifying whether cash, time, trust, or certainty needs protection and which practical, measurable control addresses it this year.

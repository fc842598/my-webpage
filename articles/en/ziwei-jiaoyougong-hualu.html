Friends Palace With Hua Lu: Knowing Everyone and Having Real Allies Are Different

Hua Lu in the Friends Palace gives you a wide social circle. But a big network is not the same as a deep one. Read it against the Brothers Palace to see the full picture.

Zi Wei Dou Shu2026-08-11 10:15

A quick note on terms. In the classical texts, the Friends Palace was called the "Servants Palace" — it ruled your staff, your followers, and your relationship with the crowd. In modern readings it covers three things: your wider social circle, your employees or direct reports, and how you get along with most people in general. Hua Lu is the "Resource Star." When Hua Lu lands here, you tend to be well-liked across a broad group. But the Brothers Palace, not the Friends Palace, is where you find the few people who will actually show up at 2 a.m.

This article covers when a wide network actually converts into opportunity, when it's just noise, and how the Friends and Brothers palaces must be read as a pair.

First Question: Wide Network or Deep Network?

Hua Lu in the Friends Palace gives you what people call "people skills." You're friendly to most people, most people are friendly back, and you can walk into a room and find someone to talk to. This suits sales, PR, media, community building — any role that rewards breadth.

But Hua Lu in the Friends Palace is fundamentally different from Hua Lu in the Brothers Palace. The Brothers Palace governs one-to-one, deep peer bonds. The Friends Palace governs one-to-many, broad popularity. Someone with Friends Palace Hua Lu may have thousands of contacts and a short list of people they'd call in a real emergency. That's not a flaw — it's what this palace does. It does breadth, not depth.

To know whether the network is useful, check the triads. If Hua Lu in Friends is supported by Hua Quan (the "Authority Star") or Hua Ke (the "Recognition Star"), your circle includes people with real weight, and the connections produce outcomes. If the triads only show Wen Qu (the "Writer Star") or Hong Luan (the "Romance Star"), it's more likely dinner-party connections — fun, but not the kind that move the needle.

Employees and Teams: People Who Follow vs. People Who Deliver

The second line of the Friends Palace is direct reports and team members. Hua Lu here generally means people want to work for you — the atmosphere is good and turnover is lower than average. But "willing to follow" and "able to execute" are different.

Hua Lu with Tian Kui or Tian Yue (the "Benefactor Stars") means a standout employee shows up — someone who carries a project when you need it. Hua Lu with Zuo Fu or You Bi (the "Assistant Stars") means a stable, coordinated team that works well together over time. Hua Lu with Wen Chang or Wen Qu (the "Scholar Stars") means you attract writers, communicators, and creative types.

With challenging stars, the picture changes. Hua Lu plus Qing Yang (the "Sharpening Star") means a team with temper — conflict, arguments, people who push back hard. Hua Lu plus Di Kong or Di Jie (the "Emptiness Stars") means hiring mistakes — people who interview well and underdeliver. Hua Lu plus Tuo Luo (the "Stagnation Star") means a team that needs constant prodding. In all three cases, Hua Lu still brings the people in; it just doesn't guarantee they're the right people.

A concrete pattern: Friends Palace has Tai Yang with Hua Lu and Tian Kui, with Zuo Fu in the triads. Tai Yang manages and leads, Kui brings a standout person, Zuo Fu stabilizes the team. This is a strong leadership placement — you attract capable people and they stay.

Read It Against the Brothers Palace

The Friends Palace sits opposite the Brothers Palace. You cannot read one without the other, because breadth and depth together define your social pattern.

Hua Lu in Friends with Hua Ji (the "Obstacle Star") or harsh stars in the Brothers Palace is the classic "popular but lonely" pattern. You work a room effortlessly, but the inner circle is thin — or worse, the people closest to you cause the most trouble. The lesson here is to curate, not collect. Don't mistake a full calendar for a support system.

The reverse — Hua Lu in Brothers but challenging stars in Friends — means a few rock-solid relationships and discomfort with crowds. You don't need to become a networker. Protect the few deep bonds and don't force breadth that doesn't suit you.

If the Brothers Palace is empty, do not assume you lack close friends. An empty palace means you borrow stars from the opposite Friends Palace and check the triads. If the borrowed stars are bright and supported, depth can grow out of your wider circle — you meet many people and some of them become close. Only when both the opposite palace and triads show harsh stars without rescue do close relationships genuinely tend to disappoint.

In a Current Year: New Circles and New Hires

There are three timing triggers for the Friends Palace.

First: the yearly Life Palace lands in your Friends Palace. That year your focus shifts to people — a new job with a new team, a new community, a leadership role. You'll meet more people than usual. The task is to sort the useful connections from the noise as you go.

Second: a yearly Hua Lu flies into the Friends Palace. This is a strong social year — a helpful person appears, a good hire lands, a connection opens a door. If a yearly Hua Ji flies in instead, it's a backstabbing or team-conflict year. Don't rely on verbal promises; get roles and agreements in writing, and don't take sides in office politics.

Third: a ten-year Friends Palace carries Hua Lu. Over the decade your network expands meaningfully. This is the time to build an audience, a team, or a community. Ten-year Lu is slow — the people you meet in year one may not pay off until year six. Keep showing up; don't rush to monetize every contact.

Reading Order After You Cast the Chart

When you find Hua Lu in the Friends Palace, read it in this order:

  1. Confirm this is breadth, not depth — depth lives in the Brothers Palace.
  2. Check the triads for Hua Quan, Hua Ke, or Kui-Yue to see whether the network carries real weight.
  3. Read the employee line: supporting stars mean a strong team; challenging stars mean headcount without performance.
  4. Read the opposite Brothers Palace for the depth picture. If it's empty, borrow and check triads first.
  5. For timing, separate the yearly Life Palace landing (new circles), yearly Lu flying in (a specific ally or hire), and ten-year Lu (slow network building).

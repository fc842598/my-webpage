Anger Management in Zi Wei Dou Shu: How Quick-Tempered Qing Yang and Huo Xing Types Stay Steady

Anger in the chart is not mere bad temper but distinct malefic patterns: Qing Yang is instantly ignited sharp fire, Huo Xing a fast-come-fast-go eruption, Ling Xing smoldering silence, Tuo Luo repeated rehashing, layered with Wu Qu/Qi Sha's hardness and Lian Zhen the prisoner star's suppressed rebound. Stability depends on whether Lu/Ke harmonize the malefics and whether Karma is steady; beneath anger usually lie powerlessness and a violated boundary.

Zi Wei Dou Shu2026-09-15

Some explode and recover, some suppress then suddenly erupt, some stay angry over an old matter for half a year; these different anger styles map to different malefics and need entirely different handling.

What This Question Is Really Asking

Asking why you cannot control temper, regret after impulses or seethe silently, first see which malefic dominates and manage that eruption pattern rather than vaguely telling yourself not to be angry.

The Underlying Logic

Eruption style reads the four malefics: Qing Yang in Life/Karma/Career reacts instantly, words outrunning thought, drawing blood on the spot; Huo Xing surges fiercely but passes once vented; Ling Xing does not erupt in the moment but remembers, goes cold and accumulates before a concentrated blow; Tuo Luo keeps replaying after the fact, angrier each round. Matching your style is how you find the fix.

Suppressed rebound reads Lian Zhen and Wu Qu/Qi Sha: Lian Zhen the prisoner star locks feelings away and erupts harder at the tipping point; Wu Qu/Qi Sha are rigid and unwilling to yield, using anger to mask powerlessness in setbacks. Such setups need an early outlet, not suppression.

Harmonizing reads Lu/Ke and Karma: a malefic buffered by Hua Lu/Hua Ke/Lu Cun has cushion and can stay on the issue; steady Karma with Tian Fu/Tian Xiang recovers fast. Karma carrying further malefics/Ji pours oil on fire, magnifying a trifle into a matter of principle.

Trigger rhythm reads decade/year: periods when the year activates Qing Yang/Huo Xing see high conflict, so lower the load early and avoid head-on clashes; anger is usually a secondary emotion, beneath it often an unseen need or a crossed boundary—articulating that layer halves the fire.

A Step-by-Step Way to Read It

  1. First identify your dominant malefic—Qing Yang sharp, Huo Xing fast, Ling Xing smoldering, Tuo Luo looping—and match yourself
  2. Qing Yang types pause three seconds before speaking; Ling Xing types express on the spot instead of accumulating; Tuo Luo types practice closing the matter
  3. Read Lian Zhen/Wu Qu/Qi Sha to spot anger masking powerlessness or suppressed rebound
  4. Check whether Lu/Ke buffer the malefics and whether Karma is steady to gauge recovery speed
  5. Before anger, ask one layer down: what is my real need and which boundary was crossed
  6. In year-activated Huo/Qing phases cut load, avoid head-on clashes and release early through exercise and writing

Three Common Mistakes

Mistake 1: Prescribing one anger fix for everyone while ignoring the four malefics' distinct eruption styles

Mistake 2: Telling a Lian Zhen type to suppress until the tipping-point blow-up is harder to manage

Mistake 3: Only suppressing the fire without addressing the underlying need and boundary, so the same anger reignites again and again

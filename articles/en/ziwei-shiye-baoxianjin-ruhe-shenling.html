Zi Wei Dou Shu on Claiming Unemployment Benefits: Involuntary Loss

Whether you can claim unemployment benefits depends mainly on involuntary loss of employment, at least one year of contributions and unemployment registration. The Career and Wealth blockage in the chart is only timing; eligibility and duration follow the social-insurance agency.

Zi Wei Dou Shu2026-10-29

Unemployment benefits are a legal right after paying unemployment insurance; claim them when eligible, not a handout.

What This Question Is Really Asking

Some see claiming benefits as shameful or harmful later and do not apply even when eligible, giving up protection they paid for.

The Underlying Logic

Claiming benefits corresponds to Career and Wealth blockage, marking interrupted income and the need for transitional support.

Conditions usually include a year of contributions, involuntary loss, unemployment registration and willingness to seek work.

Duration ties to contribution years, and basic medical insurance is paid from the unemployment fund during the claim.

Voluntary resignation generally does not qualify, but constructive dismissal due to employer fault may count as involuntary.

A Step-by-Step Way to Read It

  1. Confirm whether the loss of employment is involuntary
  2. Check whether unemployment insurance has been paid for at least a year
  3. Register unemployment, state willingness to work and prepare documents
  4. Read the Career and Wealth Palaces to judge the transition length
  5. Confirm duration and medical-insurance coverage by contribution years
  6. Follow the social-insurance agency and claim when eligible

Three Common Mistakes

Mistake 1: Seeing benefits as shameful and giving up rights

Mistake 2: Not checking the reason and assuming resignation qualifies

Mistake 3: Not registering unemployment and missing the claim

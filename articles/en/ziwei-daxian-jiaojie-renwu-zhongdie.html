The Major-Cycle Handover: Why Old and New Tasks Overlap

Why the year of a major-cycle palace change brings both old endings and new beginnings, and how to order them.

Zi Wei Dou Shu2026-11-13 11:04

A major cycle covers roughly ten years of palaces. In the handover year, the old cycle's endings and the new cycle's start often run together, so tasks and responsibilities appear to overlap.

Why overlap happens

The change is not a sudden cut. Matters from the old palace need closure while the new palace's themes begin. In real life, an old project may still be open when a new role already arrives. Treating it as strictly old or new makes the overlap surprising.

Judgment steps

  1. Confirm the old and new major-cycle palaces and mark the handover year.
  2. Read the new cycle's stars, transformations, and supporting palaces for the next theme.
  3. List the old cycle's unfinished items and identify which must close.
  4. Read the annual palace to see whether the year leans old or new.
  5. Stabilize endings first, then shift weight gradually before concluding.

Reading order

  1. Fix the inputs and software version, then confirm the two palaces and year.
  2. Record old endings and the new main line separately.
  3. Check stars, transformations, and the annual cycle for the direction of shift.
  4. Put tasks in time order and separate must-finish from deferrable items.
  5. Write the answer as closure plus launch, not one single theme.

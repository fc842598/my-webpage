Tian Ji in the Wealth Palace: Salary or Project Fees?

Use Tian Ji, Hua Ke, Wen Qu, and the Friends Palace to compare predictable salary income with project-based professional fees.

Zi Wei Dou Shu2026-08-23 09:18

Two people can sell the same analytical skill in very different ways. One receives a predictable monthly salary. The other quotes each assignment, manages revisions, and waits for the final payment. A high project price may look attractive, but it does not automatically create dependable income.

In Zi Wei Dou Shu, Tian Ji in the Wealth Palace starts with the idea of regular earned income. Wen Qu and Hua Ke can add a professional skill that clients recognize and pay for separately. The question is therefore not which job title Tian Ji should choose. It is whether the chart and the person's actual records support salary, project fees, or a measured mix of both.

Tian Ji in Wealth Starts With Predictable Earned Income

In traditional Zi Wei Dou Shu, Tian Ji in the Wealth Palace is associated with steady earned income. Its most direct modern expression is work performed for an organization in exchange for an amount that can be anticipated each month. The value lies in cash-flow visibility rather than dramatic upside.

A person does not need to reject salary to prove independence. When household costs are high or client demand is irregular, predictable pay is a useful base. Tian Ji alone does not require a freelance business or guarantee that occasional requests will become revenue.

Project Fees Need a Professional Deliverable

Hua Ke in the Wealth Palace points to recognized expertise, and Wen Qu can support work expressed through a clear professional output. Together they make it more reasonable to charge for a report, plan, analysis, or another defined service. They do not promise a full calendar or prompt payment.

Example 1: Tian Ji is in the Wealth Palace without a strong professional-recognition pattern, while Tian Ji and Tian Ma appear in Friends. The person receives many requests and introductions but few signed projects, so salary remains the reliable income source. Extra work should be priced before substantial time is committed.

Activity in Friends Is Not the Same as Income

Tian Ji with Tian Ma in the Friends Palace describes movement and coordination for clients, partners, or introductions. Calls, meetings, and referrals can consume a week without producing personal revenue. The Wealth Palace still has to be confirmed through a price, a contract, a deposit, and final payment.

Example 2: Tian Ji in Wealth is supported by Wen Qu and Hua Ke, and the Body Palace also falls in Wealth. A defined service has produced repeat projects and reliable payment for six months, making a gradual increase in project income reasonable. A cash reserve is still needed because project timing varies.

Let Six Months of Accounts Set the Mix

Track salary, signed project value, money actually received, delivery hours, revision time, and client-acquisition cost for six months. Use net receipts rather than quoted prices. Repeat business is stronger evidence than one unusually profitable assignment.

If project income reliably covers essential expenses and the person has at least six months of reserves, the project share can rise gradually. If most work comes through one friend, payments are late, or coordination remains unpaid, keep salary as the base. Changing the ratio is safer than forcing an immediate all-or-nothing decision.

Practical Reading Order

Start with Tian Ji in the Wealth Palace as a regular-income pattern. Then check Wen Qu and Hua Ke for a professional deliverable that can be priced, and note whether the Body Palace also emphasizes Wealth. Read Tian Ji with Tian Ma in Friends as coordination for other people until contracts and payments prove otherwise. Use six months of receipts, repeat business, work hours, and cash reserves to set the actual salary-to-project ratio.

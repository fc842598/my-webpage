Zi Wei Dou Shu on Non-Compete Compensation: Career, Travel and Wealth

A non-compete bars you from competitors for a period after leaving, often linking the Career Palace to the Travel and Wealth Palaces. It requires monthly economic compensation; without it, or with an excessive duration, the clause may not be enforceable.

Zi Wei Dou Shu2026-10-29

A non-compete restricts your choice of work, so the employer must pay monthly compensation, not impose duties without payment.

What This Question Is Really Asking

Some sign a non-compete when leaving, receive no payment, and still avoid peers, needlessly restricting themselves.

The Underlying Logic

A non-compete links the Career Palace to the Travel and Wealth Palaces, marking restricted job choice and movement between platforms.

During the period the employer must pay monthly compensation, generally not below a set share of prior pay or the local standard, and the term usually cannot exceed two years.

If the employer fails to pay for three months, the worker may seek to end the non-compete; a clause imposing duties without payment is hard to enforce.

When Hua Ji enters Wealth and Travel, compensation tends to be delayed or re-employment blocked, so rely on the written agreement to make claims.

A Step-by-Step Way to Read It

  1. Confirm whether the non-compete is written, with its scope and term
  2. Check whether monthly compensation and its standard are set
  3. Keep the agreement, separation paperwork and pay records
  4. Read the Career Palace with Travel and Wealth to gauge how much job choice is restricted
  5. If the employer does not pay, seek to end the clause or claim compensation
  6. Where needed, rely on professional legal advice and arbitration

Three Common Mistakes

Mistake 1: Bearing non-compete duties without checking compensation

Mistake 2: Self-restricting and avoiding peers even when the employer does not pay

Mistake 3: Not questioning a clearly excessive term or scope

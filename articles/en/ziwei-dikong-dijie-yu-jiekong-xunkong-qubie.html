Learn Zi Wei/Assistant & Malefic Stars

Di Kong/Di Jie vs. Jie Kong/Xun Kong: One Scatters, the Other Interrupts—Do Not Mix Them

Di Kong/Di Jie are the void pair among the six malefics—scattering resources and mental/material loss. Jie Kong/Xun Kong (Xun Kong/Tian Kong) are a separate void-auxiliary set—interruption, suspension and discounting good things. They differ in origin, strength and how events manifest.

Zi Wei Dou Shu2026-09-08

Two groups of stars carry "void," and beginners mix them. One scatters what exists, the other suspends or interrupts; separating them keeps you from blanket verdicts of "void means nothing."

What This Question Is Really Asking

Some read any void as loss or no connection and are right only sometimes. Ask whether it is Di Kong/Di Jie's substantive dissipation or Jie Kong/Xun Kong's "looks available but interrupted at the last step."

The Underlying Logic

Di Kong/Di Jie are placed by birth hour and sit adjacent, with heavier, longer-term force: Di Kong leans to mental expectations falling through, Di Jie to material resources taken, financial loss and change.

Jie Kong/Xun Kong come from the xun-void method (matching ten stems to twelve branches), lighter and event-based: sudden interruption, suspension, missing by a hair through timing.

The void pair is a "leak"—things truly leave the hand; the xun-void is a "jam"—things remain but are temporarily unusable or suspended, possibly flowing again after the node.

Both buffer Hua Ji and malefics (voiding part of their force) and both discount auspicious stars in the same palace, so always read which palace and matter is made void.

A Step-by-Step Way to Read It

  1. Locate Di Kong/Di Jie and Jie Kong/Xun Kong separately and identify which group sits in the palace in question
  2. For Di Kong/Di Jie read scattering/leaking, focusing on how resources and expectations drain
  3. For Jie Kong/Xun Kong read interruption/suspension/missing-by-a-hair, focusing on timing nodes
  4. See who shares the palace: voiding an auspicious star discounts it; voiding a malefic/Ji buffers it
  5. With decade/year, void pairs show as stage-level dissipation, xun-voids as specific event jams
  6. Match the remedy: plug leaks and reduce for scattering; wait out the node and fix paperwork for interruption

Three Common Mistakes

Mistake 1: Lumping the two void groups and always predicting loss

Mistake 2: Reading "void" as total absence while xun-void only suspends temporarily and can reopen

Mistake 3: Seeing only the negative and missing that voids also buffer part of a malefic/Ji force

Back to Your Own Chart

Both voids make the full feel empty, but Di Kong/Di Jie truly leak while Jie Kong/Xun Kong temporarily jam. Distinguish them on your chart, and you will know whether a falling-through matter calls for cutting losses or simply outlasting a suspended node.

Reading Order

  1. Cast the natal chart first and locate the palaces and major stars tied to the difference between Di Kong/Di Jie and Jie Kong/Xun Kong before judging anything
  2. Open the triple-direction of those palaces and count the auspicious, malefic and assistant stars separately
  3. Check where the natal Four Transformations land to set the chart's baseline tone
  4. Read the decade-limit transformations to see whether the the difference between Di Kong/Di Jie and Jie Kong/Xun Kong line is activated in this stage
  5. Then read the annual transformations and the year's palace to pin down this year's trigger and timing
  6. Layer natal, decade and year before concluding, then decide how to act rather than labeling good or bad

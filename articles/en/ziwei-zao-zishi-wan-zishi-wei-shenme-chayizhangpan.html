Why Early and Late Zi Hour Produce Different Zi Wei Charts: The Line Across Midnight

The Zi hour spans 23:00–01:00; 23:00–24:00 belongs to the current day (late Zi) and 00:00–01:00 to the next (early Zi). Crossing midnight changes the day pillar and Life Palace, so ignoring this split can produce a wrong chart.

Eleven at night is the day's trickiest boundary: crossing it changes the date and potentially the whole chart, so night births need the early/late Zi check.

What This Question Is Really Asking

Many born near 23:00 never get a matching chart—not because the system fails but because the cross-midnight line was mishandled; a wrong day pillar means a wrong whole chart.

The Underlying Logic

Every block lasts two hours, but Zi alone crosses midnight, split into late Zi (23–24) and early Zi (00–01).

Late Zi keeps the current day's stem-branch; early Zi enters the next day. Different day pillars can yield entirely different Life Palace and stars.

Schools differ on whether late Zi changes the day pillar; the safe approach casts both and verifies.

Cross-midnight births must verify with birth records and major life events; memory alone is error-prone.

A Step-by-Step Way to Read It

  1. Confirm whether birth time falls between 23:00 and 01:00
  2. Distinguish late Zi (23–24) from early Zi (00–01)
  3. Cast both the current-day and next-day candidate charts per your school
  4. Verify both against personality, family and major past events
  5. Adopt the chart that fits on more points and record the rule used
  6. Run all later decades and years on the verified chart

Three Common Mistakes

Mistake 1: Counting all post-23:00 births as the same day with no split

Mistake 2: Not knowing the early/late Zi distinction and doubting the system when charts do not fit

Mistake 3: Picking the preferred candidate instead of verifying with facts

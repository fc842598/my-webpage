The Friends Palace: Friends, Subordinates, and Your Social Network

Once called the Servants Palace, the Friends Palace now describes friends, colleagues, subordinates, and the wider support network.

Zi Wei Dou Shu2026-11-16 10:35

The old name means servants and reflects an older social structure. The modern reading treats this palace as your social network: friends, colleagues, subordinates, employees, and the people around you who are not family. Understanding that shift keeps the reading from becoming too narrow.

Who belongs to this palace

In modern practice, it describes the kinds of friends and colleagues you meet, whether subordinates are reliable, and whether support is available when problems arise. It differs from the Siblings Palace, which leans toward close peers and family, while the Friends Palace covers a wider layer including work partners and people who report to you.

How stars describe social life

A stable palace with many helpful stars often points to sincere friends and smooth teamwork. Tense stars or a strong challenging transformation call for care in judging people, being dragged down, or staff turnover. The palace reads the overall quality of relationships rather than the fate of one specific friend, so the opposite and supporting palaces should be included.

Step-by-step reading

  1. Separate the topic into friends, colleagues, subordinates, or the wider social circle.
  2. Confirm the distinction from the Siblings Palace so close peers are not mixed in.
  3. Read the major and helper stars to judge social style and team stability.
  4. Check whether transformations bring support or complications.
  5. Include the opposite and supporting palaces before concluding on the network.

Reading limits

The palace describes social tendencies; it does not decide how one friend will behave or promise a permanently stable team. Tense stars should not be read as guaranteed betrayal. Real communication, management, and boundaries decide the outcome, while the chart flags risks.

Chart reading order

  1. Fix the chart and locate the Friends Palace with its supporting palaces.
  2. Identify the real group of people before choosing a reading.
  3. Read the palace stars for social quality, then the transformations.
  4. Include the opposite and supporting palaces rather than judging one box alone.
  5. Match the conclusion to real relationships and teams without absolute claims.

Zi Wei Dou Shu Schools Compared: How to Choose Between San He, Flying Star and Zhongzhou

Different Zi Wei Dou Shu schools read the same chart differently: San He stresses star combinations, Flying Star stresses palace-stem transformations, and Zhongzhou stresses star-system structure. Learn what each does well, then choose by your question instead of panicking at conflicting answers.

Zi Wei Dou Shu2026-09-30

After studying for a while, people notice that different teachers read the same palace differently—one calls it auspicious, another points to a transformation. It is rarely a matter of right or wrong; the schools are looking at different layers.

What This Question Is Really Asking

The question is where the schools actually differ and how to choose. The key is understanding the entry points of the three main approaches, then deciding which one leads and which one supplements based on what you are asking.

The Underlying Logic

The San He school stresses star combinations: how major stars share palaces, shine across, meet in the triple-direction, and pair with assistant and malefic stars. It excels at describing temperament, relationship patterns and the overall shape of a matter.

The Flying Star school stresses palace-stem transformations: the Lu, Quan, Ke and Ji set flying from each palace stem. It excels at reading energy exchange between palaces, causal pulls and the comings and goings of specific events.

The Zhongzhou school stresses star-system structure: fixed star systems such as the Zi Wei and Tian Fu systems and how the decade and annual cycles align with them. It excels at staged reasoning and big-picture layers.

The schools are not rivals but layers: set the structural tone with systems and combinations, then use flying transformations to see how events trigger, and the conclusion is steadier than clinging to one school.

A Step-by-Step Way to Read It

  1. First decide whether you are asking a structure question or an event question
  2. Structure questions—temperament, relationship patterns, long-term patterns—lead with San He and star-system reading
  3. Event questions—how a matter unfolds and who pulls whom—lead with Flying Star transformations
  4. For decade and annual staged reasoning, consult the Zhongzhou star-system alignment
  5. When two answers clash, first check whether the stars and transformations used even match, then whether the layers differ
  6. Finally layer the structure and flying-transformation readings yourself instead of trusting a single label

Three Common Mistakes

Mistake 1: Treating school differences as true versus false and insisting on one correct answer

Mistake 2: Splitting the difference between conflicting answers and ending up using neither method correctly

Mistake 3: Learning one school’s rhymes and dismissing the others without seeing you only read one layer

Back to Your Own Chart

No school is simply superior; the match is to the question—structure from San He, events from Flying Star, stages from Zhongzhou. Treat the three as three lenses on one chart and your judgment gains real depth.

Reading Order

  1. Cast the natal chart first and locate the palaces and major stars tied to differences between Zi Wei schools before judging anything
  2. Open the triple-direction of those palaces and count the auspicious, malefic and assistant stars separately
  3. Check where the natal Four Transformations land to set the chart's baseline tone
  4. Read the decade-limit transformations to see whether the differences between Zi Wei schools line is activated in this stage
  5. Then read the annual transformations and the year's palace to pin down this year's trigger and timing
  6. Layer natal, decade and year before concluding, then decide how to act rather than labeling good or bad

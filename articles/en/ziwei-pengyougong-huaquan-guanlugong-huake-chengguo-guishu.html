Friends Palace Hua Quan and Career Palace Hua Ke: Who Gets Credit and Who Gets Paid?

When a partner controls the client and schedule while you create the professional work, separate project authority, authorship, licensing, and payment.

Zi Wei Dou Shu2026-08-05 21:49

A partner brings in the client, runs the meetings, and sets every deadline. You do the research, write the report, and solve the technical problem. When the project ends, the partner claims that controlling the account also means controlling the work, the credit, and the money. Those are four different rights, not one package.

In Zi Wei Dou Shu, the Friends Palace includes collaborators and business partners. Hua Quan emphasizes authority and control, while Hua Ke in the Career Palace makes professional work and reputation more visible. The chart can clarify the role split, but the contract must decide authorship, approval, licensing, and payment.

Friends Palace Hua Quan Shows Who Runs the Workflow

The Friends Palace describes people who collaborate, introduce opportunities, or share a project. Hua Quan there can put client access, scheduling, revisions, and delivery approval in the partner's hands. Operational authority matters, but it does not automatically make that partner the author or owner of every deliverable.

Example 1: Hua Quan is in the Friends Palace and Hua Ke is in the Career Palace. The partner controls client communication and deadlines while the specialist writes the report; the contract identifies the author separately from the person who approves delivery.

Career Palace Hua Ke Identifies the Professional Contribution

Hua Ke can describe writing, teaching, examinations, creative work, or specialized knowledge. In the Career Palace, it brings attention to the professional contribution: the report, model, course, design, or code that can be identified and evaluated. Name the deliverable before discussing who may use it.

A useful agreement lists four roles separately. The author creates the original work, the approver accepts delivery, the licensor controls copying and later use, and the payee receives the money. One company or person can hold several roles, but none should be assumed from project control alone.

Without Hua Lu in the Wealth Palace, Verify the Payment Path

When the Friends-Palace Hua Quan and Career-Palace Hua Ke pattern receives no Hua Lu support from the Wealth Palace, project control and professional visibility do not prove that income reaches the specialist. Follow the invoice, fee agreement, and bank deposit instead.

Example 2: The same Friends-Palace Hua Quan and Career-Palace Hua Ke pattern appears, but the Wealth Palace has no Hua Lu support. The specialist earns the reputation, but all client payments enter the partner's account; a fixed service fee plus a written license fee creates a payment path tied to the work.

Even with Hua Lu, Keep Licensing Separate from Payment

Hua Lu in Wealth can strengthen the connection to income, but it still does not define the legal scope of use. A one-time delivery, a limited license, and a full buyout carry different rights and prices. State the territory, duration, editing rights, attribution, fee, and payment date.

Example 3: Hua Quan is in Friends, Hua Ke is in Career, and Hua Lu supports Wealth. A training platform controls sales and scheduling while the instructor keeps named authorship and receives a per-cohort license fee; invoices, split statements, and deposits verify the arrangement.

Practical Reading Order

Read Hua Quan in the Friends Palace first to identify who controls the client, schedule, revisions, and approval. Then read Hua Ke in the Career Palace and name the report, course, design, code, or other professional deliverable. Check the Wealth Palace for authority, Lu support, and the actual payment route. Before delivery, write down the author, approver, license scope, term, service or license fee, reconciliation date, and receiving account.

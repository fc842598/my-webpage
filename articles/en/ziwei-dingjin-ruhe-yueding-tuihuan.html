Zi Wei Dou Shu on Deposit Clauses and Refunds: Read the Wealth Palace First

Paying a sum up front to secure a contract often corresponds to the annual Wealth Palace being activated together with the Property or Career Palace that supplies the deal. A penalty deposit is not the same as a refundable booking payment, so the wording and rules should be checked before paying.

Zi Wei Dou Shu2026-10-30

The key is not whether to pay, but first telling a penalty deposit from a refundable booking payment, then deciding the amount and the written terms.

What This Question Is Really Asking

Many transfer the money as soon as they hear the word deposit without checking which term the contract uses, only to find it is non-refundable when they change their mind, or not knowing they can claim double return when the other side breaches.

The Underlying Logic

A deposit is a form of guarantee, often appearing when the Wealth Palace is activated together with the property or career deal, marking an upfront sum that binds the later contract.

A penalty deposit carries rules: if the paying party breaches, the deposit is kept; if the receiving party breaches, it must be returned double. A booking payment is usually treated as an advance and is refundable without special terms.

The deposit amount is agreed by the parties but cannot exceed a set share of the main contract value, and the excess does not carry deposit effect.

When the Wealth Palace stars are steady and the written terms are clear, the deal tends to proceed as agreed; with Hua Ji and malefics, put the term, amount and refund conditions in the contract rather than relying on words.

A Step-by-Step Way to Read It

  1. Confirm whether the contract says penalty deposit or refundable booking payment, as the rules differ
  2. Check whether the amount is within the legal ratio, since the excess does not carry the deposit penalty
  3. Put the amount, timing and refund conditions in the contract and keep proof of payment
  4. Read the annual Wealth Palace and the deal palace to judge whether the guarantee is sound
  5. If you may change your mind, weigh the cost of a non-refundable deposit before paying
  6. When the other side breaches, claim double return on the written terms, with the law and contract as the basis

Three Common Mistakes

Mistake 1: Ignoring the difference between the two deposit terms and finding it non-refundable after paying

Mistake 2: Not checking an excessive amount, leaving the excess unenforceable

Mistake 3: Relying on verbal terms without a written contract and payment proof

Born Overseas for a Zi Wei Dou Shu Chart: Time Zone and Daylight Saving Checks

What to verify when a birth record uses a foreign local time, including time zone and daylight saving rules.

Zi Wei Dou Shu2026-11-13 11:04

A birth recorded abroad uses local clock time, with a time zone and often daylight saving behind it. Entering that number as if it were Beijing time can shift the whole chart, so clarify the source before converting.

Three time layers to separate

First is the local clock time on the birth paper. Second is the time zone and whether daylight saving was active. Third is the time after conversion into the chart's convention. Mixing these layers often leads to a missed daylight saving hour or a double-counted offset.

Verification steps

  1. Keep the original local date, clock time, and birthplace exactly as recorded.
  2. Confirm the local time zone in use at the time and its offset.
  3. Check whether daylight saving applied that year, with its dates and clock shift.
  4. Restore the recorded time to standard time before converting it.
  5. Check whether the result crosses a date or hour boundary and list the candidates.

Reading order

  1. Fix the original local record and birthplace, then check zone and daylight saving.
  2. Restore standard time using the rules of that year, not today's rules.
  3. After conversion, check whether the date or hour changed.
  4. Enter the chart in one fixed software version when comparing candidates.
  5. Read stars, supporting palaces, and cycles only after the input is stable.

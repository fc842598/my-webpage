The Po Jun Decade: Breaking First to Build After, Replacing the Old with New Ground

Po Jun governs transformation, restart and success after expenditure. In its decade, life often brings restructuring, moves, transitions and reshuffled relationships, as the old must go for the new to come. The strength is the courage to begin again; the risk is major breaks, upheaval and loss, so the key is being clear on what to break, what to build and what serves as a safety net.

Zi Wei Dou Shu2026-10-01

A Po Jun decade often feels unusually eventful: work, housing and relationships may all be rebuilt; pushing through leads to an entirely new pattern, though the breaking brings unavoidable upheaval and cost.

What This Question Is Really Asking

The question is how to use a Po Jun decade and guard against major breaks. Understand its break-first-build-after nature, then see whether Lu aids rebuilding or malefics add upheaval.

The Underlying Logic

Po Jun governs expenditure, change and pioneering, and this decade suits ending situations that no longer fit and replanning life direction and rhythm.

Its breaking is not destruction but making space: breaking without clarity on what to build easily leads to long upheaval.

With Lu Cun or Hua Lu, resources exist to rebuild after the break and things improve; with malefics or Ji, major breaks and repetition damage both relationships and assets.

The lesson is moving with a plan: set the goal and safety net first, then break in an orderly way and complete the restructuring in stages.

A Step-by-Step Way to Read It

  1. First confirm Po Jun hosts the decade Life Palace and check its brightness
  2. List the situations you want to end or change these years
  3. First clarify the new direction and goal to build before breaking
  4. Assess resources and fallback—money, housing, relationships—and keep a safety cushion
  5. Carry out restructuring in stages rather than tearing everything down at once
  6. With the annual cycles, see which years suit transformation and which favor settling the gains

Three Common Mistakes

Mistake 1: Taking the Po Jun decade only as big change and upheaval while ignoring rebuilding plans

Mistake 2: Breaking before knowing what to build and falling into long instability

Mistake 3: Tearing down work, relationships and assets together with no fallback or buffer

Back to Your Own Chart

The Po Jun decade gives ten years of breaking first and rebuilding after. Moving with a plan, keeping a fallback and advancing in stages lets the old situation give way to new ground that fits you better.

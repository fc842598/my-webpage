Zi Wei Dou Shu on Rental Contracts: Property for Housing, Wealth for Deposits

A rental contract often maps to the Property Palace for housing and the Wealth Palace for rent and deposits. The term, deposit arrangement, repairs, subletting and termination should be clear before paying and inspecting the place, to avoid disputes after moving in.

Zi Wei Dou Shu2026-10-30

The key is checking the terms and inspecting the place first, then paying the deposit and rent, with every sum and duty on paper.

What This Question Is Really Asking

Some transfer money once they like the place, without a written contract or checking the appliances, and later find faults or lose the deposit at move-out with nothing to prove their case.

The Underlying Logic

A rental contract is usually triggered when the Property Palace covers housing and the Wealth Palace deposits, marking a tenancy and its cash arrangement.

Core terms include the term, rent and payment, deposit return, repair duties, subletting, termination and breach handling.

Before signing, inspect the place and appliances, check utility and property fees, and write a handover list with the current condition.

When the Property Palace is steady and the contract and handover records are complete, living there tends to go smoothly; with Hua Ji and malefics, put deposit return and repair duties in writing rather than trusting verbal promises.

A Step-by-Step Way to Read It

  1. First confirm the landlord’s identity and right to rent out
  2. Check the term, rent and deposit arrangement and the return conditions
  3. Clarify repairs, subletting, termination and breach duties
  4. Inspect appliances and utilities at viewing and make a written handover list
  5. Read the Property and Wealth Palaces to judge housing and deposit risk
  6. Pay only after the terms are complete and inspection is done, keeping the contract and proof

Three Common Mistakes

Mistake 1: Paying without a written contract, leaving duties unclear

Mistake 2: Not inspecting the place and appliances, making responsibility hard to assign

Mistake 3: Not writing deposit return terms, risking deductions at move-out

Children Palace With Hua Lu: Kids, Creations, and Passive Income Are Not the Same Thing

The Children Palace covers more than children. It also rules your creative work, students, and investments you don't manage day to day. Hua Lu here means returns on what you put out.

Zi Wei Dou Shu2026-08-11 10:15

A quick note on terms. In Zi Wei Dou Shu, the Children Palace does not only cover your children. It covers everything you create and send out into the world: your kids, your students, your books and products, and money you invest rather than earn directly. Hua Lu is the "Resource Star." When Hua Lu lands in the Children Palace, the things you extend outward tend to come back with something attached. But which thing — a child, a creation, or a return on investment — depends on the stars.

This article separates the three lines so you don't read a creative breakthrough as a pregnancy, or a profitable investment as a sign about your kid.

The Children Line: Close Bond Is Not the Same as Easy to Raise

Hua Lu in the Children Palace does suggest a warm bond with your children. The relationship tends to be affectionate, and kids gravitate to you. But "close" and "easy" are different words.

Hua Lu with Tian Liang (the "Elder Star") or Tian Tong (the "Comfort Star") gives you a gentle, sweet child — but one who may be dependent and slow to leave the nest. Hua Lu with Tan Lang (the "Desire Star") gives you a bright, curious, quick-learning kid who also gets bored fast and needs steering. Hua Lu with Qi Sha (the "General Star") or Po Jun (the "Destroyer Star") means a strong-willed, rebellious child. The bond is deep; the arguments are real.

Always check the opposite palace, which is the Property Palace. If the Children Palace has Hua Lu but the Property Palace holds challenging stars or Hua Ji (the "Obstacle Star"), the child is fine but the home environment — moving, renovations, fights over space — creates stress around them. If the Children Palace is empty, do not assume a weak bond. You must borrow stars from the opposite Property Palace and check the triads. If the borrowed stars are bright and supported, the parent-child bond is strong even with no major star sitting in the palace itself.

Creations and Students: What You Make Finds an Audience

The second line of the Children Palace is creative and intellectual output — your work, your teaching, the people you mentor. Hua Lu here means what you produce tends to be received well.

Hua Lu with Wen Chang or Wen Qu (the "Scholar Stars") gives your writing and ideas reach. People share what you make. Hua Lu with Tian Ji (the "Advisor Star") suits clever, technical, or ingenious products. Hua Lu with Tai Yang (the "Sun Star") or Ju Men (the "Great Speaker") is a teaching placement — students come to you, and the ones you teach tend to do well.

A concrete pattern: Children Palace has Tai Yang with Hua Lu and Wen Chang, with Tian Liang in the triads. Tai Yang broadcasts, Wen Chang writes, Tian Liang teaches. This is someone whose courses, articles, or mentorship produce visible results and income.

Extended Wealth: Will the Money You Put Out Come Back?

The third line is what the classical texts call "extended wealth" — money you've handed off: investments, shares in someone else's business, a partnership where you're not the one running it daily. The Children Palace is the "property palace" of the Friends Palace, meaning it's the foundation underneath a partnership. Hua Lu here suggests your stake tends to grow.

But extended wealth behaves differently from direct income. Hua Lu with Lu Cun (the "Storage Star") means the investment returns and holds. Hua Lu with Di Kong or Di Jie (the "Emptiness Stars") means you invest with confidence and watch it evaporate. Hua Lu with Qing Yang (the "Sharpening Star") means there's a return, but you fight for it — disputes, delays, possibly legal action to get what you're owed.

The pattern to watch most carefully is Hua Lu and Hua Ji in the same palace, or Hua Ji directly opposite. Lu and Ji together in the Children Palace means "first gain, then loss" — the child delights you and then worries you, the product launches well and then gets panned, the investment pays early and then reverses. If you see this, take profit at the high point. Don't ride it down.

In a Current Year: Romance, Partnerships, and New Projects

When the Children Palace activates in a given year, there are three separate triggers. Don't lump them together.

First: the yearly Life Palace or a yearly romance star lands in the Children Palace. This spikes romance and, potentially, pregnancy. But romance and children are not the same event. A Hong Luan star points to a relationship; the Children Palace itself points to children. Check the yearly Spouse Palace to tell whether this is a marriage year or a baby year.

Second: a yearly Hua Lu flies into the Children Palace. Something you've put out bears fruit that year. Which line it hits depends on where the Lu comes from. If it flies from the Career Palace, it's a new project or product paying off. If from the Wealth Palace, it's investment income. If from the Spouse Palace, the relationship may lead to children.

Third: a ten-year Children Palace carries Hua Lu. Over the decade, what you build compounds — a business, a body of work, a team you've trained. Ten-year Lu is slow. It won't explode in year one. Keep producing; the back half of the decade is where the results show up.

Reading Order After You Cast the Chart

When you find Hua Lu in the Children Palace, read it in this order:

  1. Decide which line is active — children, creations, or extended wealth — based on the main star and the triads.
  2. Check the opposite Property Palace for home-environment factors.
  3. If the palace is empty, borrow from the opposite palace and triads before judging.
  4. Look for Lu Cun, Kong-Jie, and Qing Yang to see whether returns actually land.
  5. For timing, separate romance stars (relationships), yearly Lu flying in (a specific payoff), and ten-year Lu (slow compounding).

Two Charts with Different Decade Ages: First Check Virtual Age, Five-Element Phase, and Direction

How starting age, five-element phase, and clockwise or counterclockwise direction explain the difference between two charts before reading decade content.

Zi Wei Dou Shu2026-11-17 10:48

When comparing the decade phases of two charts, the same age sometimes lands in different palaces and the starting age differs. This is often not an error but a difference in starting age, the five-element phase, and whether a male or female chart moves clockwise or counterclockwise. Checking these items first before reading the decade keeps the two charts from becoming confused.

What decides the decade start

The age at which a decade phase starts relates to the five-element phase, such as water two, wood three, metal four, earth five, and fire six. Whether it moves clockwise or counterclockwise is decided by the yin-yang of the birth year and gender. When these conditions differ between two charts, the starting age and direction naturally differ, so each item must be checked rather than applying one chart's rule to the other.

Step-by-step starting check

  1. Confirm the five-element phase of each chart and check their starting ages.
  2. Confirm the yin-yang of the birth stem and gender to judge the direction.
  3. From the starting palace, arrange each ten-year decade palace by direction.
  4. Match ages to concrete palaces and check why the two charts differ.
  5. Once start, phase, and direction are correct, read the decade transformations and content.

Set the skeleton before discussing content

If the decade ages and palaces are wrong, the later transformations and annual readings are wrong with them. Clearing these basic items first makes the conclusion reliable and avoids treating normal chart differences as errors.

Chart reading order

  1. Fix the two charts and confirm each five-element phase.
  2. Check starting age, then direction.
  3. Arrange the ten-year decades by direction and match ages.
  4. Once the skeleton is correct, read the decade transformations.
  5. Keep the conclusion conditional rather than mixing the two charts' rules.

A Crowded Palace: What Should You Read First?

In a crowded palace, read the main star first, then the transformations, then supporting stars, and finally the difficult stars as cost and risk.

Zi Wei Dou Shu2026-08-19 16:44

A chart area can contain a main star, several supporting stars, one or more Four Transformations, and difficult stars at the same time. Reading whichever symbol looks most favorable or frightening produces a contradictory answer: excellent prospects in one sentence and disaster in the next.

A palace is simply the chart area assigned to a life topic such as career, money, partners, or family. Once that topic and its subject are clear, sort the symbols by function. The main star explains how the situation works. The Four Transformations mark the strongest emphasis. Supporting stars add tools or access, while difficult stars identify where the process can fail.

The main star names the topic before anything else

Start with the question and the palace. A Hua Quan in the Life Palace begins with the owner's style, while Hua Quan in Spouse begins with the partner and Hua Quan in Career begins with formal responsibility. The same symbol cannot keep the same subject after it moves.

Next write the operating pattern from the main star or main-star pair. Mark Hua Ke, Hua Quan, Hua Lu, and Hua Ji immediately because they show recognized skill, authority, resources, and the central obstacle. They are important, but they still modify a specific person, process, and life area.

Transformations highlight the active emphasis

Wen Chang and Wen Qu can add study, writing, expression, or creative skill. Tian Kui and Tian Yue can add educational opportunity, recognition, or timely assistance. Zuo Fu and You Bi can provide practical support. Name the help and connect it to the main process instead of announcing a vague benefactor.

Example 1: Po Jun, Tian Yue, and Wen Qu with Hua Ke in the Wealth Palace point first to earning through a recognized skill or body of work. Po Jun adds movement, sustained effort, and a heavier operating cost, while Tian Yue adds access or help. Check both what clients pay for and how much travel, labor, coordination, and delivery the income requires.

Supporting stars add tools while difficult stars add cost

Qing Yang, Tuo Luo, Fire, Bell, Kong, and Jie do not erase the main pattern. They show friction, delay, conflict, sudden cost, or missing support. Their palace placement and strength determine what deserves attention and how hard it may be to manage.

Example 2: Tian Ji and Tian Ma in the Friends Palace describe information and movement through other people. Financial support may help, while Ju Men with Hua Ji or other difficult stars still warn about scope, pricing, and settlement disputes. Record the client source and the dispute point separately.

Practical Reading Order

Name the question, palace, and person first. Read the main star or star pair as the operating pattern, then mark Hua Ke, Hua Quan, Hua Lu, and Hua Ji as the strongest emphasis. Add supporting stars as specific tools or access and difficult stars as specific failure points. Finish with strength, connected palaces, and real evidence such as duties, payment, contracts, and account control.

How to Derive Annual Four Transformations in Zi Wei Dou Shu: Where This Year's Lu-Quan-Ke-Ji Come From and How to Layer Them

Annual transformations follow the year stem; as the stem changes, Lu/Quan/Ke/Ji land on different stars. It is not a separate chart but the current-year fuse layered over natal and decade transformations—all three must be read together.

Zi Wei Dou Shu2026-09-09

Many remember only natal transformations and do not know where the year's come from, so they bet the whole year on its Life star. What actually ignites a matter this year is usually which palace the annual transformations fly into.

What This Question Is Really Asking

Asking whether something has a chance this year and where the block is means first deriving the year's transformations from its stem, then seeing which natal palaces they enter and how they meet the natal and decade sets.

The Underlying Logic

Natal transformations from the birth-year stem set lifelong themes; decade transformations from each ten-year palace stem set its main line; annual transformations from the current-year stem set this year's trigger—a baseline-stage-fuse relationship.

To derive them, look up the year stem's table (e.g. a Jia year gives Lian Zhen Lu, Po Jun Quan, Wu Qu Ke, Tai Yang Ji), then see which natal palaces hold those four stars—those are this year's landing points.

In layering, annual Lu landing in a palace already strong natively or in the decade means good made real this year; annual Ji landing in a natively stressed palace means an old issue ignited this year—the verdict depends on the landing's foundation.

Watch flight direction: the palace annual Ji flies into is this year's sensitive, sticky point; where annual Lu flies carries resources and chance; Quan drives and competes; Ke brings reputation, smoothing and document-related help.

A Step-by-Step Way to Read It

  1. Find the stem of the year in question and its four transformed stars
  2. On the natal chart, locate which palaces hold those four stars and mark them as this year's Lu/Quan/Ke/Ji positions
  3. Mark natal and current-decade transformations too, checking whether all three share a palace or clash across
  4. Read annual Lu and Ke positions for chance and help, Quan for drive and competition
  5. Focus on annual Ji: whichever palace it flies into is where things stick most this year
  6. Conclude from all three layers before setting pace, rather than judging from the year Life star alone

Three Common Mistakes

Mistake 1: Not knowing annual transformations follow the year stem and reusing natal ones every year

Mistake 2: Reading only the year Life star while missing that where annual transformations fly is the real fuse

Mistake 3: Separating the three layers and declaring a great year from one annual Lu despite a weak natal base

Back to Your Own Chart

Annual transformations are the fuse that ignites what the chart already holds in a given year. Find this year stem's Lu/Quan/Ke/Ji and layer them over natal and decade, and this year's chances and blocks become concrete.

Reading Order

  1. Cast the natal chart first and locate the palaces and major stars tied to how annual transformations are derived and layered before judging anything
  2. Open the triple-direction of those palaces and count the auspicious, malefic and assistant stars separately
  3. Check where the natal Four Transformations land to set the chart's baseline tone
  4. Read the decade-limit transformations to see whether the how annual transformations are derived and layered line is activated in this stage
  5. Then read the annual transformations and the year's palace to pin down this year's trigger and timing
  6. Layer natal, decade and year before concluding, then decide how to act rather than labeling good or bad

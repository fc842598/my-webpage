What "Resetting the Taiji" Means in Zi Wei Dou Shu: Re-Centering on Another Palace Changes the Whole View

To examine a matter, treat its palace as a new Life Palace and re-map the twelve around it; centering on the Career palace, for example, reveals that matter's own resources, process and outcome.

Zi Wei Dou Shu2026-09-04

The Life Palace is only the self-centered view; resetting the Taiji points the camera at the matter itself and reads its own twelve-palace relations.

What This Question Is Really Asking

Beginners route every question back to the Life Palace and explain career or love only as personality, missing the matter's own chain of cause and effect.

The Underlying Logic

The twelve palaces are relative relations; whichever you treat as Life redefines the rest—that is resetting the Taiji.

For career, center on Career: its wealth becomes Friends (clients and team), its career becomes Life (you), its karma becomes Wealth (monetized satisfaction).

For relationships, center on the Spouse palace to read that relationship's own resources, pressure and environment.

Resetting is not casting a new chart but re-centering the same chart to dig into one specific matter.

A Step-by-Step Way to Read It

  1. Decide which matter to dig into and lock its palace
  2. Treat that palace as the new Life Palace, the Taiji point
  3. Recount its sibling, spouse, wealth and career palaces at fixed intervals
  4. Read the matter's own wealth (resources), career (process) and karma (felt outcome)
  5. Return to the original Life view and layer self over the matter
  6. Conclude only when the two layers agree; their mismatch is often the tension and the opening

Three Common Mistakes

Mistake 1: Explaining everything from the Life Palace, unable to go deep on a matter

Mistake 2: Counting palace intervals wrong when re-centering and mismatching relation palaces

Mistake 3: Treating a reset as a separate chart instead of layering it over the natal chart

Back to Your Own Chart

Resetting the Taiji is the key move from who you are to how a matter works; learning to re-center often dissolves a stuck question at once.

Reading Order

  1. Cast the natal chart first and locate the palaces and major stars tied to resetting the Taiji (re-centering) before judging anything
  2. Open the triple-direction of those palaces and count the auspicious, malefic and assistant stars separately
  3. Check where the natal Four Transformations land to set the chart's baseline tone
  4. Read the decade-limit transformations to see whether the resetting the Taiji (re-centering) line is activated in this stage
  5. Then read the annual transformations and the year's palace to pin down this year's trigger and timing
  6. Layer natal, decade and year before concluding, then decide how to act rather than labeling good or bad

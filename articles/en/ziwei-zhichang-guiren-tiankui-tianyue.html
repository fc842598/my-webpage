Tian Kui and Tian Yue: Recognizing Practical Support at Work

Tian Kui and Tian Yue are supporting stars traditionally associated with help and introductions. Read them in context without assuming a helper's gender or status.

Zi Wei Dou Shu2026-09-24Updated 2026-11-06 15:10 (UTC+8)

Tian Kui and Tian Yue are often called benefactor stars. In ordinary English, discuss help, introductions, mentoring, or access to an opportunity. Their presence does not guarantee that someone will arrive to solve a problem.

Do Not Guess Who the Helper Must Be

Traditional yin-and-yang descriptions should not be used to identify a helper's gender, rank, or motives. Nor is one star's effect a measured half of a pair's effect. Ask who actually provides advice, access, feedback, or practical assistance.

Read the Palace Context

In the Career Palace, discuss work-related support; in the Friends Palace, peers and collaborators; in the Migration Palace, connections beyond an established circle. Read the major stars and transformations too. The placement does not verify a promised referral or a favorable offer.

Separate an Opening From an Outcome

An introduction is not the same as demonstrated skill, a completed application, or a confirmed role. Compare the reading with what the person can show and follow through on. A useful next question is what help is needed and whether the expectations around that help are clear.

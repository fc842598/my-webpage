Lu and Ji in the Same Palace: Revenue on Paper Does Not Mean Cash Collected

When Lu and Ji share a palace, opportunity and obstruction occupy the same channel. Lu points to resources, demand, or income; Hua Ji points to delay, entanglement, or a cost that keeps returning. The pattern often fits businesses that look busy on paper while cash remains tied up in receivables, inventory, disputes, or unclear profit sharing.

The last mile of money matters

A signed order is not collected cash, and revenue is not net profit. In Zi Wei Dou Shu, the palace tells us where this tension occurs. In Wealth it can concern payment and liquidity; in Friends it can concern partners and allocation; in Travel it can concern distant clients, logistics, or unfamiliar rules. The combination is conditional, not a prediction that every transaction will fail.

Two combinations with different business risks

  • Hua Lu and Hua Ji together in the Wealth Palace may show plenty of orders but slow final payments, contested invoices, or margins reduced by delivery costs. A strong Career Palace can still produce good work, yet the cash cycle needs deposits and milestones.
  • Lu and Ji on the Friends or Spouse line may bring opportunity through a partner, spouse, or familiar contact while creating ambiguity over ownership and responsibility. The relationship can remain valuable if capital, labor, guarantees, and exit terms are written down.

If Lu is supported by Career and Ji is softened by clear auxiliary stars, the issue may be manageable delay rather than loss. If Kong Jie or further Ji pressure the same axis, leverage and long payment terms deserve more caution.

Chart-reading order

Locate Lu and Ji, then name the real-life channel: income, partnership, property, or outside market. Check Wealth for liquidity, Career for delivery capacity, and Friends or Spouse for shared obligations. Next compare natal structure with the decade and annual trigger to see whether the issue is persistent or temporary. The practical reading ends with controls: deposits, short billing cycles, written scopes, credit limits, and a distinction between booked revenue and banked cash.

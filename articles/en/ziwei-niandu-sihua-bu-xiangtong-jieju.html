The Annual Transformations Belong to Everyone: Why They Are Not the Same Ending for All

Why the shared transformations are only a background, and how landing palaces, the decade phase, and personal choices produce different outcomes.

Zi Wei Dou Shu2026-11-17 10:09

The 2027 transformations are the same set for everyone reading the year, which can mislead people into thinking everyone has the same year. But the shared transformations are only a background, and what truly decides personal experience is the palace they land in on your chart and how they interact with your natal structure. Separating the shared background from the personal ending keeps the reading from becoming rigid.

The difference between shared and personal

The annual transformations describe the overall atmosphere and theme of the year, in which everyone takes part. But the same Hua Ji lands in a different palace on your chart than on another's, and triggers different things. With natal stars, the decade phase, and personal choices added, the same transformation shows entirely different concrete stories.

Step-by-step separate reading

  1. First accept that the annual transformations are a shared background, not personal property.
  2. Locate which palace each transformation lands in on your chart.
  3. Use the landing-palace function to judge the topic triggered for you this year.
  4. Then overlay natal stars and the current decade phase to see how the background acts on you.
  5. Separate "shared theme" and "personal performance" rather than judging the ending directly.

The ending is not fixed

The transformations give tendencies and directions where effort is likely, not fixed results. Personal choices, actions, and real arrangements still change the concrete path. Keeping this boundary prevents annual reading from becoming a fatalistic assertion.

Chart reading order

  1. Fix the chart and mark the landing palaces of the annual transformations.
  2. Understand the shared background first, then read the personal palaces.
  3. Judge through the overlay of natal and decade phases.
  4. Keep the conclusion conditional with room to adjust.
  5. Do not read the shared transformations as the same ending.

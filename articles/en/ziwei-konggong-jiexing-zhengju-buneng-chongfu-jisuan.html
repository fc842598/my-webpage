Borrowed Stars in an Empty Palace: Don't Count Them Twice in Zi Wei Dou Shu

When a palace holds no major star, you may reference the opposite palace's star for structure, but it is only a reference. Treating it as a resident star and weighing it again doubles one star and inflates the verdict.

Zi Wei Dou Shu2026-09-26

People panic at an empty palace, then borrow the opposite star and count it in both places, so one star does the work of two.

What This Question Is Really Asking

Separate two kinds of evidence: what the palace itself holds and receives is direct, while the opposite star is an indirect reference across the axis, weighted less and never double-booked.

The Underlying Logic

An empty palace holds no major star but is not void; its assistant stars, malefics and transformations still act there.

Borrowing the opposite star gives the palace a structural reference for the force that pulls the matter.

A borrowed star is not a resident one: it is already counted in its own palace and only earns partial reference weight here.

List the palace's own evidence first, note the opposite reference separately, and no single star gets magnified.

A Step-by-Step Way to Read It

  1. Confirm the palace truly has no major star without overlooking assistant stars
  2. Find the opposite palace's major star as a structural reference
  3. List the palace's own assistant stars, malefics and transformations as direct evidence
  4. Mark the borrowed star as a partial reference rather than counting it again
  5. Open the palace triple-direction for the net effect instead of relying on one borrowed star
  6. When concluding, state clearly which evidence is native and which is borrowed

Three Common Mistakes

Mistake 1: Treating an empty palace as void and refusing to read it

Mistake 2: Taking the borrowed star as resident and double-weighting it

Mistake 3: Concluding from one borrowed star while ignoring the palace's own stars

Back to Your Own Chart

Borrowing is a ruler for understanding an empty palace, not a license to count a star twice. Keep the evidence distinct and an empty palace reads just as clearly.

Reading Order

  1. Cast the natal chart first and locate the palaces and major stars tied to borrowed stars in an empty palace before judging anything
  2. Open the triple-direction of those palaces and count the auspicious, malefic and assistant stars separately
  3. Check where the natal Four Transformations land to set the chart's baseline tone
  4. Read the decade-limit transformations to see whether the borrowed stars in an empty palace line is activated in this stage
  5. Then read the annual transformations and the year's palace to pin down this year's trigger and timing
  6. Layer natal, decade and year before concluding, then decide how to act rather than labeling good or bad

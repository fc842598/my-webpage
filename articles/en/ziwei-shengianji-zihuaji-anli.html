Natal Hua Ji Versus Self-Transformed Hua Ji in Zi Wei Dou Shu: Different Kinds of Attachment in the Same Palace

Natal Hua Ji is a lesson placed at birth and carried for life, while a self-transformed Hua Ji means the palace itself leaks or backtracks at the last moment. Sharing one palace is not double blockage; tell which part is innate and which part is self-inflicted.

Zi Wei Dou Shu2026-09-28

Many panic when a natal Hua Ji shares a palace with a self-transformed Hua Ji, assuming that palace is ruined. The two Ji are not the same: one is an innate debt, the other is backing out at the last minute.

What This Question Is Really Asking

What the question really asks: natal Hua Ji asks why this matter is inherently hard to let go, while self-transformed Hua Ji asks why it repeatedly falls apart at your own hand. The first you face; the second you watch yourself doing.

The Underlying Logic

Natal Hua Ji is fixed by the birth year stem and lands in a palace from birth, marking a lifelong recurring lesson in that area.

Self-transformed Hua Ji arises within a palace itself, meaning its energy leaks out and returns: plans change, doubts surface, one deflates at the finish line.

Natal Ji plus self-Ji in one palace is not double blockage; it layers a self-sabotaging habit on top of an innate lesson.

Ask first whether the friction in that palace is a built-in lesson or a habit of letting go at the last step.

A Step-by-Step Way to Read It

  1. Find the natal Hua Ji palace from the birth year stem, the innate lesson's position
  2. Scan every palace for self-transformed Hua Ji, the habit-pattern positions
  3. Flag overlaps and ask which kind of friction recurs there
  4. Read the natal Ji's triple-direction to see which area carries the lesson
  5. Check whether self-Ji matters tend to fail at the last step
  6. Conclude separately: face natal Ji over the long run, observe self-Ji in the moment

Three Common Mistakes

Mistake 1: Reading natal Ji plus self-Ji as simply double misfortune

Mistake 2: Blaming fate for every Ji without asking whether you back out at the finish

Mistake 3: Blaming outside rivals for self-Ji while missing that the palace leaks itself

Back to Your Own Chart

Ji on the chart is not a verdict but a reminder. Natal Ji tells you which lesson lasts a lifetime; self-Ji warns you not to let go just before the result arrives. Tell which is which, and you know whether to work on fate or on yourself.

Reading Order

  1. Cast the natal chart first and locate the palaces and major stars tied to natal Hua Ji vs self-transformed Hua Ji cases before judging anything
  2. Open the triple-direction of those palaces and count the auspicious, malefic and assistant stars separately
  3. Check where the natal Four Transformations land to set the chart's baseline tone
  4. Read the decade-limit transformations to see whether the natal Hua Ji vs self-transformed Hua Ji cases line is activated in this stage
  5. Then read the annual transformations and the year's palace to pin down this year's trigger and timing
  6. Layer natal, decade and year before concluding, then decide how to act rather than labeling good or bad

Wu Qu and Tian Fu in the Spouse Palace: How to Discuss the Financial Execution Division

How the practical, steady stars support clear roles for accounting, payments, savings, and large expenses, with shared decisions and regular review.

Zi Wei Dou Shu2026-11-17 10:39

Wu Qu and Tian Fu are both practical, steady stars related to resource management. In the Spouse Palace they often indicate that both sides are serious about money and suited to discussing the financial execution division. But "can manage money" does not mean the division is naturally clear, and concrete discussion is still needed to avoid one side silently carrying everything while the other is unaware.

The financial nature of this star pair

Wu Qu leans toward execution, decisions, and practicality, while Tian Fu leans toward storage, steadiness, and coordination. In the Spouse Palace they often show a reliable, prudent partner in money. In reality, this practical nature can be used to clarify who handles accounting, who pays bills, and how large expenses are decided.

Step-by-step division discussion

  1. Confirm Wu Qu and Tian Fu in the Spouse Palace and understand their practical, steady, managing nature.
  2. Split family finance into daily costs, accounting, payments, savings, and large expenses.
  3. Discuss who is responsible for each item and how it is checked, naming a concrete person.
  4. Agree that large expenses are decided together and that accounts are reviewed regularly.
  5. Combine the transformations to see blockages or resources, then write the division clearly rather than relying on "they can manage."

The division needs communication

However skilled at money, both sides should stay informed and involved to avoid one person carrying everything or information asymmetry. Setting the division and reconciliation mechanism lets the practical advantage truly work.

Chart reading order

  1. Fix the chart and confirm Wu Qu, Tian Fu, and the transformations.
  2. Split financial matters first, then assign each item to a person.
  3. Large expenses are shared decisions, with regular reconciliation.
  4. Check blockages through the transformations.
  5. Keep the conclusion conditional rather than treating "good at managing" as "already divided."

On a shared phone, the first privacy risk is usually what the next person can casually see, not the deepest technical possibility.

That is why local traces such as autofill, recent pages, screenshots, and history deserve your attention first.

See which layer the data lives in first

Shared-device risk is mostly about visible leftovers.

Autofill, recent views, gallery images, and search history are the usual weak spots.

Questions like "If You Try AI Fortune Telling Once on a Shared Phone, What Should You Clear First" make more sense when you separate the account layer, the device layer, and the contact layer first.

How the common risks actually show up

A saved email or birthplace can appear as soon as someone taps the form field.

A chart screenshot can still sit in the album even after the page is closed.

Reading the public rule, then checking local traces, then checking the support path is usually more useful than obsessing over one button alone.

Do not treat vague wording as safety

Cleanup usually does not require advanced steps to be useful.

Still, the safest long-term move is to avoid leaving full records on devices you do not control.

Safety cannot live only in a feeling. It needs a clear account, device, record, and contact path.

A steadier handling order

  1. Log out, then clear screenshots and deleted photos.
  2. Clear history, autofill, and recent page traces.
  3. Use your own device for later verification rounds.

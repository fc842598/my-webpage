There is no universal rule for chart-first versus login-first. The right order depends on whether you are just testing or preparing for ongoing use.

Testing users usually benefit from chart first. Continuity users usually benefit from login first.

What matters first

Chart first separates product value from account commitment.

Login first improves record, order, and membership carry-over.

The more useful answer usually explains why it is reading the chart that way, what to verify next, and where not to over-claim.

A practical scenario

A one-time structure check fits chart-first.

A same-theme tracking session fits login-first.

When a reading lands on chart structure, a real-life scenario, and a next verification step at the same time, it becomes much easier to judge whether it is actually useful.

The boundary to remember

Trial mode does not need full account setup too early.

Long-term use should not pretend guest mode is enough forever.

Whether the layer is free or paid, a safer product lets you verify in a small scope before asking for deeper commitment.

A safer order

  1. Decide whether you are testing or tracking.
  2. Use chart-first for testing and login-first for continuity.
  3. Do not judge both flows by the same goal.

What to Do When the Birth Hour Is Uncertain: Correcting It with Multiple Clues

The birth hour should be accurate to about half an hour; one shichen off shifts all twelve palaces. When it is unclear, combine the hair whorl, face shape, sibling-birth order and past events, then confirm by chart verification.

Many know only that they were born around evening, and casting from such a vague hour is risky. Fortunately, several supporting clues can narrow it down.

What This Question Is Really Asking

The hour cannot be guessed. No single clue is enough; cross physical traits, birth order and past events so several evidence types point to the same hour.

The Underlying Logic

One shichen spans two hours; one shift off moves the Life Palace and all twelve positions, changing the verdict.

Supporting clues include the hair whorl position and count, the shape of the face, the fingers and toes, and sibling-birth order.

Then reverse-check the hour using the timing of schooling, moves, job changes and marriage.

Several clue types pointing to one hour, confirmed by verification, make the correction accurate.

A Step-by-Step Way to Read It

  1. Gather the known birth-time range as precisely as possible
  2. Use the hair whorl, face shape and fingers or toes to narrow it
  3. Reference the sibling-birth order clues
  4. Reverse-check candidate hours using several past events
  5. Cross the clue types and take the hour they all point to
  6. Cast from the corrected hour and confirm by chart verification

Three Common Mistakes

Mistake 1: Guessing an hour from vague information and casting at once

Mistake 2: Fixing the hour from a single clue such as the hair whorl or birth order

Mistake 3: Skipping past-event checks so the correction cannot be confirmed

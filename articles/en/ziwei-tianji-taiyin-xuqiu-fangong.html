Tian Ji and Tai Yin When Requirements Keep Changing

Set a revision boundary without treating a star pair as a personality verdict.

Zi Wei Dou Shu2026-10-30 09:07

Tian Ji with Tai Yin may invite a discussion about careful planning, but it does not explain every revision. A new legal condition or user test warrants a change; repeated preference shifts without new evidence call for a decision maker to set priorities. Note the palace and transformations, then agree on scope, approval, and a freeze date. Judge the outcome from delivery and feedback.

Tian Ji can frame changing plans and Tai Yin attention to detail, but the pair does not cause endless revisions. A client changing scope without budget differs from a team rewriting copy without an owner. Track who requested each change, its cost, and the decision deadline.

Qi Sha and Po Jun in Annual Timing: Effort, Movement, and Wear Come First

A practical annual-reading guide for Qi Sha and Po Jun years, where pressure, movement, rework, and cost often appear before visible reward.

Zi Wei Dou Shu2026-07-26 23:23

A Qi Sha or Po Jun year often feels active long before it feels easy.

What This Means

This timing tends to push action, change, disruption, and expenditure of force. The person may get more done or move faster, but the cost in effort, instability, and wear usually appears first.

How To Read It

Read the active palace, then compare Career, Travel, and Health pressure. Ask what must be cut, rebuilt, or carried, and whether the year's movement creates meaningful progress or only rough expenditure.

Simple Examples

  • Read the star through the palace and the real-life role it points to, rather than using a vague fixed prediction.
  • When the travel palace is involved, outside platforms, clients, or new environments matter more.
  • A year of restructuring can create future value while still feeling exhausting in the moment.

Practical Order

First define the question. Then read the palace, its opposite palace, the supporting palaces, and the ten-year or annual trigger. This keeps the reading useful for career, money, relationships, and real choices.

When Natal, Decade and Annual Transformations Conflict: Read in Layers, Not Evenly

When natal Lu meets a decade Ji and an annual Lu, the conflicting signals confuse. The key is layering: natal transformations set the lifelong tone, decade transformations set the ten-year stage and annual transformations only set the year trigger, with different weight rather than an even average.

Zi Wei Dou Shu2026-10-05

The three layers are like three levels of decisions: the board sets direction (natal), the department sets the stage (decade) and the duty shift sets temporary arrangements (year), which cannot overturn the main direction.

What This Question Is Really Asking

Some see an annual Hua Lu and expect a turnaround, ignoring a decade Ji and an unsupporting natal, then miss; the issue is not contradiction but treating the three layers as equally weighted.

The Underlying Logic

Natal transformations are part of the natal structure, setting the lifelong tone and main line with the highest, steadiest weight.

Decade transformations govern the ten-year stage, adding and subtracting on the natal baseline to activate or press certain lines.

Annual transformations govern only the year trigger and rhythm for a short time and cannot overturn the natal and decade main line.

When layers conflict, judge in the order of natal direction, decade stage and annual timing, then decide how to act.

A Step-by-Step Way to Read It

  1. First list the natal transformation palaces to set the lifelong tone
  2. Then read the current decade transformations to set whether the ten years help or press
  3. Then read annual transformations only as the year trigger
  4. When layers conflict, judge by weight rather than being led by the year alone
  5. Turn the conclusion into timing: what to do this decade and what this year
  6. In review, see which judgments respected layering and which short signals skewed

Three Common Mistakes

Mistake 1: Using the annual layer to overturn the natal and decade main line

Mistake 2: Treating the three layers as equally weighted and getting lost in many signals

Mistake 3: Calling the chart inaccurate at conflicting signals without understanding time layers

Back to Your Own Chart

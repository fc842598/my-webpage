Project Cooperation Conflicts: What the Children Palace Can and Cannot Show

How the palace only covers project nurturing and interaction in a cooperation, while funding, decisions, and exit terms need other palaces and the contract.

Zi Wei Dou Shu2026-11-17 09:21

When cooperating on a project, duties, division of work, and who decides are often the most tense points. Some people try to judge the whole cooperation from the Children Palace, but it only shows part, such as the personally nurtured project and the way they interact with juniors and partners. It cannot answer every cooperation question.

What the Children Palace can show

The palace symbolizes the project you personally build and the interaction with juniors, students, and partners. It indicates how you tend to invest and guide, and the rhythm likely to appear in cooperation. Yet this is information about interaction and output, not the full basis for success or failure.

Step-by-step cooperation check

  1. Split the cooperation question into funding, division of work, decision rights, and payment collection.
  2. Use the Children Palace for project nurturing and interaction rhythm, answering only that part.
  3. Return to the Sibling and Friends palaces for the relationship and trust.
  4. Use the Wealth and Career palaces to check funding, duties, and profit sharing.
  5. Judge after placing the palaces side by side rather than declaring failure from tense stars.

Where not to cross the line

Tense stars in the Children Palace only mean possible friction in interaction or nurturing, not that cooperation must fail. Decision rights, funding ratios, and exit mechanisms need the contract and other palaces together. Keeping this palace's limits prevents an over-broad conclusion.

Chart reading order

  1. Fix the chart and Children Palace, knowing it only covers project and interaction.
  2. Split the cooperation question, then let each palace answer separately.
  3. The Children Palace shows rhythm, Friends and Sibling show relationship, Wealth and Career show money and duties.
  4. Check real contract terms and funding evidence separately.
  5. Keep the conclusion conditional rather than treating one palace as the cooperation result.

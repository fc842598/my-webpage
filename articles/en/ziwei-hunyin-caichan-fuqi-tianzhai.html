Marital Property in Zi Wei Dou Shu: Spouse and Tian Zhai Palace Relations

Marriage is not only an emotional union but also a financial union. From the chart perspective, the combination of Spouse Palace (partner relationship) and Tian Zhai Palace (family assets) reflects property patterns in marriage—joint or separate, whether property brings closeness or conflict. Understanding this helps protect both the relationship and rights in marriage.

Zi Wei Dou Shu2026-09-24

Chart patterns of marital property: first, "joint accumulation type"—Spouse Palace Prosperity + Tian Zhai Palace Prosperity, both willing to contribute to family, jointly buy houses and save, the most stable property structure. Second, "one-party dominant type"—Spouse Palace Authority + Tian Zhai Palace Jealousy, one party controls family finances, the other has no say in property, easily causes conflict. Third, "separate independent type"—Spouse Palace Di Kong/Di Jie + Tian Zhai Palace malefics, clear boundaries in property, each earns and spends separately, suitable for economic independence but relationship may be more distant. Fourth, "property dispute type"—Spouse Palace Jealousy + Tian Zhai Palace Qing Yang/Tuo Lo, marriage easily has arguments over property, even falling out over property division during divorce. Pre-marital property protection: first, "pre-marital property notarization"—if one party has significant pre-marital property (real estate, savings, equity), can do pre-marital property notarization to clarify ownership. Legal matters follow professional lawyers and正规 channels. Second, "do not add name to pre-marital house"—a house fully paid before marriage, without adding the partners name after marriage, remains personal property. Third, "do not commingle pre-marital and post-marital property"—do not transfer pre-marital savings into a post-marital joint account, otherwise may be deemed joint property. Post-marital property management: first, "joint account + personal accounts"—establish joint account for household expenses, personal accounts for personal spending, ensures family common interests while retaining personal space. Second, "joint decisions for large expenses"—buying house, car, large investments need both parties consent. Third, "do not guarantee large debts for the partner"—if partner has high-risk investments or business, do not easily sign guarantees. Fourth, "use insurance as property isolation tool"—life insurance and annuity insurance with designated beneficiaries can achieve property isolation to some extent. Property division during divorce: first, "understand legal provisions in advance"—what is joint property, what is personal property, law has clear provisions. Second, "keep property evidence"—bank statements, purchase contracts, proof of contribution should be saved. Third, "do not sign agreements when emotional"—during divorce easily signs unfair property division agreements due to anger or guilt, recommend signing after calming down or consulting a lawyer. What This Question Is Really Asking

Practical case: Someone with Prosperity in Wealth but Jealousy in Tian Zhai has good income but cannot save—earns much but spends much, or loses money through property or family expenses. The key is "income looks at Wealth, preservation looks at Tian Zhai"—both palaces must be read.

The Underlying Logic

Only looking at Wealth Palace without Tian Zhai, earning much but not preserving.

Equating Prosperity with "will definitely get rich," ignoring that Prosperity can also mean "more money more problems."

Not watching Decade/Annual wealth changes, making wrong investments at wrong times.

Treating chart financial tendencies as investment advice, without own research and risk assessment.

A Step-by-Step Way to Read It

  1. Chart the Wealth, Tian Zhai, and Fortune palaces major stars and transformations.
  2. See the Decade Wealth Palace state to judge the ten-year income trend.
  3. Distinguish between "regular income" (salary) and "windfall" (investment, side business) sources.
  4. Check Tian Zhai wealth-preservation ability, set savings and asset allocation plans.
  5. Take initiative in good wealth years, hold steady in bad wealth years.
  6. Regularly review financial status and adjust strategy based on actual conditions.

Three Common Mistakes

Mistake 1: The chart only indicates financial tendencies; it does not constitute investment advice.

Mistake 2: Good wealth years can still lose money from wrong decisions; bad wealth years can still preserve capital through caution.

Mistake 3: Financial content is based on licensed institutions and official product descriptions; investments carry risk.

Back to Your Own Chart

The core of wealth management is not "whether you have money in your destiny" but "whether you can preserve and grow what you earn." The chart helps you understand your financial personality and risk points; real wealth accumulation comes from consistent saving habits, reasonable asset allocation, and continuously improving earning ability.

Lu Follows Ji and Flying-Ji Out/In: Why Some Money Arrives and Leaves—Follow Hua Ji to Understand

Lu and Ji are a causal pair: where Lu gives, Ji collects—Lu follows Ji. Ji-in means energy gets stuck in a palace; Ji-out means a palace pushes its debt outward. Following where Lu comes from and Ji goes explains why gains and losses repeat.

Zi Wei Dou Shu2026-09-08

Reading Lu alone looks like net gain and misses the Ji behind it; Lu starts a matter, Ji closes it. Trace both to learn where the gain must be paid for.

What This Question Is Really Asking

People praise a Lu palace yet the benefit never stays. Ask who gives the Lu, where its paired Ji lands, whether Ji-in jams it or Ji-out pushes it away; without the full chain the verdict is half right.

The Underlying Logic

Lu follows Ji: in one transformation set Lu and Ji often pair—Lu is the entry of resource/opportunity/gain, Ji the exit of fixation/debt/cost; always find Ji with Lu to know the gain's cost and destination.

Ji-in: another palace's stem flies Ji into a palace, sticking energy and responsibility there—the matter is clung to, owed or repeatedly problematic; someone/something delivers the lesson here.

Ji-out (flying Ji): a palace's stem flies Ji elsewhere, pushing its own block/debt to the landing palace—I keep paying and worrying for that landing palace's people or matters.

Chain Lu's origin and Ji's route: Lu into Wealth with Ji into Property means earnings ultimately sink into home/family; Lu into Life with Ji-out to Friends means I gain yet keep paying for friends. A clear chain explains repeated gains and losses and grounds trade-offs.

A Step-by-Step Way to Read It

  1. Find every Hua Lu and locate the same-stem Hua Ji to pair them
  2. For each pair set direction: which palace Lu flies from and where Ji flies in/out
  3. Distinguish Ji-in (lesson sent in) from Ji-out (I push debt out) to fix responsibility
  4. Translate the Lu-Ji chain into people/matters: who benefits me and for whom I keep paying/worrying
  5. Check whether Hua Ke/Quan buffer or self-transformation cuts the chain, sizing the cost
  6. With decade/year, simultaneous activation of the chain is when gains and losses concentrate

Three Common Mistakes

Mistake 1: Praising Lu without its paired Ji, unable to explain why gains slip away

Mistake 2: Confusing Ji-in with Ji-out, reversing incoming lessons and outgoing debt

Mistake 3: Reading "Lu follows Ji" as fatalistic gain-loss instead of a tool to see costs and choose deliberately

Back to Your Own Chart

Lu follows Ji is not pessimism but clear-sighted cost vision. Pair every Lu and Ji on your chart into a line and you will see which gains are net and which merely pass through, focusing energy on the chain with the smallest cost that actually stays.

Reading Order

  1. Cast the natal chart first and locate the palaces and major stars tied to Lu follows Ji and flying-Ji out/in before judging anything
  2. Open the triple-direction of those palaces and count the auspicious, malefic and assistant stars separately
  3. Check where the natal Four Transformations land to set the chart's baseline tone
  4. Read the decade-limit transformations to see whether the Lu follows Ji and flying-Ji out/in line is activated in this stage
  5. Then read the annual transformations and the year's palace to pin down this year's trigger and timing
  6. Layer natal, decade and year before concluding, then decide how to act rather than labeling good or bad

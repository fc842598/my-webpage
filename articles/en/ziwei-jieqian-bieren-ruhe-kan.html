What to Do When Someone Asks to Borrow Money: Separate Goodwill From Rules, Lend Within Means and Write a Note

Before lending, separate urgent relief from chronic need or investment and assess what you can bear. Lending is goodwill while a written note and repayment terms are rules, and keeping them apart protects both the relationship and yourself.

Zi Wei Dou Shu2026-10-05

For lending, first ask whether you could accept never getting it back; if yes, talk goodwill, and if no, say so honestly, which beats forcing it and then falling out.

What This Question Is Really Asking

Some lend to spare face and feel awkward writing a note, then lose both money and friend; the issue is not a bad person but mixing goodwill and rules when the rules should have been named.

The Underlying Logic

First see the nature of the loan and the other side ability to repay; urgent relief can be helped while filling a chronic hole needs caution.

Lend within means: only what would not affect your life even if never returned.

Write the amount, repayment time and method clearly, relying on a written contract and proper channels when needed.

Separate goodwill from rules and remind gently when due rather than letting it become bad debt to spare face.

A Step-by-Step Way to Read It

  1. First ask the purpose, amount and repayment plan
  2. Assess what you can bear and only lend what will not affect your life
  3. If you lend, write a note with repayment time and method
  4. If you do not lend, explain honestly and politely without vague excuses
  5. When due, remind as agreed and keep transfer and communication records
  6. For large amounts or disputes, rely on a qualified lawyer and proper channels

Three Common Mistakes

Mistake 1: Lending beyond your means to spare face

Mistake 2: Skipping the note and feeling awkward to remind until it becomes bad debt

Mistake 3: Treating chronic need as urgent relief and pouring money into a bottomless hole

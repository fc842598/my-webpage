Zi Wei Dou Shu on Adjusting Excessive Liquidated Damages: Check Real Loss First

A contract may set a sum payable on breach, often corresponding to Hua Ji in the Wealth Palace and responsibility through the Career Palace. Liquidated damages mainly compensate real loss rather than being safer when set higher; if they are far above the loss, a court or arbitration may reduce them appropriately.

Zi Wei Dou Shu2026-10-30

The core of liquidated damages is checking real loss first and the agreed amount second; they are compensation-based, not a tool to punish the other side.

What This Question Is Really Asking

Some think a higher clause means better protection, or panic and pay in full when claimed under a high amount, without first calculating the actual loss.

The Underlying Logic

A liquidated-damages clause is often triggered when the Wealth Palace meets Hua Ji and the Career Palace pulls in responsibility, marking a sum owed after breach.

The damages are based on compensating real loss while considering performance and fault; if below the loss they may be raised, and if far above they may be reduced appropriately.

Whether they are excessive depends on real loss together with how far the contract was performed, expected benefits and each party’s fault.

When the Wealth Palace stars are steady and both loss and terms are documented, the dispute is easier to settle; with heavy malefics and Hua Ji, keep loss evidence and adjust through the written contract and legal procedure rather than paying everything.

A Step-by-Step Way to Read It

  1. Confirm whether the contract sets liquidated damages and how they are calculated
  2. Work out the actual loss from the breach and provable expected benefits
  3. Compare the agreed amount with the real loss to judge a clear imbalance
  4. Read the Wealth and Career Palaces to weigh responsibility and loss
  5. If the amount is far above the loss, prepare evidence and ask a court or arbitration to reduce it
  6. Keep the contract, loss evidence and communication records, with the legal ruling as the basis

Three Common Mistakes

Mistake 1: Thinking a higher clause is safer and ignoring its compensation basis

Mistake 2: Paying a high claim in full without checking the real loss

Mistake 3: Not keeping loss evidence, making adjustment impossible to prove

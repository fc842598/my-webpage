Jie Kong and Xun Kong Voids in Zi Wei Dou Shu: Stars That Discount Auspiciousness and Empty Outcomes

Jie Kong (including Xun Kong/Sky-void) governs sudden interruption, giving-up and falling-through; Xun Kong slow drain and looks-present-but-ungrabbable. These void stars discount co-located auspicious stars and derail plans, yet also buffer malefic/Ji harshness. Reading voids explains why good conditions still fall one step short.

Zi Wei Dou Shu2026-09-07

A void is a leak valve: solid turns hollow, full gets a gap—auspicious stars lose force while malefics soften; the key is its co-star and domain.

What This Question Is Really Asking

Auspicious-stacked palaces predicted to succeed fall through, often because a void leaks; feared malefics/Ji can be partly disarmed by the same void.

The Underlying Logic

Jie Kong (related to Di Kong, with Xun Kong/Sky-void): sudden interruption, abrupt stop, last-minute giving-up and plan change—fast.

Xun Kong: slow hollow drain, dragging fall-through, looks-present-but-ungrabbable—lingering.

Mechanism: with auspicious stars it discounts force and makes good things stumble at the finish; with malefics/Ji it buffers harshness—two-sided.

Response: for important matters under a void keep alternatives, avoid overpromising, put contracts in writing, and guard against sudden change and unfinished endings when activated.

A Step-by-Step Way to Read It

  1. Mark Jie Kong/Xun Kong palaces across the chart
  2. See whether it shares auspicious or malefic stars
  3. With auspicious stars: lower guaranteed-success expectations, keep alternatives
  4. With malefics/Ji: know harshness is buffered, no need to panic
  5. Put plans in writing with exits to guard the final-step letdown
  6. When a decade/year activates a void, slow major decisions a beat

Three Common Mistakes

Mistake 1: Guaranteeing success from auspicious stars, ignoring void leakage

Mistake 2: Taking every void as bad, unaware it softens malefics

Mistake 3: Overpromising under a void with no alternative

Back to Your Own Chart

Voids explain the missing final breath: they make good things stumble and bad things less absolute. Seeing where a void falls and with whom, you understand why sure things slip and how to close that gap ahead of time.

Reading Order

  1. Cast the natal chart first and locate the palaces and major stars tied to Jie Kong and Xun Kong voids before judging anything
  2. Open the triple-direction of those palaces and count the auspicious, malefic and assistant stars separately
  3. Check where the natal Four Transformations land to set the chart's baseline tone
  4. Read the decade-limit transformations to see whether the Jie Kong and Xun Kong voids line is activated in this stage
  5. Then read the annual transformations and the year's palace to pin down this year's trigger and timing
  6. Layer natal, decade and year before concluding, then decide how to act rather than labeling good or bad

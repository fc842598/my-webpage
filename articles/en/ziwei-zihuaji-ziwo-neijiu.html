Self-Jealousy in Zi Wei Dou Shu: From Self-Obsession to Deep Reflection

Self-Jealousy occurs when a palace stem flies Jealousy, meaning that palace actively turns inward, ruminates, and cannot let go. Unlike natal Jealousy, which is "a lesson given by external circumstances," Self-Jealousy is "you being hard on yourself." It makes thinking deep and perfection-seeking, but also easily leads to internal friction, regret, and self-doubt.

Zi Wei Dou Shu2026-09-24

The core of Self-Jealousy is "inward" and "repetitive." In palaces with Self-Jealousy, you unconsciously overthink, ruminate, and keep checking after finishing. Used in professional research, artistic creation, or quality control, Self-Jealousy is depth and refinement; used in relationships or past events, it is internal friction and regret. Palace-specific manifestations: Life Palace Self-Jealousy—prone to self-doubt, overthinking, repeatedly second-guessing decisions. Suitable for work requiring deep thought, but should practice "letting go after doing." Wealth Palace Self-Jealousy—ruminates about money, regrets spending and regrets not spending, easily delays financial decisions. Career Palace Self-Jealousy—demanding about work details, revises repeatedly, prone to overtime and perfectionism, but produces high quality. Spouse Palace Self-Jealousy—ruminates about partners words, easily overthinks and suspects, magnifies small issues. Fortune Palace Self-Jealousy—prone to racing thoughts, insomnia, emotional internal friction, needs deliberate relaxation training. Children Palace Self-Jealousy—excessively worries about children, repeated anxiety. The difference between Self-Jealousy and Self-Authority: Self-Authority is "grasping outward," Self-Jealousy is "drilling inward." One is tired from fighting the world, the other from fighting oneself. If a chart has much Self-Jealousy, the person is deep and sensitive, but also prone to unhappiness.

What This Question Is Really Asking

A typical case: Life Palace Self-Jealousy, with Authority in Career Palace. These people are sensitive and overthink (Self-Jealousy in Life), but are very controlling and executive at work (Authority in Career). They are often "strong at work, anxious in life"—can make decisive decisions at work, but after work repeatedly wonder if what they said was right or if they did something wrong. The breakthrough is "limiting Self-Jealousy to the work domain": use it for quality control and deep thinking at work, but deliberately switch modes after work—exercise, socializing, entertainment—to interrupt the thought loop. Another case: Spouse Palace Self-Jealousy. These people especially "overthink" in relationships—if the partner replies slowly, they guess if they are upset; one sentence can be pondered all night. The suitable approach is "direct communication instead of inner drama": ask when in doubt, express when dissatisfied, do not rehearse repeatedly in the mind. Partners also need to understand this is a personality trait, not distrust.

The Underlying Logic

Dismissing Self-Jealousy as "bad fortune," ignoring that it is the ability for deep thinking and perfection-seeking.

Not checking which palace Self-Jealousy falls in, assuming every aspect involves rumination.

Trying to suppress Self-Jealousy with "stop thinking," which makes it worse.

Attributing Self-Jealousy internal friction to external causes ("others make me unhappy"), ignoring ones own thinking patterns.

A Step-by-Step Way to Read It

  1. Locate palaces with Self-Jealousy and confirm whether it returns to self or flies to another palace.
  2. Observe the corresponding domain and whether there is "repeated rumination, still thinking after finishing."
  3. Channel Self-Jealousy energy into domains requiring depth and quality (profession, research, creation).
  4. In domains not requiring depth (daily小事, relationships), deliberately practice "letting go after doing."
  5. Build methods to interrupt thought loops: exercise, meditation, writing it down, talking to someone.
  6. During Decade or Annual periods activating Self-Jealousy, be especially careful not to "delay action due to overthinking."

Three Common Mistakes

Mistake 1: Self-Jealousy is not "cannot let go"; it is a manifestation of deep thinking, which when used correctly becomes a core competitive advantage.

Mistake 2: People with Self-Jealousy are not "unhappy"; their happiness requires more self-awareness and deliberate practice.

Mistake 3: When Self-Jealousy meets Prosperity, it is not "anguish then gain" but "finding the optimal solution through repeated deliberation."

Back to Your Own Chart

People with Self-Jealousy naturally have the tendency to "think things through thoroughly," a scarce quality in an age of superficiality. But the purpose of thinking is action, not trapping oneself. Understanding where your Self-Jealousy lies lets you turn "fighting yourself" into "doing things deeply"—deliberating where you should, letting go where you should, both profound and at ease.

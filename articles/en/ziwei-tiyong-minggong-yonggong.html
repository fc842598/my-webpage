Ti and Yong in Zi Wei Dou Shu: Why Life Is the Body and the Asked Palace the Function, and How to Switch

Ti (body) is who experiences it—always the Life Palace and the chart holder's whole structure; yong (function) is the matter asked, landing in Wealth, Spouse, Career and so on. Every topic is the Life body carrying a specific function palace: establish the Life body first, then read the function palace, so you never paste another person's fine pattern onto yourself.

Zi Wei Dou Shu2026-09-10

The same Spouse-Palace Hua Lu feels utterly different on a stable Life versus a weak Life; the difference is not the function palace but the body carrying it—why no palace can be read apart from Life.

What This Question Is Really Asking

Asking why the same star combination ends differently for you and another person, return to ti-yong: the function palace writes the event, but the Life body decides whether you can carry it.

The Underlying Logic

The body is the fixed "me": Life, Body Palace, life/body masters and the overall trine, setting temperament, ability structure and carrying capacity; it does not change with the question and underlies every topic.

The function is the moving "matter": Wealth for money, Spouse for relationships, Career for work; it writes the matter's own flow but only means something when it lands on the body of "me."

Ti-yong sets the ceiling: an auspicious function on a strong body is luck you truly catch; an auspicious function on a weak body is opportunity you cannot hold or that does not last; a weak function on a strong body can be carried and gradually repaired by personal foundation.

Switching ti-yong is advanced: for relatives you can "set the taiji" and temporarily treat Parents or Spouse as that person's Life (body), then read their trines—a second switch—but always locate the first layer, "my body," first.

A Step-by-Step Way to Read It

  1. For any question first establish the Life body: temperament, overall trine strength and carrying capacity
  2. Then fix this question's function palace and read that matter's structure
  3. Weigh the function's luck against the body: a strong body catches it, a weak body discounts it
  4. When body and function disagree, let the body set the ceiling and the function set the event's form
  5. For relatives, set a second taiji, treating their palace as their body before reading trines
  6. Frame the conclusion as "with a body like mine, experiencing this function-palace matter plays out how"

Three Common Mistakes

Mistake 1: Discussing a palace in isolation from Life, ignoring that the body decides whether you catch it

Mistake 2: Pasting another's fine pattern onto yourself despite different bodies

Mistake 3: Not knowing relatives need a second taiji, using only your own body to guess their matters

Back to Your Own Chart

Ti-yong is the subject and verb of a reading: the Life body is the subject, the asked palace the verb, and luck without a subject is empty words. Establish the body, then discuss function, and your readings land on a real person.

Reading Order

  1. Cast the natal chart first and locate the palaces and major stars tied to Life as the body and the asked palace as function before judging anything
  2. Open the triple-direction of those palaces and count the auspicious, malefic and assistant stars separately
  3. Check where the natal Four Transformations land to set the chart's baseline tone
  4. Read the decade-limit transformations to see whether the Life as the body and the asked palace as function line is activated in this stage
  5. Then read the annual transformations and the year's palace to pin down this year's trigger and timing
  6. Layer natal, decade and year before concluding, then decide how to act rather than labeling good or bad

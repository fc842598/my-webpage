Ban Kong Zhe Chi: Why a Weak Lian Tan Axis Can Break Midway

Ban Kong Zhe Chi is an old Zi Wei Dou Shu label for a pattern that can begin with momentum but lose support before the plan is complete. It is not a prediction of ruin, nor does it fix a crisis at a particular age.

The phrase is often discussed around a weakened Lian Zhen-Tan Lang axis. Lian Zhen concerns rules, desire, and boundaries; Tan Lang concerns appetite, networks, and opportunity. When both are poorly placed and receive difficult influences, ambition may outrun structure.

What “breaking midway” means

In practical terms, a project may expand before cash flow, governance, or trust is ready. The chart describes a vulnerability in how momentum is managed. It does not say that every venture, relationship, or career will collapse.

Two combination examples

For example, a weak Lian Zhen-Tan Lang combination in the Career Palace with Hua Ji may point to role conflict, unclear incentives, or reputation pressure. In the Wealth Palace with Di Kong or Di Jie, it may describe promised revenue that arrives late, costs that were underestimated, or an investment that lacks a stable base.

A second example is Tian Ma joining the pattern. Movement can bring clients and opportunity, but frequent changes of market or partner may also interrupt execution. Support from Hua Ke, Hua Quan, or well-placed stabilizing stars can turn the same drive into disciplined growth.

Timing changes the reading

If the pattern belongs to the natal chart, it is a recurring management theme. If it appears only in a ten-year or annual cycle, it may describe a temporary period of restructuring. “Midway” refers to the stage at which support weakens, not automatically to midlife.

A practical reading order

Start with the palace containing the Lian Zhen-Tan Lang axis and check star strength. Then read the opposite palace, the triad, the Four Transformations, and stabilizing support. Add the ten-year and annual cycles last, and translate the result into concrete checks on cash flow, contracts, workload, and decision rights.

Tan Lang and Wen Chang in Zi Wei Dou Shu: When Marketing Outruns Delivery

A practical Zi Wei Dou Shu reading of attraction, written promises, and the gap between a campaign and actual service.

Zi Wei Dou Shu2026-11-04 21:11

A campaign can attract customers and still create complaints when the delivered service differs from the promise. Tan Lang and Wen Chang in a Zi Wei Dou Shu chart can frame two jobs: drawing attention and explaining terms clearly. They do not prove that someone is a good marketer or a dishonest one.

Locate the work in the chart

Career may point to outreach and presentation, Wealth to pricing, Travel to external communication, and Friends to the team that must deliver. Check whether the stars share a palace, face each other, or connect through supporting palaces. The placements suggest questions; the written offer and customer record answer them.

Test the promise

Imagine an online course advertised as something a student can “master in seven days.” If the course provides only three introductory sessions, the team should define the actual learning goals, feedback, and refund terms. The instructor should approve promises that sales staff use. A restaurant promoting a limited menu faces a different test: booking rules, stock, and substitution terms must match what guests see on the platform.

Separate wording from capacity

Put the ad, sales conversation, contract, and delivery record on one timeline. Overstated copy calls for clearer language; accurate copy with too little staff calls for an operational fix. Assign an owner to each promise and keep versions aligned. Read transformations as a possible focus for review, not as proof of wrongdoing or a substitute for checking the service.

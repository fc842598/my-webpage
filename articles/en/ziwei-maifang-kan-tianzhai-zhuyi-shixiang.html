How to Read Buying a Home in Zi Wei Dou Shu: The Property Palace First, with Installments and Contract Details Checked Item by Item

Buying a home is a major matter; read the Property Palace mainly for fixed assets and dwelling luck, then the Wealth Palace for affordability. Wen Chang and Wen Qu often correspond to installments and loans, while Ju Men with Hua Ji warns against traps in contracts, title and verbal promises. The palaces are reference; before buying, review the contract item by item, verify the title and take on debt within one's means, not swept up by a bargain.

What This Question Is Really Asking

Some see a good Property Palace and buy on impulse, ignoring affordability and contract details, ending with heavy monthly payments or title and contract disputes.

The Underlying Logic

The Property Palace governs fixed assets and dwelling luck; read its star strength and whether Hua Lu appears to judge the overall ease of buying.

The Wealth Palace reads income and burden; if it is weak and the debt ratio too high, even a good Property Palace leaves one pressed by monthly payments.

Wen Chang and Wen Qu relate to documents, installments and loans, and their meeting favors proper loans; unclear procedures warn of lending and transfer problems.

Ju Men is the star of disputes and hidden matters; with Hua Ji in the Property Palace or trine, guard against traps in clauses, title and verbal promises, and read item by item before signing.

A Step-by-Step Way to Read It

  1. Read the Property Palace first to judge the overall ease of buying
  2. Then read the Wealth Palace and calculate income and affordable debt
  3. Read Chang-Qu to confirm whether loan and installment procedures are smooth
  4. Focus on guarding Ju Men with Hua Ji and review the contract and title item by item
  5. Verify the title, check for mortgages and do not trust verbal promises
  6. Take on debt within one's means and do not be swept up by bargains and limited-time offers

Three Common Mistakes

Mistake 1: Buying on impulse from a good Property Palace alone

Mistake 2: Ignoring Wealth and taking on too much debt under monthly payments

Mistake 3: Not reviewing the contract and title and trusting verbal promises

Zi Wei Dou Shu After a Layoff: Job, Side Business, or Startup?

Compare runway, delivery duties, and family commitments before using a Zi Wei Dou Shu chart to discuss a startup decision.

Zi Wei Dou Shu2026-11-22 09:03

A layoff can make a new job, a side business, and a full-time startup look like three versions of the same career choice. They carry different cash and delivery risks. Before reading a Chinese astrology chart, write down available savings, monthly essentials, and the date by which another job search must begin.

Separate role from income

The Career Palace describes the kind of work and responsibility under discussion. The Life Palace adds how a person handles decisions. Someone who coordinated a team inside a company may have to handle support, billing, and complaints alone as a founder. Check those missing duties instead of treating a strong career marker as permission to quit.

Put cash flow beside the Wealth Palace

The Wealth Palace helps frame income sources and payment timing. A promising lead is not cash received. Compare salary, paid pilot orders, taxes, and the number of months savings can cover essentials. Hua Lu may direct attention to a resource channel; Hua Ji may direct attention to collection or delay. Neither replaces a bank statement.

Test the outside market and household agreement

Use the Travel Palace to ask about clients and outside opportunities, then price travel and delivery time. Use the Spouse Palace to discuss shared obligations with a partner, not to claim the partner will approve. A three-month pilot with a cash floor and a clear return-to-job trigger is more useful than a vague prediction. Read natal structure first, add the ten-year and annual layers, and compare all three choices against actual contracts.

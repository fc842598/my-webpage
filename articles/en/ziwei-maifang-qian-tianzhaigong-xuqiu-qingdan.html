A Home-Buying Needs List from the Zi Wei Dou Shu Home Palace

Turn Home Palace questions into a practical checklist for space, location, and affordability before viewing property.

Zi Wei Dou Shu2026-10-26 22:13

Asking whether the Home Palace is favorable cannot tell you if a property has water damage or whether the mortgage fits your income. It can help you clarify what kind of home you need before you visit listings.

Write three lists

For space, note privacy, light, bedrooms, and household routines. For location, note commute, schools, caregiving, and services. For finances, include the down payment, monthly costs, repairs, and an emergency reserve. The Home Palace can prompt priorities; the Travel, Wealth, and Career Palaces add context about movement and income.

Someone who values quiet should inspect a property at night. Someone who wants control over renovations should set a budget and schedule first. Neither chart pattern verifies the building's condition.

Include everyone affected

A couple should agree on ownership, debt, and who will handle daily care. Unstable bonuses should not become the baseline for a mortgage. If older relatives want the home nearby, write down the expected care schedule and what happens if work changes.

Start with needs, use the chart to ask sharper questions, then verify title, financing, inspection reports, contracts, and the family's consent. That is a safer use of the Home Palace than treating it as a purchase signal.

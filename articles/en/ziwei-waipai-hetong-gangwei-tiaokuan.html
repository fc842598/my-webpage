Before a Work Relocation: How to Turn Travel Palace Stars into Contract Terms

How to translate the Travel Palace tendency into a checklist of written contract terms rather than signing on a vague "good for going abroad."

Zi Wei Dou Shu2026-11-17 09:24

The Travel Palace describes what happens after you leave a familiar environment, including relocation, transfer, and developing elsewhere. But the stars only give tendencies, while the concrete contract terms decide whether a relocation is stable. Translating the star hints into terms to verify prevents signing merely because the chart "suits going abroad."

Star hints must land in terms

The star combination in the Travel Palace shows how you adapt away from home and the rhythm you may meet, such as active versus passive, or frequent change versus strong support. These are not conclusions. They must land in the contract: the role, who you report to, the duration, pay and allowances, and the mechanism for returning.

Step-by-step relocation contract check

  1. Read the major stars and transformations in the Travel Palace for overall adaptation.
  2. Turn that tendency into a question list mapped to contract terms.
  3. Verify duties, reporting lines, and relocation duration, confirming no oral promises.
  4. Check pay, relocation allowance, housing, and insurance arrangements.
  5. Confirm return, termination, and breach clauses before signing.

"Good for going abroad" is not enough

Even a smooth Travel Palace cannot replace contract review. If terms are vague and rely only on oral promises, a good chart can still lead to loss away from home. Keep the chart hints and the written contract separate: stars set the direction, terms set the risk.

Chart reading order

  1. Fix the chart and Travel Palace, then confirm the star combination.
  2. Note the star hints separately and list the terms to verify.
  3. Review the contract term by term, focusing on role, duration, pay, and return.
  4. Require every oral promise to be written down.
  5. Keep the conclusion conditional rather than treating the travel tendency as a guarantee.

Flying-Star Reading in Zi Wei Dou Shu: Three Signs, One Event and How Palace, Star and Transformation Merge

To read a concrete event you need three kinds of information: the palace sets the life domain, the star sets the manner and the person, and the transformation sets the state in which it happens. When several palaces send the same sign toward one event, that is three-signs-one-event; a single point never makes a verdict.

Zi Wei Dou Shu2026-09-10

Beginners see Hua Ji and call it bad, but which palace it lands in, which star carries it, and what it chains to decide what the event actually is and how serious it gets.

What This Question Is Really Asking

Asking whether you will change jobs, reconcile, or get money back, let palace, star and sign identify each other; only when at least two point to the same event do you conclude.

The Underlying Logic

The palace is where the event lives: career reads Career, relationships Spouse, money Wealth. The same Ji in Career is a work knot and in Spouse a relationship knot; fix the palace first.

The star is the event's shape and role: Ji on Wu Qu tends to involve money and hard decisions, on Tian Ji overthinking and plan changes, on Ju Men words and communication; the star sets the form.

The transformation is the event's state: Lu is origin and gain, Quan is striving and expansion, Ke is steadiness and reputation, Ji is closure and owed ties; the three together show whether the matter arrives, is fought over, eased or settled.

Three-signs-one-event requires convergent points: when home, opposite, trine or flying chains send two or more matching signs to the same person or event, the verdict holds; one lone sign is only a tendency and cannot be asserted as fact.

A Step-by-Step Way to Read It

  1. Translate the question into a concrete palace, fixing the life domain
  2. Read the major stars of that palace and its triple-direction to see the form and the person
  3. See where the transformation lands and which star carries it to set gain, striving, easing or closure
  4. Follow flying transformations and trines to find the second and third sign on the same event
  5. Conclude only when multiple points converge; a single point stays a tendency to verify
  6. Finally use decade/year for timing rather than only yes-or-no

Three Common Mistakes

Mistake 1: Asserting an event from one transformed star without assembling palace-star-sign or a second convergent point

Mistake 2: Saying only "Ji is bad" without naming the event, its form and severity

Mistake 3: Treating a single tendency as inevitable, ignoring that three-signs-one-event needs cross-confirmation

Back to Your Own Chart

Palace sets the domain, star the shape, transformation the state, then let two or three points converge on one event. Master three-signs-one-event and you move from memorizing luck to assembling evidence, with sharper, better-grounded readings.

Reading Order

  1. Cast the natal chart first and locate the palaces and major stars tied to the three-signs-one-event method before judging anything
  2. Open the triple-direction of those palaces and count the auspicious, malefic and assistant stars separately
  3. Check where the natal Four Transformations land to set the chart's baseline tone
  4. Read the decade-limit transformations to see whether the the three-signs-one-event method line is activated in this stage
  5. Then read the annual transformations and the year's palace to pin down this year's trigger and timing
  6. Layer natal, decade and year before concluding, then decide how to act rather than labeling good or bad

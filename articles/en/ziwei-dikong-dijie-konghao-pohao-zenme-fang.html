Di Kong and Di Jie in Zi Wei Dou Shu: Telling Empty Drain from Sudden Loss—and How to Guard

Di Kong is mental empty-drain—much thought, little landing, detachment; Di Jie is material sudden loss and resource leakage. One leaks in ideas, the other in things, so the guards differ.

Zi Wei Dou Shu2026-09-05

Guard Di Kong against cycling ideas that never land, and Di Jie against sudden resource breaks; find the leak before patching.

What This Question Is Really Asking

Treating both as money loss leaves Di Kong's long-term drain and drifting direction unaddressed—the real inner leak stays open.

The Underlying Logic

Di Kong is yin fire—ideation, inspiration and mental detachment; creative and philosophical, but ungrounded, plans go empty.

Di Jie is yang fire—sudden breakage and resources taken away, more direct and material than Di Kong.

Together they bring fluctuation but also non-attachment, suiting creative, R&D and spiritual fields.

Guards differ: Di Kong needs landing mechanisms and focus; Di Jie needs financial buffers, backups and less single dependence.

A Step-by-Step Way to Read It

  1. Tell Di Kong from Di Jie and their palace—mental versus material leak
  2. Di Kong: set landing deadlines and minimal actions to stop empty cycling
  3. Di Jie: build emergency funds, backups and multiple channels to cut single-point risk
  4. In Wealth or Property: avoid leverage and heavy concentration; defend over attack
  5. Use their willingness-to-let-go for innovation without risking core resources
  6. Use the decade to spot activated years and shrink early rather than expand

Three Common Mistakes

Mistake 1: Treating both as money loss and ignoring Di Kong's mental drain

Mistake 2: Only defending, never converting their creativity and non-attachment

Mistake 3: Concentrating and leveraging in a Di Jie year, magnifying loss

Back to Your Own Chart

Empty stars are not a verdict of keeping nothing. Train Di Kong to land and Di Jie to defend; once the leaks close, they give room to break and rebuild.

Reading Order

  1. Cast the natal chart first and locate the palaces and major stars tied to Di Kong and Di Jie before judging anything
  2. Open the triple-direction of those palaces and count the auspicious, malefic and assistant stars separately
  3. Check where the natal Four Transformations land to set the chart's baseline tone
  4. Read the decade-limit transformations to see whether the Di Kong and Di Jie line is activated in this stage
  5. Then read the annual transformations and the year's palace to pin down this year's trigger and timing
  6. Layer natal, decade and year before concluding, then decide how to act rather than labeling good or bad

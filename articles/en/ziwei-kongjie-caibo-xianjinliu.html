Kong Jie, commonly referring to Di Kong and Di Jie, can make Wealth Palace results feel less solid than the headline number suggests. The issue is often not an absence of income, but delayed collection, unexpected leakage, or plans that never become usable cash.

Revenue is not the same as retained money

Di Kong carries the idea of emptiness or an unrealized promise; Di Jie often points to depletion or interruption. In the Wealth Palace, signed work may not be paid on time, projected profit may disappear into costs, or a sudden change may cancel the expected return. The placement calls for cash-flow discipline, not a lifetime verdict of poverty.

Combinations that show where money leaks

  • Di Kong or Di Jie in the Wealth Palace with a strong Travel Palace may bring many external clients but slow settlement. Deposits, milestone billing, and credit limits help convert market demand into cash.
  • Kong Jie in Wealth with malefic pressure in the Friends Palace may make familiar partnerships or informal loans a source of loss. If Hua Lu supports Career, income can still be healthy, but shared accounts and vague expenses remain risky.

Timing changes the scale of the problem

A profitable ten-year cycle can contain one annual period of write-offs or high spending without destroying the entire decade. Conversely, a favorable annual income signal may not last if the larger structure is weak. Maintain reserves, distinguish booked revenue from cleared funds, and avoid expanding on money that has not arrived.

A clear chart-reading order

First judge Di Kong and Di Jie in the natal Wealth Palace. Second read Career for the income engine and Travel for the real market. Third inspect Friends for counterparties and Property or Inner Life for large spending and impulse. Finally compare ten-year and annual cycles to decide whether the issue is a structural retention habit or a temporary cash-flow gap.

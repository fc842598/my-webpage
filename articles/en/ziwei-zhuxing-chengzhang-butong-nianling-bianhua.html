Does Your Major Star Change With Age: The Same Star Expresses Very Differently at Twenty and Forty

The Life Palace major star sets a lifelong temperament baseline, but a baseline is not fixed behavior. The same major star expresses noticeably differently at different ages and stages: when young, one easily amplifies the star’s weaknesses, such as impulsiveness, stubbornness or hesitation, while with experience one can better use the strengths and hold the shortcomings in check. The key is reading a person through three layers, baseline, stage and choices, rather than labeling them for life from one star. Growth is moving from instinctive reaction to skilled use of the star’s nature.

Many people are told when young that they have a certain star’s temperament, yet by middle age find their way of doing things has changed a lot. The baseline remains, but it is simply used more maturely.

What This Question Is Really Asking

The question is how to understand a major star’s variation across ages. First distinguish the unchanging baseline from variable expression, then read the stage through the decade cycles.

The Underlying Logic

The Life Palace star is the baseline: Zi Wei always values vision and authority, the Sun always cares about being seen and recognized, and this layer does not change.

What changes is expression: a young Qi Sha may impulsively force through, while a mature one may become strategically decisive; a young Tai Yin may drain emotionally, while a mature one may refine and manage carefully.

The decade cycles drive change: each ten years under a different palace star pushes one toward different lessons and contexts, forcing the original nature into a new mode.

The mark of growth is not becoming another person, but using the same baseline more appropriately: strengths amplified, shortcomings held, and knowing which side to use when.

A Step-by-Step Way to Read It

  1. First confirm your Life Palace major star and write down its core baseline and typical strengths and weaknesses
  2. Review how you behaved at twenty, thirty and now, identifying what stayed the same and what changed
  3. Read the current decade Life Palace star to understand what this ten-year period is pushing you to practice
  4. Compare the abilities this stage needs with your star nature, finding strengths to amplify and shortcomings to hold
  5. Deliberately practice: in concrete matters, switch to a more mature expression rather than following instinct
  6. Review regularly to see whether you are using the same baseline more appropriately, rather than trying to become someone else

Three Common Mistakes

Mistake 1: Assuming the major star fixes you for life and that people do not change, ignoring growth and stages

Mistake 2: Taking a temporary behavior shift as the star nature changing, when the baseline remains

Mistake 3: Negating a star from youthful behavior, or glorifying shortcomings from mature behavior

Back to Your Own Chart

The major star is a baseline, not a cage, and the same star can be used very differently at different ages. Seeing the baseline and practicing with the stage, you will find that growth is not replacing yourself but using yourself more maturely.

How to Layer the Decade, Minor and Annual Limits: Ten-Year Background and Yearly Triggers

The decade governs the roughly ten-year background, the year governs that year's progress, and the minor limit and Tai-Sui serve as auxiliary checks. Every dynamic layer returns to natal capacity; the ten-year theme cannot be copied to each year.

Zi Wei Dou Shu2026-09-25

Whether a good decade means ten good years and whether the year or minor limit takes priority are confusions from not layering. Timing charts are like map scales: the decade shows the trend and the year shows triggers, each at its own scale.

What This Question Is Really Asking

The common error is mixing information across scales, skipping the natal, copying the ten-year theme to every year, and double-counting the minor and annual limits. Layering means each level answers only its own question.

The Underlying Logic

The decade is responsible for the roughly ten-year stage theme, and the year for that year's concrete progress.

The minor limit and Tai-Sui are auxiliary checks and should not be double-counted with the year.

The natal chart is the foundation for every dynamic layer, which cannot detach from natal capacity.

Schools differ in how they set the minor and annual limits, so declare the rules used first.

A Step-by-Step Way to Read It

  1. First read the natal chart to confirm the foundation's carrying conditions
  2. Then cast the decade to set the current ten-year stage theme
  3. Then read the year to set that year's progress on the decade theme
  4. Use the minor limit and Tai-Sui as auxiliary checks without double-counting
  5. Check whether the ten-year verdict has been copied directly onto one year
  6. Use concrete matters such as promotion and moving to show why the decade and the year say different things

Three Common Mistakes

Mistake 1: Spreading the decade verdict evenly across every year of the ten

Mistake 2: Double-counting the minor and annual limits on the same information

Mistake 3: Talking about limits while skipping the natal, leaving the foundation empty

Back to Your Own Chart

Layering is the basic skill of timing charts. The decade gives background, the year gives triggers, and the natal gives the foundation; when each is in place, you understand one matter across different scales.

Reading Order

  1. Cast the natal chart first and locate the palaces and major stars tied to the layering of decade, minor and annual limits before judging anything
  2. Open the triple-direction of those palaces and count the auspicious, malefic and assistant stars separately
  3. Check where the natal Four Transformations land to set the chart's baseline tone
  4. Read the decade-limit transformations to see whether the the layering of decade, minor and annual limits line is activated in this stage
  5. Then read the annual transformations and the year's palace to pin down this year's trigger and timing
  6. Layer natal, decade and year before concluding, then decide how to act rather than labeling good or bad

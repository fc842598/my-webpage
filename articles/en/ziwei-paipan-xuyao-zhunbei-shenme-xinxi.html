What Information Is Needed to Cast a Zi Wei Chart: Birth Date, Time, Place, Gender, Plus Past Events for Rectification

To cast a Zi Wei chart, the basic information is birth year, month, day, time, birthplace and gender, but these alone are not fully stable. Because hour boundaries, early and late Zi hour, true solar time and leap months can shift the chart, you also need several past major events, schooling, work, relationships, moves and illness, to rectify and verify. The key is the more accurate the information the better; if the hour is uncertain, cast several candidate charts and reverse-engineer from major events, and confirm that casting tools and calendar settings match, avoiding one using lunar and another solar.

Many only give a birth date when casting, and the resulting chart does not match them, often because the hour, calendar or birthplace was not corrected. Preparing the information before casting saves much back-and-forth later.

What This Question Is Really Asking

The question is what information to prepare before casting and why. First understand that Zi Wei casting is sensitive to hour and calendar, then prepare item by item and keep major events for rectification.

The Underlying Logic

Six basic items: birth year, month, day, time, precise to the hour and ideally to the minute, birthplace, province and city level for true solar time correction, and gender.

The hour is the most error-prone: the Zi hour must be split into early Zi, midnight to one, and late Zi, eleven to midnight, and births near hour boundaries can produce completely different charts, so cast candidates when uncertain.

The calendar must be unified: confirm whether lunar or solar is used, how leap months are handled, and whether the casting tool settings match, as different tools may produce different results from calendar differences.

Rectification needs past major events: list several memorable events with clear dates, schooling, work changes, marriage, moves and illness, to verify which candidate chart’s cycles fit best; this step is more reliable than birth date and hour alone.

A Step-by-Step Way to Read It

  1. First confirm the birth date and time, as precise to the minute as possible, marking what is uncertain
  2. Confirm the birthplace, at least province and city level, for true solar time correction
  3. Confirm gender and calendar, lunar or solar and whether a leap month is involved, and unify the casting tool settings
  4. If the hour is uncertain or near a boundary, cast two to three candidate charts
  5. List about five past major events with clear dates for rectification
  6. Compare the candidate charts’ decade and year cycles against the events, choose the best fit as the official chart, and review again if questions arise later

Three Common Mistakes

Mistake 1: Giving only the birth date without the hour, so the Life Palace cannot be determined

Mistake 2: Insisting on one hour when uncertain, without casting candidates or rectifying with events

Mistake 3: Ignoring birthplace and true solar time, as local time differs from Beijing time by longitude

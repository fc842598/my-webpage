Plenty of Orders but No Cash: Reading Tan Lang and Hua Ji

Separate sales volume, collections, profit, and usable cash when Tan Lang brings business while Hua Ji exposes leakage in the Wealth Palace.

Zi Wei Dou Shu2026-08-01 01:06

A busy business can still be short of cash. Messages arrive all day, contracts are signed, and staff are fully occupied, yet payroll or inventory requires another loan. The problem is often not a lack of demand. It is the distance between an order and money that remains available after delivery.

Tan Lang can increase commercial activity, social reach, and the appetite to pursue opportunity. Hua Ji in the Wealth Palace asks where value becomes trapped, disputed, or lost. A useful reading follows the movement of cash instead of calling the whole period prosperous or unlucky.

Tan Lang Measures Commercial Motion Before Profit

In a business context, Tan Lang can support selling, negotiation, new contacts, and a larger flow of opportunities. The same expansion may raise entertainment costs, discounts, promises, and the number of projects that require funding before payment arrives.

Replace the question about how much was sold with how much was collected. An invoice is not cash, and an order with a negative delivery margin makes the shortage worse. Tan Lang can find demand; it does not automatically protect the margin.

Hua Ji Locates the Financial Obstruction

Hua Ji includes loss, dispute, and a tendency to remain caught in an unproductive loop. In the Wealth Palace, inspect the mechanism: refunds, bad debt, compensation, inventory, customer credit, or expansion undertaken before the earlier work has paid.

Example 1: Tan Lang with Hua Ji in the Wealth Palace can bring fast promotional orders whose cash is lost to discounts, returns, advances, and slow payment. Measuring profit per order and days to collection is more useful than celebrating gross sales.

Money Under Control May Still Belong to Someone Else

Wealth joined by authority can create financial control: the person may approve budgets, hold customer deposits, or move substantial amounts. Revenue, however, still contains tax, supplier obligations, labor, partner shares, and the cost of unfinished delivery.

A weekly cash view should separate collected funds, committed delivery cost, accounts payable, and unrestricted balance. This prevents a large bank inflow from being mistaken for profit that can safely be spent.

Partnership Friction Can Delay Every Collection

Supportive stars in the Friends Palace can help partners earn together. Ju Men, Hua Ji, Qing Yang, Fire and Bell, or Kong Jie increase the need for precise rules around promises, accounts, and allocation. A friendly relationship does not make ambiguous money harmless.

Example 2: Strong Tan Lang business activity with Ju Men Hua Ji or Kong Jie in the Friends Palace can produce customers but repeated disputes over pricing and revenue sharing. Fix who owns the client, who may quote, and where payment is received before chasing another wave of sales.

Practical Reading Order

First check whether the annual Life Palace reaches the natal palace containing Tan Lang, then judge how strongly that natal placement expresses itself through the career question. Examine the Wealth Palace for Lu, Hua Ji, and financial authority, distinguishing earned income from money merely controlled. Then inspect the Friends Palace for collection and sharing disputes. Reconcile orders, receivables, gross margin, and unrestricted cash before choosing to grow, collect, renegotiate, or stop a loss-making offer.

Why Zi Wei Dou Shu Charts Need Apparent Solar Time Correction: Longitude Can Shift Your Hour Block

Clocks use unified Beijing time, but casting uses the apparent solar time at the birthplace. Every 15 degrees of longitude is about an hour, and with daylight saving and the equation of time, a boundary-hour birth can land in the wrong palace without correction.

Zi Wei Dou Shu2026-09-09

A 7:00 birth on the eastern coast and one in the west sit at very different actual sun positions. Casting asks where the local sun actually is, not the unified clock time; skip this and the whole hour block can shift.

What This Question Is Really Asking

When born near an hour boundary (within about fifteen minutes of 7, 9 or 11 o'clock) and far from 120°E longitude, apparent-time correction often decides which two-hour block you truly belong to.

The Underlying Logic

Beijing time is based on 120°E as a unified average; apparent solar time corrects by birthplace longitude, about four minutes per degree—later than the clock in the west, earlier in the east.

A second correction is the equation of time: orbit and axial tilt make the true solar day vary around the average all year by up to about fifteen minutes, included in precise casting.

A third is daylight saving: China set clocks forward one hour during 1986–1991 and other periods, so births then must first be set back before longitude correction.

If correction still lands on a boundary, verify as with early/late Zi using past events; most apps correct automatically from birthplace, but confirm longitude and daylight saving are actually applied.

A Step-by-Step Way to Read It

  1. Get the exact solar birth time and birth city, then look up its longitude
  2. Handle daylight saving first: if born during a DST period, set the clock time back one hour
  3. Apply longitude correction (subtract in the west, add in the east, about four minutes per degree)
  4. Add the equation of time if needed to obtain the true local solar time used for casting
  5. Check whether the corrected time crosses an hour boundary; if critical, cast both adjacent blocks
  6. Verify the two against past events, fix the correct hour, and only then interpret formally

Three Common Mistakes

Mistake 1: Casting straight from clock time and ignoring longitude, especially error-prone for western births

Mistake 2: Forgetting daylight saving and treating the advanced hour as real, shifting the whole chart

Mistake 3: Entering the city but never confirming the app truly applies longitude and DST correction

Back to Your Own Chart

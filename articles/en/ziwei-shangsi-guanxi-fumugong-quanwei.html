Superior Relationships in Zi Wei Dou Shu: Parents Palace as Authority Mapping

In Zi Wei Dou Shu, the Parents Palace represents not only parents but also "authority figures"—superiors, leaders, teachers, elders. Your relationship pattern with parents often replicates in relationships with superiors. Understanding the star combinations in the Parents Palace helps explain why you always clash with certain types of bosses and how to improve.

Zi Wei Dou Shu2026-09-24

Correspondence between Parents Palace and superior relations: Parents Palace has auspicious stars (Zi Wei, Tian Fu, Tian Xiang, Prosperity, Recognition)—good relations with authority, easily appreciated and promoted by superiors, suitable for organizations with clear hierarchy. Parents Palace has malefics (Qing Yang, Tuo Lo, Huo Xing, Ling Xing)—easily conflicts with authority, resents being managed, talks back to superiors, suitable for flat management or being ones own boss. Parents Palace Jealousy—relations with superiors are a long-term lesson, easily encounters harsh superiors or feels pressure in front of authority. Parents Palace empty or no major star—unstable attitude toward authority, sometimes dependent sometimes rebellious, need to look at the opposite palace (Health Palace) stars. Parents Palace Wu Qu—boss is pragmatic type, only looks at results, need "speak with data and outcomes." Parents Palace Tian Tong—boss is easygoing type, relationship easily like friends, but also maintain professional boundaries. Methods to improve superior relations: first, "identify boss type"—based on Parents Palace stars, determine what type of boss you easily encounter and your own reaction pattern. Second, "adjust communication style"—not all bosses respond to the same approach; to dominant bosses speak logic and respect, to delicate bosses speak feelings and details. Third, "manage expectations"—those with Parents Palace Jealousy should not expect a perfect boss, focus on "what can I learn from this boss" not "is this boss good." Fourth, "if really cannot get along"—when Decade/Annual Parents Palace has malefic activation, it is a window period for changing bosses or jobs.

What This Question Is Really Asking

Practical case: Someone with Zi Wei in Life Palace and Authority in Career Palace is often a strong manager, but if Fortune Palace has Jealousy, they easily burn out from stress. The key is not "whether to be strong" but "in what scenario to be strong"—decisive in work decisions, flexible in relationships.

The Underlying Logic

Treating chart career signals as fixed labels, ignoring Decade/Annual changes.

Only looking at major stars without transformations, missing the key impact of Authority/Jealousy on career.

Interpreting "suitable career" as "can only do this," limiting own possibilities.

Ignoring the positive side of malefics—Qing Yang/Tuo Lo can be advantages in certain professions (surgery, legal).

A Step-by-Step Way to Read It

  1. Chart the Life, Career, and Wealth palaces major stars and transformations.
  2. See which palace the current Decade moves through to determine the ten-year career focus.
  3. Find "advantage palaces" (many auspicious stars, Prosperity/Authority) and push in that direction.
  4. Identify "challenge palaces" (Jealousy, many malefics) and prepare coping strategies in advance.
  5. Combine personal interests and real conditions to choose 2-3 best-matching career directions.
  6. Adjust in practice—the chart gives tendencies, action gives answers.

Three Common Mistakes

Mistake 1: The chart only indicates tendencies; career development is affected by education, opportunity, personal effort and many factors.

Mistake 2: Decade/Annual changes may make an "unsuitable" career become suitable in a certain period.

Mistake 3: Do not blindly resign because the chart says "suitable for entrepreneurship"; need to combine actual resources and ability.

Back to Your Own Chart

There is no absolute "good fortune" or "bad fortune" in the workplace, only whether your strengths are used in the right place. The chart helps you see your talents and lessons; the rest is sustained effort in the right direction. Understand first, then act—more effective than blind job-hopping or grinding through.

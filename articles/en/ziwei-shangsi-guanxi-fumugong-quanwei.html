The Parents Palace and Relationships With Supervisors

The Parents Palace can frame discussion of authority figures. Compare the interpretation with actual expectations, communication, and workplace support.

Zi Wei Dou Shu2026-09-24Updated 2026-11-06 15:10 (UTC+8)

The Parents Palace is also used in traditional readings about teachers, elders, and workplace authority figures. It does not identify a supervisor's personality or establish that a difficult relationship is inevitable. Keep the chart owner's perspective separate from claims about another person.

Describe the Working Arrangement

Start with reporting lines, responsibilities, and how decisions are communicated. A disagreement about changing requirements may need a different response from unclear feedback or a mismatch in authority. The chart cannot verify any of these facts.

Read Related Palaces

Compare the Parents Palace with the Career Palace and the wider support structure. Use Zi Wei, Tian Ji, Tai Yang, or other star themes as questions to explore, not a typology that tells you how a boss must behave. Tian Ji is the star's name; 'Ji Ji' is not the preferred English term.

Keep Practical Steps Evidence-Based

Clarifying expectations and keeping an accurate record of agreed work can help make a discussion specific. A challenging cycle is not, by itself, a reason to expect mistreatment or change jobs. Separate symbolic interpretation from decisions based on observed working conditions.

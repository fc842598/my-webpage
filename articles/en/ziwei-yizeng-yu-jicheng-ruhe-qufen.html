Zi Wei Dou Shu on Legacy vs Inheritance: Timing and Order

Inheritance passes to statutory heirs in order, while a legacy leaves property to someone outside the statutory heirs, read through the Parents, Property and Wealth Palaces. The key difference is that a legacy must be accepted within two months of knowing; silence is treated as refusal.

Zi Wei Dou Shu2026-10-29

Inheritance and legacy differ in recipient, order and timing; when dealing with an estate, identify your status and respond within the period.

What This Question Is Really Asking

Some treat a legacy to a non-relative as inheritance and do not accept within two months, and are deemed to have refused.

The Underlying Logic

Taking an estate corresponds to the Parents, Property and Wealth Palaces, marking property and homes left by elders.

Statutory inheritance has first and second orders, and a will or legacy is handled according to the will.

A legacy goes to an organization or person outside the statutory heirs and must be accepted within two months of knowing.

Inheritance generally begins at death, and renunciation must be made before the estate is distributed.

A Step-by-Step Way to Read It

  1. Confirm whether you are a statutory heir and your order
  2. Check whether there is a will and whether it is inheritance or a legacy
  3. If it is a legacy, clearly accept within two months of knowing
  4. Read the Parents, Property and Wealth Palaces to clarify the estate
  5. Where needed, handle inheritance notarization or transfer
  6. Follow the written will and the law, consulting a lawyer where needed

Three Common Mistakes

Mistake 1: Treating a legacy as inheritance and missing the two-month acceptance

Mistake 2: Not checking the will and heir order

Mistake 3: Not responding in time and being deemed to refuse

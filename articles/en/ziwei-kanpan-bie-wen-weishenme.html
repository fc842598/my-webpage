Don't Ask "Why Me": Ask "What Do I Do Next"

Many people read charts to find causes — "why is my love life bad" or "why can't I earn money." But Zi Wei Dou Shu is better at answering "what's the trend" and "how to respond" than "why."

Methods2026-08-20 10:15

"Why me?" is the most common question in chart reading. Why is my love life bad? Why can't I earn money? Why do I always meet petty people? These sound reasonable, but Zi Wei Dou Shu isn't good at answering "why." The chart shows "what" and "when" — your traits, what you'll encounter, when it happens. As for "why," that involves past lives, karma, free will — things a chart can't explain.

The most valuable part of Zi Wei isn't "explaining the past" but "predicting the future and giving strategy." Knowing "why" changes nothing; knowing "what comes next and how to respond" helps you decide. Like weather forecast — you don't need to know "why it rains today"; you need "will it rain tomorrow and should I bring an umbrella."

Why "Why" Questions Are Useless

"Why" points to the past — it already happened; knowing the cause changes nothing.

"Why" leads to victim mentality — "it's all fate's fault," then stop trying.

"Why" answers are often guesses — a reader can give ten reasons, none verifiable.

"Why" keeps you stuck — you keep searching for causes instead of moving forward.

What to Ask Instead

"What comes next?" — ask trends, not causes.

"What should I watch for?" — ask risks, not fate.

"When is the critical period?" — ask timing, not right/wrong.

"What's my best move?" — ask strategy, not verdict.

"Can this work out?" — ask probability, not guarantees.

Converting "Why" to "What Now"

"Why is my love life bad?" → "What's my relationship pattern? What to watch for next time?"

"Why can't I earn money?" → "What's my wealth pattern? When are opportunities? How to seize them?"

"Why do I always meet petty people?" → "What's my interpersonal pattern? Which year to be careful?"

"Why am I so tired?" → "What's my energy pattern? When should I rest?"

Reading Order

When reading a chart, ask in this order:

  1. First "what" — what patterns and trends does the chart show.
  2. Then "when" — when do cycles activate.
  3. Finally "how" — how should I respond, what to watch for.
  4. Don't ask "why me" — no answer, no help.
  5. Remember: Zi Wei is "navigation," not "archaeology" — it tells you the road ahead, not how you got here.

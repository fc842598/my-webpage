How to Find Where a Pattern Is Broken in a Zi Wei Chart

A pattern is a net combination of stars, and its breaking point is the one or two malefics, Ji, emptiness or clashes that disrupt the structure. Check in the order of home palace, trines, opposite and flanking palaces to distinguish a valid pattern with flaws from one that exists in name only.

Zi Wei Dou Shu2026-10-06

Do not only count auspicious stars for a pattern; reverse the lens and look for the break. A break at the core means it does not hold, while one at the edge only discounts it.

What This Question Is Really Asking

Some declare a pattern once auspicious stars gather, while others call it wholly broken at one malefic; the real task is judging where that star breaks and how heavily.

The Underlying Logic

A pattern holds through structure, and its breaking point is the star that disrupts that structure; position sets the degree of damage.

A malefic or Ji in the home palace weighs most, then the trines and opposite, then the flanking palaces.

However many auspicious stars there are, one Hua Ji or malefic at a key position can keep the pattern from holding.

Distinguish flawed (the pattern holds with discounted benefit) from broken (the structure does not hold); they are not the same.

A Step-by-Step Way to Read It

  1. Write the pattern conditions clearly and check them one by one
  2. List the home palace major and assistant stars to see whether the core holds
  3. Count malefics, Ji, emptiness and clashes in the triple-direction to locate the break
  4. Then check the flanking palaces for hidden disruption
  5. Judge whether the break is at the core or edge, meaning discount or failure
  6. Only then give action guidance rather than a vague good or bad label

Three Common Mistakes

Mistake 1: Calling a pattern wholly broken from one malefic and frightening yourself

Mistake 2: Counting only auspicious stars without finding the break, treating a failed pattern as valid

Mistake 3: Being unable to locate the break and vaguely calling the pattern good or bad

Back to Your Own Chart

Finding the breaking point moves you from admiring a pattern to reading its structure. Knowing where and how heavily it breaks tells you whether the pattern can be relied on and how to reinforce it.

Reading Order

  1. Cast the natal chart first and locate the palaces and major stars tied to where a pattern is broken before judging anything
  2. Open the triple-direction of those palaces and count the auspicious, malefic and assistant stars separately
  3. Check where the natal Four Transformations land to set the chart's baseline tone
  4. Read the decade-limit transformations to see whether the where a pattern is broken line is activated in this stage
  5. Then read the annual transformations and the year's palace to pin down this year's trigger and timing
  6. Layer natal, decade and year before concluding, then decide how to act rather than labeling good or bad

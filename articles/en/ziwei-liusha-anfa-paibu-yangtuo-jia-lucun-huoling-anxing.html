How the Six Malefics Are Placed: Why Qing Yang-Tuo Luo Always Flank Lu Cun and Fire-Bell-Void Follow Rules

The six malefics are not random: Qing Yang/Tuo Luo are set by the year stem and always flank Lu Cun; Huo Xing/Ling Xing are placed by year and hour branches; Di Kong/Di Jie sit adjacent. Understanding placement explains why certain combinations are inevitable.

Zi Wei Dou Shu2026-09-08

Many memorize malefics only as bad omens, but their positions are calculated. Understand how they are placed and patterns like Yang-Tuo flanking Lu or the paired voids stop looking like coincidence or personal bad luck.

What This Question Is Really Asking

People wonder why Lu Cun is always next to a malefic. The chart is not broken—placement rules guarantee it. The question is not luck but whether you can read this fixed structure.

The Underlying Logic

Lu Cun is set by the year stem, with Qing Yang one palace before and Tuo Luo one after, so "Yang-Tuo flank Lu" is structural—Lu Cun always carries a sense of something hard-won that must be guarded.

Huo Xing and Ling Xing are placed from the year and hour branches and usually sit a few palaces apart, one a quick burst and the other a delayed slow burn, also fixed.

Di Kong and Di Jie always sit adjacent (one palace apart), one voiding on the mental plane and one taking on the material plane, so they appear as a pair hitting the same palace group.

Because placement is rule-based, much of the clasping/meeting/clashing in a chart is structural; learning the rules lets you predict which two palaces a malefic pair links even before the chart is fully laid out.

A Step-by-Step Way to Read It

  1. Use the birth year stem to find Lu Cun, then one palace forward for Qing Yang and back for Tuo Luo, verifying Yang-Tuo flank Lu yourself
  2. Use the year branch plus birth hour to place Fire/Bell and see why they sit a fixed distance apart
  3. Remember voids are adjacent; when you see one, look for its partner
  4. Apply this fixed structure to your own chart and mark which palaces each malefic group links
  5. Then add major stars and transformations to read the specific expression within the fixed structure, not treat rules as fortune
  6. Only then discuss remedies: knowing the fixed direction a malefic comes from tells you which side to reinforce

Three Common Mistakes

Mistake 1: Assuming malefics fall randomly and reading fixed patterns as unusually bad luck

Mistake 2: Reading Yang-Tuo flanking Lu as "wealth always brings disaster" when it really means guarding wealth takes effort

Mistake 3: Memorizing single stars without placement rules, unable to foresee how malefics link

Back to Your Own Chart

Malefic placement is fixed geometry, not fate's bias. Derive Lu Cun, Yang-Tuo, Fire-Bell and the voids on your chart once, and you will see everyone has these "harsh combos"; the difference is only which palace they land in and which major star receives them.

Reading Order

  1. Cast the natal chart first and locate the palaces and major stars tied to the placement rules of the six malefics before judging anything
  2. Open the triple-direction of those palaces and count the auspicious, malefic and assistant stars separately
  3. Check where the natal Four Transformations land to set the chart's baseline tone
  4. Read the decade-limit transformations to see whether the the placement rules of the six malefics line is activated in this stage
  5. Then read the annual transformations and the year's palace to pin down this year's trigger and timing
  6. Layer natal, decade and year before concluding, then decide how to act rather than labeling good or bad

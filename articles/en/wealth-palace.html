Wealth Palace in Zi Wei Dou Shu: How Money Comes In

The Wealth Palace describes the route by which money is earned, collected, managed, and retained. It does not reduce wealth to one number. Salary, professional fees, projects, business turnover, and income from outside markets can all appear through different palace and star conditions.

Identify the Income Path

Wu Qu in the Wealth Palace often emphasizes measurable value, budgeting, and direct financial management. Tai Yin may favor patient accumulation, planning, or work that rewards precision. Tan Lang can connect money with clients, networks, products, or appetite for expansion. These tendencies still depend on star strength and on whether Career provides a usable role and Travel provides a market.

Separate Earning From Holding

A busy Wealth Palace can generate transactions without producing savings. Hua Lu may increase access to income or resources, while Hua Quan can attach money to responsibility and control. Hua Ke often supports earnings based on expertise, credentials, or a service clients can evaluate. Hua Ji may show delayed collection, thin margins, debt pressure, or a habit that blocks cash flow. Di Kong and Di Jie call for closer attention to what is promised versus what actually arrives.

Read Connected Palaces Through Examples

For example, Wealth with Wu Qu and Hua Lu, supported by a stable Career Palace, may favor salary growth, financial work, or a business with clear pricing. Wealth linked strongly to Travel may earn through remote clients, other cities, online platforms, or a larger market; weak Career support can make that income irregular. Wealth stars placed mainly in Career may describe authority over budgets rather than personal ownership, so the natal Wealth Palace must still show whether compensation can be received and retained.

Chart-Reading Order

Begin with the Wealth Palace, its main stars, and their strength. Next read Career for the role that produces income and Travel for customers, platforms, and external reach. Compare the opposite palace and wider triad, then place the Four Transformations. Add the ten-year and annual cycles last. The final reading should identify an income path, a collection risk, a retention pattern, and one realistic money strategy.

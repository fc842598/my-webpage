How to Handle Workplace Faction Alignment

Workplace factions are a reality of power structures; read the Career and Friends Palaces in the chart. But in the long run, guarding professional competence and boundaries, not easily taking sides and protecting yourself through documentation and process is steadier than attaching to one faction.

Zi Wei Dou Shu2026-10-06

Taking sides essentially means choosing a backer, but backers can fall while competence and reputation do not; holding irreplaceable ability in hand is safer than choosing a side.

What This Question Is Really Asking

Some rush to take sides at the sight of factional struggle, while others withdraw entirely and get marginalized; what is needed is guarding professionalism, not readily declaring allegiance and using process to protect yourself.

The Underlying Logic

Workplace factions exist objectively, ignoring them entirely is unrealistic, but there is no need to rush to choose.

Read the Career (work) and Friends (relationships) Palaces to assess how factions affect you.

Core strategy: professional competence is hard currency, process and documentation are a protective shell.

Do not readily declare allegiance in public, do not spread gossip, do not join personal attacks.

A Step-by-Step Way to Read It

  1. First observe the faction structure and key figures in the organization, do not rush to declare
  2. Assess whether your role and professional competence are irreplaceable
  3. Put important communication and decisions into emails or written records
  4. When courted by a faction, use issue-not-person language to deflect
  5. Do not join gossip or personal attacks, maintain professional neutrality
  6. Continuously build professional competence and external options, holding choice in your own hands

Three Common Mistakes

Mistake 1: Rushing to take sides and betting the future on one person

Mistake 2: Withdrawing entirely, getting marginalized and losing opportunities

Mistake 3: Joining gossip and personal attacks, leaving leverage for others

Using a Real Bill: How to Separate Income Flow from Accumulated Stock

How to divide a real bill into flow and stock, and how to compare the net flow with the ending balance to locate the real issue.

Zi Wei Dou Shu2026-11-17 09:57

To know where money really goes, the most direct way is to read a real bill rather than rely on feeling. A bill contains both a flowing part and a stock part, and reading them together makes decent income feel like nothing remains. Separating flow and stock makes the problem clear.

The difference between flow and stock

Flow is money in and out over a period, such as monthly wages, daily costs, and bill payments. Stock is money truly left at a point in time, such as savings and assets that can be converted. High income only means large flow, not thick stock. Separating the two shows whether the issue is earning little or failing to keep.

Step-by-step bill classification

  1. Choose a time period and list all income and expenses during it.
  2. Put wages and incoming money into income flow, and daily costs into expense flow.
  3. Count separately the savings and convertible assets left at period end as stock.
  4. Compare the net flow with the change in stock to see whether money was spent or kept.
  5. Locate whether the issue is income or accumulation, then adjust.

Chart and bill confirm each other

Wealth and Career show flow, Property shows stock, and the bill provides verifiable real numbers. Placing the chart hints and the bill side by side keeps the conclusion from being empty talk. Sort the accounts first, then discuss how to change.

Chart reading order

  1. Fix the chart and locate Wealth, Career, and Property.
  2. Use a real bill to list income and expenses by time period.
  3. Separate flow and stock and count them individually.
  4. Match the chart palaces to the corresponding links.
  5. Keep the conclusion conditional and let the numbers speak.

Partner Financial Fortune in Zi Wei Dou Shu: Spouse and Wealth Palace Combinations

In marriage, money is one of the most common sources of conflict. From the chart perspective, the combination of Spouse Palace (partner traits) and Wealth Palace (your own fortune) reflects the financial pattern between you and your partner—complementary or conflicting, joint accumulation or separate management. Understanding this pattern helps build healthy financial division in marriage.

Zi Wei Dou Shu2026-09-24

Reading partners financial traits from Spouse Palace: Spouse Palace Wu Qu, Tian Fu—partner is pragmatic, good at managing money, suitable for handling finances. Spouse Palace Tan Lang, Po Jun—partner spends generously, likes risky investments, needs moderation. Spouse Palace Tai Yin—partner is detail-oriented, saves money, but may be too conservative. Spouse Palace Tian Tong—partner has no concept of money, easily spends all income. Spouse Palace Zi Wei—partner has control desire, wants to主导 family finances. Spouse Palace Prosperity—partner has good fortune, brings resources. Spouse Palace Jealousy—partner has lessons in finances, may have unstable income or overspends. Financial patterns between spouses: first, "complementary type"—one earns well (strong Wealth Palace), one saves well (strong Property Palace), the ideal family financial structure. Second, "same type"—both can earn and spend, need to establish joint savings mechanism. Third, "conflict type"—one wants to invest one wants to save, easily argues over money, need to clarify individual "allowance" and "joint account." Fourth, "independent type"—each manages own money, suitable for couples with independent incomes and large differences in spending values. Methods to avoid money hurting the relationship: first, "talk about money before marriage"—discuss income, debt, spending values before marriage, do not discover differences after. Second, "establish joint account"—household expenses from joint account, personal spending from personal accounts, reduces friction. Third, "joint decisions for large expenses"—expenses above a certain amount need both parties agreement, do not decide unilaterally. Fourth, "do not mix money with love"—willingness to spend on you does not equal love, unwillingness does not equal not loving, everyones money values relate to upbringing. What This Question Is Really Asking

Practical case: Someone with Prosperity in Wealth but Jealousy in Tian Zhai has good income but cannot save—earns much but spends much, or loses money through property or family expenses. The key is "income looks at Wealth, preservation looks at Tian Zhai"—both palaces must be read.

The Underlying Logic

Only looking at Wealth Palace without Tian Zhai, earning much but not preserving.

Equating Prosperity with "will definitely get rich," ignoring that Prosperity can also mean "more money more problems."

Not watching Decade/Annual wealth changes, making wrong investments at wrong times.

Treating chart financial tendencies as investment advice, without own research and risk assessment.

A Step-by-Step Way to Read It

  1. Chart the Wealth, Tian Zhai, and Fortune palaces major stars and transformations.
  2. See the Decade Wealth Palace state to judge the ten-year income trend.
  3. Distinguish between "regular income" (salary) and "windfall" (investment, side business) sources.
  4. Check Tian Zhai wealth-preservation ability, set savings and asset allocation plans.
  5. Take initiative in good wealth years, hold steady in bad wealth years.
  6. Regularly review financial status and adjust strategy based on actual conditions.

Three Common Mistakes

Mistake 1: The chart only indicates financial tendencies; it does not constitute investment advice.

Mistake 2: Good wealth years can still lose money from wrong decisions; bad wealth years can still preserve capital through caution.

Mistake 3: Financial content is based on licensed institutions and official product descriptions; investments carry risk.

Back to Your Own Chart

The core of wealth management is not "whether you have money in your destiny" but "whether you can preserve and grow what you earn." The chart helps you understand your financial personality and risk points; real wealth accumulation comes from consistent saving habits, reasonable asset allocation, and continuously improving earning ability.

The Spouse Palace Is Also the Partnership Palace: Dividing Work When Couples Co-found

Beyond marriage, the Spouse Palace is also the partnership palace; for co-founding, read its main star and transformations to set the division of labor. Lu shows whether profit sharing runs smoothly, Quan who calls the shots, and Ji where conflicts hide. The chart is only tendency—major partnerships must rest on written contracts, equity agreements and professional legal advice.

Zi Wei Dou Shu2026-09-27

Couples who open a shop or project together most easily tangle feeling with business: arguing roles by day and money by night. The chart writes this in the Spouse Palace, but the real backstop is the contract no one wants to discuss first.

What This Question Is Really Asking

Translate transformations into roles: Lu in Spouse means profit sharing is workable but must be written; Quan together means one person deciding is efficient, with clear authority for the other; wherever Ji sits, set rules and boundaries ahead. However close, put equity, roles and exit in writing, relying on professional lawyers, proper channels and written contracts.

The Underlying Logic

The Spouse Palace covers both marriage and partnership.

The main star sets the basic mode of relating and dividing work.

Lu shows profit sharing, Quan decision power, and Ji conflict points.

Major partnerships rest on written contracts, equity agreements and professional lawyers.

A Step-by-Step Way to Read It

  1. Read the Spouse main star for the tone of relating and roles
  2. Read Lu for profit sharing
  3. Read where Quan lands to decide who calls shots
  4. Read where Ji lands to anticipate conflict points
  5. Put equity, roles and exit into a written agreement
  6. Have a professional lawyer review before signing

Three Common Mistakes

Mistake 1: Starting a couple business on feeling without a contract

Mistake 2: Taking Quan as whoever is louder deciding

Mistake 3: Ignoring Ji and fixing conflict only after it explodes

Back to Your Own Chart

When co-founding with your partner, separate romance from the books. The chart helps you foresee roles and conflict; what makes both love and business last is setting rules first and putting them on paper.

  1. Cast the natal chart first and locate the palaces and major stars tied to Spouse Palace and joint business before judging anything
  2. Open the triple-direction of those palaces and count the auspicious, malefic and assistant stars separately
  3. Check where the natal Four Transformations land to set the chart's baseline tone
  4. Read the decade-limit transformations to see whether the Spouse Palace and joint business line is activated in this stage
  5. Then read the annual transformations and the year's palace to pin down this year's trigger and timing
  6. Layer natal, decade and year before concluding, then decide how to act rather than labeling good or bad

Workplace Competition Through Qing Yang and Tuo Lo in Zi Wei Dou Shu

Workplace competition is a scenario everyone faces, but people with different charts compete very differently. Qing Yang governs "open blades"—direct, tough, face-to-face contest; Tuo Lo governs "entanglement"—circuitous, persistent, behind-the-scenes struggle. Understanding your own competitive style lets you leverage strengths and avoid weaknesses at work, rather than using the wrong approach and draining yourself.

Zi Wei Dou Shu2026-09-24

Qing Yang in Career Palace competitive style: direct confrontation type. When facing competition, tends to express position directly, speak with strength, not avoid conflict. Advantages: decisive, bold, stands out in situations requiring decisions; disadvantages: easily offends people, makes enemies, poor at circuitous approaches. Qing Yang type suits work requiring "charge"—sales, entrepreneurship, athletics, crisis management, but not positions requiring long-term relationship building. Tuo Lo in Career Palace competitive style: circuitous persistent type. When facing competition, does not rush to respond directly, but observes secretly, lays out plans slowly, wears down opponents with time. Advantages: patient, persistent, does not easily reveal hand; disadvantages: easily procrastinates, misses timing, gives a scheming impression. Tuo Lo type suits work requiring "endurance"—research, legal affairs, strategy, long-term projects, but not positions requiring quick decisions. If both Qing Yang and Tuo Lo are in Career Palace or in trine, it is the "both open and hidden struggle" type—can confront directly and plot behind the scenes, but also easily exhausts oneself. These people need to learn to "choose battles"—not all competition is worth investing in, distinguish what needs direct response from what can be handled coldly. General advice for workplace competition: regardless of type, channel competitive energy toward "improving yourself" rather than "suppressing others." Qing Yang type should practice "focus on issues not people"—when conflicting, focus on the problem not attacking personality. Tuo Lo type should practice "timely communication"—do not hold dissatisfaction until it finally erupts.

What This Question Is Really Asking

Practical case: Someone with Zi Wei in Life Palace and Authority in Career Palace is often a strong manager, but if Fortune Palace has Jealousy, they easily burn out from stress. The key is not "whether to be strong" but "in what scenario to be strong"—decisive in work decisions, flexible in relationships.

The Underlying Logic

Treating chart career signals as fixed labels, ignoring Decade/Annual changes.

Only looking at major stars without transformations, missing the key impact of Authority/Jealousy on career.

Interpreting "suitable career" as "can only do this," limiting own possibilities.

Ignoring the positive side of malefics—Qing Yang/Tuo Lo can be advantages in certain professions (surgery, legal).

A Step-by-Step Way to Read It

  1. Chart the Life, Career, and Wealth palaces major stars and transformations.
  2. See which palace the current Decade moves through to determine the ten-year career focus.
  3. Find "advantage palaces" (many auspicious stars, Prosperity/Authority) and push in that direction.
  4. Identify "challenge palaces" (Jealousy, many malefics) and prepare coping strategies in advance.
  5. Combine personal interests and real conditions to choose 2-3 best-matching career directions.
  6. Adjust in practice—the chart gives tendencies, action gives answers.

Three Common Mistakes

Mistake 1: The chart only indicates tendencies; career development is affected by education, opportunity, personal effort and many factors.

Mistake 2: Decade/Annual changes may make an "unsuitable" career become suitable in a certain period.

Mistake 3: Do not blindly resign because the chart says "suitable for entrepreneurship"; need to combine actual resources and ability.

Back to Your Own Chart

There is no absolute "good fortune" or "bad fortune" in the workplace, only whether your strengths are used in the right place. The chart helps you see your talents and lessons; the rest is sustained effort in the right direction. Understand first, then act—more effective than blind job-hopping or grinding through.

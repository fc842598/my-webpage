Ji Liang: Tian Ji, Tian Liang, and Thoughtful Planning

Ji Liang combines Tian Ji and Tian Liang themes of planning and principles. Read the actual configuration and compare ideas with practical follow-through.

Zi Wei Dou Shu2026-09-24Updated 2026-11-06 15:07 (UTC+8)

Ji Liang refers to a relationship between Tian Ji and Tian Liang in the chart. Tian Ji should not be mistranslated as 'Ji Ji.' A useful discussion connects planning and adaptability with judgment and principles, without assuming a particular profession or level of ability.

Check the Actual Star Relationship

State whether the stars share a palace or connect through related palaces. Read their conditions, companion stars, and transformations. Do not apply a pattern name solely because both stars appear somewhere in a twelve-palace chart.

Connect the Theme to a Real Task

For a planning role, ask how the person turns a complicated problem into a usable proposal, explains trade-offs, and responds when circumstances change. In a teaching role, distinguish subject knowledge from the ability to listen and communicate. Neither skill is proved by the chart.

Balance Analysis With Follow-Through

A possible question is whether repeated revision improves a plan or postpones a decision. Use actual examples and agreed requirements to assess that. Keep ten-year and annual interpretations separate from the natal combination, and do not substitute an unrelated Sun-and-Moon example for this pattern.

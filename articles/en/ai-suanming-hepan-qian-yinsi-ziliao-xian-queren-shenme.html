Questions like "合盘前担心隐私，双方哪些资料必须确认，哪些可以后补" go wrong in two opposite ways: people either hand over every detail too early, or hold back so much that the chart itself becomes unstable. The steadier move is to protect the layers that are truly required first.

Privacy handling is not about telling the whole story on round one. It is about separating chart-required data, question-boundary data, and scene-detail data so you know what each layer is doing.

Separate what is truly necessary first

Start by checking whether the information touches the hard lines of 双方出生信息 and 当前关系边界. If not, it should not be treated as automatically required.

Once the layers are separated, you can tell whether you are improving the reading or simply exposing more than the question needs.

Handle the information in layers

In an early relationship reading, both birth details and the current relationship stage usually matter more than long private story dumps.

If you hide the relationship state entirely, the answer often becomes generic comfort. If you overshare everything on round one, exposure grows faster than insight.

Do not treat extra background as required

Caution does not mean saying nothing. If you omit chart-critical input, the later judgment drifts.

But more detail is not the same thing as more accuracy. In early rounds, extra personal context often enlarges exposure more than insight.

A steadier data-handling order

  1. Fill in the chart-critical details first.
  2. Add only the data that directly shapes the question boundary.
  3. Leave scene detail for later unless the first judgment truly needs it.

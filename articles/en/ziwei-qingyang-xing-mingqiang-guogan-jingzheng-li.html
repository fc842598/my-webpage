Qing Yang in Zi Wei Dou Shu: Open-Blade Ferocity and How to Turn Drive Into Competitiveness

Qing Yang is yang metal, qi of punishment, the fiercest and most direct of the four malefics, governing clash, decisiveness, blade energy and competition. Positively it is resolve, daring and hard skill; negatively impatience, head-on collision, injury and disputes. Strong and auspicious it converts punishment to courage, suiting police, surgery, engineering and competitive roles—the key is giving this blade the right place to work.

Zi Wei Dou Shu2026-09-07

Qing Yang is an open spear: fast and out in the open, never secretly clinging. It gives unstoppable sharpness—a weapon for hard problems and competition, but wounding in relationships and emotion.

What This Question Is Really Asking

People fear blood and lawsuits at the sight of it, yet a strong Qing Yang often gives exceptional resolve for hard problems; guard against being unforgiving and head-on, turning competitiveness into confrontation.

The Underlying Logic

Qing Yang is yang metal, qi of punishment, always one palace before Lu Cun, governing fierce, direct, fast disruption and resolve—an open spear.

Positive: brave, decisive, strong executor who rises in high-pressure competition and hard technique, favoring police, surgery, machinery, engineering, sports and enforcement.

Negative: impatient, combative, prone to injury/surgery and disputes, especially when fallen or with Ji.

Conversion: strong with Lu/Quan/Ke or assistants turns punishment to courage; discipline, expertise and rules sheath the edge, avoiding head-on clash and emotional decisions.

A Step-by-Step Way to Read It

  1. Locate Qing Yang to see where the fierce energy applies
  2. Strong and auspicious: take competitive, hard-technique, decisive routes
  3. Fallen with malefics: practice delayed response, avoid rush and head-on clash
  4. Bind the edge with expertise and rules; pause in conflict
  5. Use protection in blade/fire/metal sports and health safety
  6. In years it activates, attack hard problems rather than start conflicts

Three Common Mistakes

Mistake 1: Predicting injury/lawsuits at the sight of it

Mistake 2: Mistaking recklessness for bravery, always clashing

Mistake 3: Letting real competitiveness stay unshielded, making enemies everywhere

Register-first versus pay-first sounds like a checkout question, but it is really a carry-over question.

If the chart, record, and paid access cannot travel cleanly into the next session, the smoother flow still becomes expensive later.

Separate the layers first

The main test is continuity: can tomorrow's session still recognize today's chart and paid layer?

Payment is not the risk by itself. Unclear carry-over is.

Questions like "For AI Fortune Telling, Is It Safer to Register Before Paying or Pay Before Filling More Data" are really about what each layer solves for you, not about price alone.

Look at a real usage case

Trying the chart and the first follow-up before paying often gives you a cleaner screening round.

A pay-first flow that never explains how records and access continue can create repeated work later.

The steadier order is still to use the basic layer for one real verification round, then judge whether continuity and quota actually matter for you.

Cheap is not the same as worth it

Register-first is not automatically safer, and pay-first is not automatically worse.

Neither path improves answer quality by itself if the rules stay unclear.

So do not isolate the public price or the daily quota from the service shape behind them. The real value test is whether you will use that continuity.

A steadier spending order

  1. Check how records, access, and orders carry over.
  2. Choose the order only after that.
  3. If the rule is unclear, pause before payment.
